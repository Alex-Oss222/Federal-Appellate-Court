# Commitments: In re Secured Equipment Trust of Eastern Air Lines, Inc. (Nos. 94-5015, 94-5017)

**Stage:** neutral modeling. **Legal cutoff:** October 17, 1994. **Inputs:** neutral.md, ENGINE.md, and the public sources cited below.

Common frame (both judges). Appellate jurisdiction rests on 28 U.S.C. § 158(d) (final decisions of the district court on bankruptcy appeals); dismissal of the petition for ineligibility is a final disposition, and timeliness is unverified in the packet. Legal classification is reviewed de novo and findings for clear error (*In re Ionosphere Clubs, Inc.*, 922 F.2d 984, 988-89 (2d Cir. 1990)). Petitioners bear the eligibility burden. The record on the Trust's terms and operations is largely documentary and stipulated, so the question is chiefly legal. The Code does not define "business trust" (§ 101(9)(A)(v), within "corporation"; § 101(41), "person"; § 303(a)), and no controlling Second Circuit or Supreme Court definition exists on this date.

## Amalya L. Kearse (Active Circuit Judge)

**Disposition:** One component, Trust eligibility. Affirm the dismissal: petitioners did not show the Trust is a "business trust," so it is not a person against whom an involuntary case may be commenced under § 303(a). No ruling on other § 303 conditions, creditor qualification, abstention, priorities, or plan treatment. Confidence moderate on result, lower on rationale (see gaps).

**Ground:** Her verified opinions start from the Code's words and structure, use legislative history to confirm, and decline to add or subtract limits on policy grounds. In *Century Brass* she read § 1107(a)'s "Subject to any limitations on a trustee" together with a Senate report, rejected congressional acquiescence absent "settled judicial constructions," and wrote "we are not entitled to reweigh those interests." In *400 North Midler* she resolved the question by the plain wording and interaction of the rules. Applied here, an undefined Code term takes its ordinary, contemporary, common meaning (*Perrin*; *Germain*, "says in a statute what it means"). The common-law and Supreme Court description of a business trust is a vehicle whose object "is not to hold and conserve particular property" but "to provide a medium for the conduct of a business and sharing its gains" (*Morrissey*, a tax case, used by analogy only). Section 303(a) confirms the commercial focus by excluding from involuntary cases a corporation "that is not a moneyed, business, or commercial corporation." Trustee title and central management appear in the record; the contested element is business purpose with gains shared by beneficiaries. The indenture's purpose is to secure payment of the certificates; the parties agree the economics are secured financing; holders receive a fixed return as lenders; surplus goes to Eastern; the property is reconveyed on payment. The default powers to possess, lease, and sell are incidents of enforcing and conserving collateral, not an enterprise whose gains holders share. Classification turns on purpose and structure fixed by the documents, so later volume of management does not convert the Trust. The standard is de novo; she would not disturb the documentary findings.

**Remedy:** Affirm; no remand. Judgment limited to eligibility, expressly leaving open other § 303 predicates and any trust whose beneficiaries share residual gains.

**Sources:** *In re Century Brass Products, Inc.*, 22 F.3d 37 (2d Cir. 1994) (Kearse, J.); *400 North Midler Avenue Corp. v. Depo*, 793 F.2d 48 (2d Cir. 1986) (Kearse, J.); *In re Chateaugay Corp.*, 988 F.2d 322 (2d Cir. 1993) (Kearse, J.) (decides only what is necessary: "We do not reach the merits"); *Germain v. Connecticut National Bank*, 930 F.2d 1038 (2d Cir. 1991) (Kearse, J.) (appellate jurisdiction read narrowly). Authorities: 11 U.S.C. §§ 101(9), 101(41), 303(a); 28 U.S.C. § 158(d); *Connecticut National Bank v. Germain*, 503 U.S. 249 (1992); *Perrin v. United States*, 444 U.S. 37 (1979); *Morrissey v. Commissioner*, 296 U.S. 344 (1935); *In re Secured Equipment Trust of Eastern Air Lines, Inc.*, 153 B.R. 409 (Bankr. S.D.N.Y. Apr. 16, 1993) (Lifland, C.J.); *In re St. Augustine Trust*, 109 B.R. 494 (Bankr. M.D. Fla. 1990) (persuasive only). No pinpoints are given where the text lacked page markers.

**Join limits:** She would not join a rule that a residual-profit interest is categorically required, that security or collateral trustees can never qualify, or that post-default management is legally irrelevant, because her method disfavors rules broader than the Code's text and the case need. She would not join reasoning resting on the convenience of other forums or consolidated disputes (neutral.md says that does not redefine the debtor). She would join a narrower affirmance on this indenture's purpose and structure. Plausible alternative, which matters: if the panel treats capital-raising through certificates plus fleet leasing and sale as business within the Code's term regardless of residual gains, she would reverse the eligibility ruling and remand for the remaining § 303 conditions and abstention, not order relief.

## Frank X. Altimari (Active Circuit Judge)

**Disposition:** One component, Trust eligibility. Affirm the dismissal on the ground that, on the whole arrangement, the Trust is not a business trust; reach nothing else under § 303. Confidence moderate on result, lower on rationale.

**Ground:** His verified opinions read statutory text with legislative history and practical effect, and will not follow a literal reading that "leads either to unintended results or great uncertainty" (*Brandt-Airflex*, limiting § 505(a) to avoid a "second tax court system"). In *Vessel American Maine* he construed "wages of the crew" through the text and precedent-developed tests, then declined the broader fringe-benefit question; in *48th Street Steakhouse* he read a broadly worded Code provision broadly where text and reports supported it. The Code's term is open and the Act continuity described in the packet points to businesses conducted through trustees with certificated interests. Applying a functional whole-arrangement inquiry (trust documents, purpose, beneficiaries' interests, operations; compare *St. Augustine*, persuasive only), the Trust was formed to secure a debt, pays holders a fixed contractual return, returns surplus to Eastern, and manages aircraft after default to maximize recovery on that debt. A holding that those enforcement powers make a trust a business trust would expose any collateral or indenture trustee holding possess, lease, and sell powers to involuntary petitions by junior holders, an unintended breadth with no textual or predecessor support. Review of the classification is de novo; the stipulated secured-financing character is accepted. He would stress that the holding does not govern liquidating trusts winding up a profit-seeking predecessor, which the bankruptcy court distinguished.

**Remedy:** Affirm on a narrow ground; no remand; leave other § 303 predicates, broader nonprofit and liquidating-trust questions, and plan consequences undecided.

**Sources:** *In re 48th Street Steakhouse, Inc.*, 835 F.2d 427 (2d Cir. 1987) (Altimari, J.); *In re Brandt-Airflex Corp.*, 843 F.2d 90 (2d Cir. 1988) (Altimari, J.); *Citibank, N.A. v. Vessel American Maine*, 865 F.2d 24 (2d Cir. 1988) (Altimari, J.); *Werbungs Und Commerz Union Austalt v. Collectors' Guild, Ltd.*, 930 F.2d 1021 (2d Cir. 1991) (Altimari, J.) (ambiguity decided on the whole instrument, background only). Authorities: as for Kearse, including *Ionosphere*, 11 U.S.C. §§ 101(9), 101(41), 303(a), 28 U.S.C. § 158(d), and the bankruptcy court opinion at 153 B.R. 409.

**Join limits:** He would not join a bright-line profit-motive requirement for all trusts, or a holding fixing the status of all liquidating trusts, since his record favors narrow holdings. He would not treat bankruptcy-court multi-factor tests as binding. He would join a narrower text-and-structure rationale if it stops at this indenture. Plausible alternative, which matters: his 48th Street approach (broad definitions read broadly) supports reversal if he finds that the certificate financing and sustained fleet leasing are commercial activity within the term, with remand for remaining § 303 issues and no order for relief.
