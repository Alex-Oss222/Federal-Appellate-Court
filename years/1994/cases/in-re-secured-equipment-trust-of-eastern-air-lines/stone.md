## Stone's Position

### Component 1

**Component:** Business-trust eligibility for an involuntary bankruptcy case

**Jurisdiction and reach:** The preserved question is the Trust’s statutory eligibility, decided below. Other § 303 conditions, creditor qualification, priorities, and eventual relief were not resolved through that ruling. The statutory route and unsettled jurisdictional predicates in the neutral baseline remain controlling; this proposal does not assume them away.

**Proposed disposition:** Propose reversal of the business-trust eligibility dismissal and remand for the remaining involuntary-case requirements.

**Governing law:** Independent review of legal classification; clear error for bankruptcy findings. In re Ionosphere Clubs, Inc., 922 F.2d 984, 988-89 (2d Cir. 1990). Petitioners must establish an eligible person under § 303(a). Sections 101(41) and 101(9)(A)(v) include a business trust through the definition of corporation. The undefined term requires examination of the trust’s formation, purposes, investment structure, and authorized operations. The presence of some businesslike conduct does not alone decide classification.

No directly controlling Supreme Court holding defining business trust for this Code eligibility question is identified. Analogies from other statutory settings do not replace the Bankruptcy Code. 11 U.S.C. §§ 101(9)(A)(v), 101(41), 303(a); predecessor Bankruptcy Act definition and relevant 1978 legislative history; In re St. Augustine Trust, 109 B.R. 494, 496 (Bankr. M.D. Fla. 1990), persuasive only.

**Legal relationship:** Resolving an open question; developing an independent statutory rationale.

**Proposed holding:** This certificate-financed, trustee-operated equipment enterprise is a business trust where its original arrangement includes substantial continuing commercial leasing and management powers. Neither fixed returns nor secured-financing economics alone exclude it.

**Legal reasoning:** Stone should propose reversal of the categorical eligibility dismissal. The statutory term should encompass this certificate-financed, trustee-operated commercial arrangement, including the substantial leasing and asset-management functions authorized from its inception. That conclusion does not require denying the parties' agreement that the sale-and-leaseback was economically a secured financing. The question is whether a financing enterprise conducted in this institutional form can be a business trust, not whether its participants must pretend that the underlying obligation was something else.

The legal error is to treat preservation of a contractually fixed return as necessarily outside business. Many businesses earn specified rather than residual returns. Nothing in the enacted inclusion of business trusts makes unlimited profit participation the decisive boundary. The predecessor statute's focus on trustee-conducted business and certificated interests reinforces that conclusion. The Trust was not a family settlement that happened to sell an asset; investors supplied capital through certificates for an organized transaction, and the trustee undertook continuing commercial responsibilities over a fleet.

The default provisions matter because they show what institution the parties created, not because subsequent distress permits the trustee to manufacture eligibility. The same indenture authorized commercial leasing, maintenance, marketing, and sales if Eastern ceased performance. The actual fleet operations make concrete an original institutional function. Stone should rely on that combined formation-and-operation account rather than pronounce that the size of the collateral pool or the number of retained professionals independently makes the Trust a business.

Appellees' concern about ordinary security trustees requires a real boundary. A trustee whose role is simply to hold a lien and distribute receipts does not automatically become a business trust because it employs counsel or sells collateral after default. Here the organized certificate investment, the integrated equipment transaction, and the trustee's substantial continuing powers together satisfy the statutory category. This is a proposed construction of the open federal term, not a universal checklist in which every characteristic must appear in all future cases.

Reversal would establish eligibility only. It would not authorize junior certificateholders to erase negotiated priorities or obtain relief merely because the equipment lost value. The bankruptcy court must decide the remaining requirements for an involuntary case, any properly presented grounds for dismissal or abstention, and the treatment of claims under their governing sources. The availability of those protections is not a substitute for reading the eligibility provision; it confirms that debtor classification need not pre-decide every later dispute.

**Independent or alternative considerations:** The predecessor Act supplies a continuity argument, not a rule that every certificated trust qualifies. A collective commercial institution differs from an ordinary lien-holding trustee even when both protect secured creditors.

**Preferred scope:** This certificate-financed, trustee-operated equipment enterprise is a business trust where its original arrangement includes substantial continuing commercial leasing and management powers. Neither fixed returns nor secured-financing economics alone exclude it. The lawful outer boundary is the issue and remedial limit stated here, not a broader rule about unpresented facts.

Case-level proposed scope: an organized investment trust conducting the integrated equipment-financing and management business shown here qualifies under § 101(9)(A)(v), notwithstanding a fixed investor return and a secured-financing purpose.

**Fallback position:** Proposed: vacate and require consideration of the complete formation-and-operation record without treating absence of residual profit as dispositive. A fallback may not equate every protective collateral sale with business-trust status.

Case-level nonjoin limits, also proposed: eligibility from financial size alone; eligibility whenever a trustee performs any commercial transaction; state-law labels controlling the federal definition; a categorical exclusion of all fixed-return investment trusts; or an order granting Chapter 11 relief without the additional statutory showings.

**Remedy and remand:** Reverse the threshold ineligibility ruling; determine the remaining § 303 requirements and any properly raised dismissal or abstention grounds. Do not alter negotiated priorities or direct entry of an order for relief.

**Reserved questions:** No universal profit-purpose prerequisite, no universal checklist for all trusts, and no conclusion on creditor standing, priorities, plan treatment, or ultimate relief.
