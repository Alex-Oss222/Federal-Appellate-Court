## Dependence on the historical judgment

- Any rehearing or certiorari petition would have been sought by the appellants (the Second and Third Priority certificate holders and Magten), because the historical judgment affirmed dismissal of their involuntary petition. The question presented would have been whether the Trust is a "business trust" under 11 U.S.C. 101(9)(A)(v). The petition would depend on (1) an affirmance of the dismissal, (2) the majority's treatment of the Trust as a financing-security vehicle with incidental operations, and (3) the express refusal to make profit generation a universal requirement.
- If a differently decided judgment reversed the dismissal, the losing parties would be the Trust and collateral trustee side, and a historical appellant petition would have no object. A reversal would also lead to remand proceedings on the remaining order-for-relief conditions, which the historical path never reached.
- A differently reasoned affirmance (for example, one resting on a profit-purpose requirement) would still fit an appellant petition, but any recorded question presented would need to be checked against the new reasoning.
