# Reconciled positions: In re Secured Equipment Trust of Eastern Air Lines, Inc. (Nos. 94-5015, 94-5017)

**Stage:** historical reconciliation. **Inputs:** neutral.md, commitments.md, history.md, ENGINE.md.

## Historical comparison

The historical panel (38 F.3d 86, October 17, 1994) affirmed dismissal of the involuntary petition: the Trust was an arrangement preserving collateral for Eastern's secured financing, not an eligible business trust. Altimari wrote the majority opinion, joined by Schwartz. Kearse dissented, stressing the certificated investment structure, the authorized commercial activities, the actual equipment operations, and continuity with the predecessor Bankruptcy Act. The majority treated the substantial post-default operations as incidental to the protective purpose and expressly declined to make profit generation a universal prerequisite. The dismissal stood; the remaining conditions for an order for relief and priorities were not decided. Mandate status is unknown.

Premises that survive here: the same parties, record, stipulated secured-financing economics, argument date (June 30, 1994), legal cutoff, open circuit question, and eligibility-only reach. The one changed premise is the third seat, which changes who else votes. It does not change the record, the governing law, or the legal question each retained judge answers, and it is not treated as a reason for either to move. History does not state the remedy in Kearse's dissent, and it does not show whether she addressed gains-sharing or the § 303(a) "moneyed, business, or commercial corporation" clause.

## Amalya L. Kearse

**Reconciled disposition:** Reverse the eligibility ruling. The Trust is a business trust within § 101(9)(A)(v) and so a person against whom an involuntary case may be commenced under § 303(a).

**Ground:** Certificated capital-raising through a trust vehicle, trustee ownership and control of the equipment, authorized leasing and disposition powers, the actual fleet operations (sixty-seven jets and 165 engines taken, forty-seven jets still managed), and continuity of the Code's term with the Act's definition of a business conducted through trustees with certificated interests. A fixed return is an investment return, and absence of residual profit does not defeat classification. This is the commitment's own stated alternative ("regardless of residual gains") and tracks the four themes of the historical dissent. It is consistent with her text-first method, with legislative history confirming. How she would treat *Morrissey*'s "sharing its gains" language is not shown in history and is left open.

**Remedy:** Reverse and remand for the remaining § 303 conditions and any abstention or dismissal grounds. No order for relief, and no ruling on creditor qualification, priorities, or plan treatment. This remedy is carried from the commitment's alternative path; history does not state it.

**Join limits:** She would not join an affirmance, narrow or broad, that rests on treating the post-default operations as merely incidental to a protective purpose. History shows she declined that rationale, and this displaces the commitment's statement that she would join a narrower affirmance on the indenture's purpose and structure. She would not join a rule that a residual-profit interest is categorically required (consistent with history, where the majority also declined to adopt one). Carried from the commitment and not shown in history: she would not join a rule that every collateral trustee is a business trust, or that post-default management alone is dispositive; she would not join reasoning resting on other-forum convenience or consolidation; and she would not join a reversal that orders relief or decides other § 303 predicates.

**Basis for departure from the commitment:** The commitment's affirmance was an inference from interpretive method; she has no pre-cutoff writing on "business trust," confidence was moderate on result, and the commitment itself called the reversal path real. History is her actual vote on this record and law, with stated grounds that match that alternative path. No changed premise points toward affirmance, and the substitution of one panelist is not a reason to hold the commitment against that evidence. The historical evidence is stronger than the commitment's basis. The departure is to the commitment's own alternative and is not a departure from history.

## Frank X. Altimari

**Reconciled disposition:** Affirm the dismissal. On the whole arrangement the Trust is not a business trust. Eligibility only.

**Ground:** A functional whole-arrangement inquiry. The Trust was formed to secure a debt, pays holders a fixed contractual return, returns surplus to Eastern, and is reconveyed and dissolved on payment. Post-default possession, leasing, and sale are incidental to protecting and recovering on the collateral, so the volume of management does not convert it. A contrary rule would expose ordinary collateral and indenture trustees to involuntary petitions by junior holders. Profit generation is not made a universal prerequisite (history confirms the majority declined to do so). His caveat that the holding does not govern liquidating trusts winding up a profit-seeking predecessor comes from the commitment; history is silent on it.

**Remedy:** Affirm; the dismissal stands; no remand. Other § 303 predicates, priorities, plan consequences, and the broader nonprofit and liquidating-trust questions stay undecided.

**Join limits:** He would not join a bright-line profit-motive requirement, a holding fixing the status of all liquidating trusts, or any treatment of bankruptcy-court multi-factor tests as binding. He would join a narrow text-and-structure rationale that stops at this indenture. He would not join a reversal rationale of the kind the dissent states.

**Basis for any departure from the commitment or from history:** None. The commitment and history agree on result, ground, remedy, and limits. The commitment's reversal alternative is not retained: history shows he reached and wrote the contrary result on the same record, and no changed premise points back toward it. Authorship is not assigned here; history shows he authored the majority, and assignment is for assembly under circuit practice.

## Agreement between the two modeled judges (by component)

- **Disposition:** Disagree. Kearse reverses the eligibility ruling; Altimari affirms the dismissal. Between them there is no two-vote judgment either way. Any majority on the judgment depends entirely on the third seat, which this document does not address.
- **Sufficient ground:** Disagree on whether the Trust is a business trust and on the weight of the post-default operations (evidence of commercial character for Kearse; incidental to securing the debt for Altimari). They share the framework: independent review of classification, petitioners' burden, an undefined term read from the Code and the whole arrangement, and no categorical residual-profit requirement.
- **Remedy:** Disagree (affirm without remand versus reverse and remand). They agree that neither orders relief or decides other § 303 predicates, creditor qualification, priorities, or plan treatment.
- **Join limits:** Shared: neither would join a categorical residual-profit rule, and both disfavor holdings broader than the case needs. The other-forum-convenience limit is stated for Kearse only; Altimari's commitment is silent, so shared agreement on it is not established. Each would decline the other's rationale for the judgment. The only propositions on which the two could supply two votes are the shared framework points and the rejection of a categorical profit requirement; those cannot make a majority on the judgment.
