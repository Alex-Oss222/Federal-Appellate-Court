## Departure from history

The simulated panel reached the same judgment as the historical panel, affirming the convictions and sentence in full and unanimously, with no resentencing, new trial, remand, or separate writing, but the lineup differs (Judge Stone sat in place of Judge Miner) and Judge Feinberg, not Judge Pierce, wrote the opinion. The reasoning differs in part: the simulated opinion measures the alternative harmlessness ground under Rule 52(a) and Kotteakos with the burden on the government and rejects the civil Rule 61 measure the historical opinion used. It also rests the ex post facto ruling on the April 1985 sale and the jury's post-October 12, 1984 overt-act finding, and expressly not relying on the October 1984 sale.

## Historical comparison extract

Historically, on December 2, 1994, Judges Feinberg, Pierce and Miner affirmed the convictions and sentence in a unanimous published opinion by Judge Pierce, with no separate opinions. United States v. Duncan, No. 93-1402, 42 F.3d 97, 101-07 (2d Cir. 1994). The simulated panel (Feinberg, Pierce, Stone) also affirmed unanimously on the same date, with Judge Stone replacing Judge Miner and Judge Feinberg writing. The judgment is unchanged; the differences are in reasoning. Both approved the agent's testimony, rejected the ex post facto objection, sustained Count One on the identified bank-fraud object, and upheld the leadership finding. The historical alternative harmlessness discussion used Civil Rule 61 and civil prejudice authorities. The simulated opinion applies Rule 52(a) and Kotteakos, puts the burden on the government, and rejects the civil rule as the measure. Its ex post facto holding rests on the April 1985 sale and the jury's post-enactment overt-act finding alone.
