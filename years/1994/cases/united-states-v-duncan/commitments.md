## Wilfred Feinberg (Senior Circuit Judge)

**Disposition:** Issue 1 (Mulligan testimony): affirm. Admits the explanation of records, flows and false filings; the discrete money-laundering and IRS-obstruction labels, if given as characterizations of the law, exceed Rule 704(a) under Scop and Bilzerian, and an investigator's role is no exemption. He would assume that error and decide harmlessness. Issue 2 (§ 1344 timing): affirm. Issue 3 (Count One): sustain on the bank-fraud object without reaching IRS timeliness. Issue 4 (leadership): affirm. Overall: affirm the judgment.

**Ground:** Issue 1: admission is reviewed for abuse of discretion and the law-versus-fact boundary independently. No Feinberg writing on Rules 702 or 704 was found, so the error question rests on circuit law alone. His three verified harmlessness opinions each say "even if" error, then decide on whole-record circumstances: isolation, the objection, curative instruction, strength of the rest. Applied here under Criminal Rule 52(a) and Kotteakos: fragmentary labels, undisputed transactions, cooperating partners and documents on knowledge. Sufficiency alone cannot carry harmlessness, and the government as beneficiary of the error cannot rest on Duncan's failure to show prejudice. Issue 2: § 1344 punishes one who "executes" a scheme, so the April 1985 sale to the bank is a post-enactment execution, not concealment of a completed 1983 wrong; Ferrara (conspiracy reaffirmed after the amendment, no ex post facto violation) and Underwood (continuation past an effective date is a finding, reviewed for clear error, on a verdict without ambiguity) fit. Issue 3: Griffin separates factual from legal inadequacy, and a time-barred object is the legal kind (Yates, as Griffin reads it), so he would not use Griffin as a general cure; the verdict's express bank-fraud object and overt act after October 12, 1984 supply an ascertainable valid basis. Issue 4: clear error, with the Castillo framework below.

**Remedy:** Affirm; no new trial, no resentencing. If the transcript showed pervasive or defendant-specific legal characterization with no cure, a new trial limited to infected counts, not acquittal.

## Lawrence W. Pierce (Senior Circuit Judge)

**Disposition:** Issue 1: affirm. He joined Scop's holding that Rule 704 does not admit opinions embodying legal conclusions, so on the packet's description he would treat the money-laundering and IRS-obstruction labels as error to the extent they characterized the law, and would deny that Mulligan's participation in the investigation cures it. He would then hold the error harmless. Issue 2: affirm. Issue 3: sustain Count One on the express bank-fraud finding. Issue 4: affirm. Overall: affirm the judgment.

**Ground:** Issue 1: abuse of discretion, with independent review of the boundary. Harmlessness under Rule 52(a) and Kotteakos, in his Castro formulation: it must be highly probable that the error did not contribute to the verdict, the government carrying that burden; Teitler applies the substantial-and-injurious-effect inquiry. Scop reversed where the improper opinions were pervasive and central; here the labels are discrete, the transactions undisputed, and knowledge was proved through Barbieri, Corpaci and documents, but because Duncan contested knowledge and intent the labels cannot be dismissed as irrelevant, so the conclusion must rest on influence, not sufficiency. Issue 2: Torres shows he enforces the Ex Post Facto Clause strictly and looks to the instruction and finding, vacating where the jury could rely on conduct before the effective date. Here the jury expressly found a post-October 12, 1984 overt act and the April 1985 sale is an execution under § 1344, so the Torres defect is absent. Issue 3: as stated for Feinberg; no Pierce writing on general verdicts. Issue 4: Pitre treats a § 3B1.1 role finding as factual and reviewed for clear error; supported where the defendant drove price, quantity and place.

**Remedy:** Affirm; no remand. If the sentencing record shows the role finding rested only on title or receipt of proceeds, vacate the adjustment and remand for findings. If the labels were pervasive or tied to Duncan personally, a new trial limited to affected counts.

**Sources:** *United States v. Pitre*, 960 F.2d 1112 (2d Cir. 1992) (Pierce, J.). *United States v. Castro*, 813 F.2d 571 (2d Cir. 1987) (Pierce, J.). *United States v. Teitler*, 802 F.2d 606 (2d Cir. 1986) (Pierce, J.). *United States v. Torres*, 901 F.2d 205 (2d Cir. 1990) (Mahoney, J.; Pierce on panel). Pinpoints unverified.

**Join limits:** His Scop concurrence doubted the credibility holding under Rules 703 and 705, so he would hesitate to join any extension of it; it is not at issue because Mulligan was not assessing other witnesses. He would not join an opinion treating Schwartz as admitting agent legal characterizations without context review, a harmlessness rationale resting on sufficiency, a Griffin reading that ignores legal invalidity, or a role holding built on title or proceeds.

## Applying Castillo (both judges), Issue 4 only

Castillo, Part VI (1994-01-28), is a prior panel decision binding both under the circuit's prior-panel rule, to the extent of its stated rule: clear-error review of the role finding, independent reading of the guideline, a preponderance burden on the government, and the discretion, planning and control inquiry. Both apply that rule. Both distinguish its application: it upheld an inference from weighing, packaging and holding most purchase money in a § 3B1.1(c) drug case, and its stated scope refuses any general rule on proceeds or role enhancements. Duncan's sub-$10,000 distribution checks and director title cannot support the finding; what can is evidence of planning the nominee purchases, concealing them from other directors, forming the Taft Group, and directing the sham lease, if the district court so found. The numerosity predicate is unchallenged.
