## Dependence on the historical judgment

- **Rehearing or en banc petition (if any):** only the defendant would plausibly have sought it, addressing the financial-record testimony ruling (permissible assistance, Scop distinguished), the ex post facto ruling, the bank-fraud conspiracy ground sustaining Count One, or the leadership enhancement. It depends on a judgment that affirmed conviction and sentence. A reversal on any ground would shift any further petition to the government.
- **Mandate:** follows the affirmance and returns the case for execution of the existing sentence; a remand for resentencing or retrial would require new district-court proceedings.
- **Certiorari (none found):** a petition by Duncan would depend on the adverse affirmance. If a differently decided judgment vacated the convictions or sentence, the government would be the possible petitioner, and the historical absence of a petition would not fit.
