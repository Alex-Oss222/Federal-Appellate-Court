## Stone's Position

### Component 1

**Component:** Investigating-agent expertise, legal conclusions, and criminal harmlessness

**Jurisdiction and reach:** The challenge concerns Mulligan’s identified testimony, not a blanket attack on financial expertise. Keep admissibility separate from any alternative harmlessness analysis. The statutory route and unsettled jurisdictional predicates in the neutral baseline remain controlling; this proposal does not assume them away.

**Proposed disposition:** Affirm the evidentiary ruling on its limited factual-explanation basis; alternatively treat any isolated conclusory phrase under Criminal Rule 52(a).

**Governing law:** Abuse of discretion for admission, independent review of the legal-conclusion boundary. Preserved nonconstitutional error is reviewed under Criminal Rule 52(a) and Kotteakos. Rules 702 and 704 permit useful expert explanation and factual conclusions, not instruction in criminal law, pronouncements of guilt, or official credibility vouching. Any preserved error requires examination of its influence in this trial under the criminal standard; sufficiency alone is not harmlessness.

Kotteakos v. United States, 328 U.S. 750 (1946), supplies the substantial-influence inquiry for nonconstitutional criminal error. Fed. R. Evid. 702, 704(a); Fed. R. Crim. P. 52(a); United States v. Bilzerian, 926 F.2d 1285, 1294 (2d Cir. 1991); United States v. Scop, 846 F.2d 135, 140-42, modified, 856 F.2d 5 (2d Cir. 1988); Hygh v. Jacobs, 961 F.2d 359, 363-65 (2d Cir. 1992); United States v. Schwartz, 924 F.2d 410, 425-26 (2d Cir. 1991).

**Legal relationship:** Applying binding law.

**Proposed holding:** An agent may explain the financial record but receives no exemption from the prohibition on legal conclusions. The limited testimony described does not establish harmful intrusion into the jury’s role.

**Legal reasoning:** Stone should propose affirmance on the issues presented, while carefully limiting the expert-testimony rationale. Mulligan could explain the financial record, identify discrepancies in filings, and describe how the transactions affected tax administration. The testimony as described did not repeatedly instruct the jury in the statutory elements or announce that Duncan possessed the criminal intent required for conviction. That is a material distinction from the sustained legal conclusions and credibility judgments condemned in *Scop*.

Stone should not adopt a categorical rule that an investigating agent may give legal conclusions because he has personal knowledge or because the conclusion names an uncharged crime. The permitted function is factual explanation within the witness's demonstrated expertise. A statement that falsified filings interfere with tax administration can be evaluated as part of that explanation; an instruction that the accused thereby committed the charged conspiracy would cross the line. The proposed affirmance rests on the actual limited use, not on privileged status for an official witness.

If a particular phrase is treated as improperly conclusory, Stone should apply the criminal harmless-error standard. The documentary proof, direct testimony concerning the transactions, and instruction that law-enforcement witnesses receive neither special deference nor special suspicion support a finding that the isolated phrase did not substantially influence the verdict. The analysis must evaluate its role in this trial, not merely repeat that sufficient evidence remained. He should expressly avoid allocating to Duncan the civil litigant's burden of proving prejudice from a preserved evidentiary error.

**Independent or alternative considerations:** If a particular phrase crossed the boundary, the proposed alternative is Kotteakos harmlessness based on its actual role, the documentary and direct testimony, and the limiting instruction. This is not Civil Rule 61 or a burden on the accused to disprove harmlessness.

**Preferred scope:** An agent may explain the financial record but receives no exemption from the prohibition on legal conclusions. The limited testimony described does not establish harmful intrusion into the jury’s role. The lawful outer boundary is the issue and remedial limit stated here, not a broader rule about unpresented facts.

Case-level proposed scope: the challenged testimony is permissible factual assistance in context; independently, any isolated conclusory phrase is harmless under the criminal standard; the post-enactment execution of the scheme and explicit verdict defeat the temporal objections; the leadership finding is supported.

**Fallback position:** Proposed: concurrence in judgment on a supported Criminal Rule 52(a) harmlessness analysis, without joining a categorical permission for legal conclusions.

Case-level nonjoin limits, also proposed: a law-enforcement exemption from the prohibition on legal conclusions; a civil harmless-error burden in a preserved criminal evidentiary claim; retroactive punishment for a completed pre-enactment offense; one valid conspiracy object automatically curing every legally invalid alternative; or a leadership increase from corporate title alone.

**Remedy and remand:** Leave the conviction intact on the properly limited evidentiary rationale or the separately established criminal harmlessness ground.

**Reserved questions:** No permission to pronounce guilt because the witness investigated, knows the participants, or names an uncharged offense.

**Separate writing:** Proposed only. The limited testimony described does not establish harmful intrusion into the jury’s role. It would explain why the authorities and record support the disposition stated above and answer the contrary position addressed in the legal reasoning. ### Component 2

**Component:** Prospective application of the bank-fraud statute to the charged scheme

**Jurisdiction and reach:** Duncan argues that the material conduct was completed before § 1344 became effective. The jury expressly found the relevant post-enactment continuation. The statutory route and unsettled jurisdictional predicates in the neutral baseline remain controlling; this proposal does not assume them away.

**Proposed disposition:** Reject the ex post facto challenge.

**Governing law:** Independent review of ex post facto and statutory scope; evaluate the verdict and evidence without substituting a different factual theory. The government may not punish an offense completed before enactment. A genuinely continuing scheme executed after enactment may be reached, but later concealment or the lingering effects of a completed wrong do not alone extend the crime.

The Ex Post Facto Clause governs; no more specific Supreme Court holding is necessary to replace the date-eligible circuit continuation cases on this record. U.S. Const. art. I, § 9, cl. 3; 18 U.S.C. § 1344, effective October 12, 1984; United States v. Torres, 901 F.2d 205, 226 (2d Cir. 1990); Ferrara, 458 F.2d 868, 874 (2d Cir. 1972); Fletcher, 928 F.2d 495, 500 (2d Cir. 1991); Knuckles, 581 F.2d 305, 313 (2d Cir. 1978).

**Legal relationship:** Applying binding law.

**Proposed holding:** The proved, intended resale to the bank after enactment was a substantive execution of the charged scheme; the conviction does not depend solely on the earlier acquisition.

**Legal reasoning:** The ex post facto objection fails because obtaining the premium through sale to the bank was a central part of the charged scheme. The April 1985 transaction and resulting distributions are not incidental aftereffects of a completed 1983 acquisition. The jury's post-enactment finding makes the temporal predicate explicit. Stone need not rely on the later agreement to continue concealment in 1990, and should not suggest that such concealment alone would indefinitely continue every fraud.

**Independent or alternative considerations:** No additional rule is proposed beyond the interpretation and application stated here.

**Preferred scope:** The proved, intended resale to the bank after enactment was a substantive execution of the charged scheme; the conviction does not depend solely on the earlier acquisition. The lawful outer boundary is the issue and remedial limit stated here, not a broader rule about unpresented facts.

**Fallback position:** Proposed: rely only on the April 1985 transaction and the express timing finding. The case-level nonjoin limits in Component 1 apply; no trade in accuracy or jurisdiction is authorized.

**Remedy and remand:** Affirm this conviction component without relying on the 1990 concealment agreement as an automatic extension of liability.

**Reserved questions:** No rule that concealment indefinitely prolongs every fraud or that all later economic effects constitute execution.

### Component 4

**Jurisdiction and reach:** Duncan challenges his leadership role, not the numerosity or extensiveness predicate. An unraised perjury-adjustment issue is outside this component. The statutory route and unsettled jurisdictional predicates in the neutral baseline remain controlling; this proposal does not assume them away.

**Proposed disposition:** Affirm the leadership adjustment.

**Governing law:** Clear error for supported role findings; independent interpretation of the guideline. United States v. Garcia, 936 F.2d 648, 656 (2d Cir. 1991). Section 3B1.1(a) requires organizer or leader responsibility in criminal activity involving five or more participants or otherwise extensive activity. Corporate title alone cannot substitute for actual authority, planning, or direction; more than one participant can exercise leadership.

No directly controlling Supreme Court holding on this particular role finding is identified. U.S.S.G. § 3B1.1(a); 18 U.S.C. § 3742; Garcia supplies the governing factual review.

**Legal relationship:** Applying binding law.

**Proposed holding:** The credited evidence of recurring planning and direction supports leadership independent of Duncan’s Taft Group presidency.

His partners described recurring planning discussions, his central role in real-estate ventures, and his involvement in specific benefits, including the sham office lease. The district court could credit that account over his description of passive participation. An appellate judge cannot substitute a different credibility choice merely because other participants also exercised authority.
