# United States v. Duncan

**Status:** Version 1; proposed, not user-approved. **Approval reference:** None. The source brief states that every Stone position, alternative, and negotiating limit is a proposal pending approval.

## Stone's proposed judicial position

Stone should propose affirmance on the issues presented, while carefully limiting the expert-testimony rationale. Mulligan could explain the financial record, identify discrepancies in filings, and describe how the transactions affected tax administration. The testimony as described did not repeatedly instruct the jury in the statutory elements or announce that Duncan possessed the criminal intent required for conviction. That is a material distinction from the sustained legal conclusions and credibility judgments condemned in *Scop*.

Stone should not adopt a categorical rule that an investigating agent may give legal conclusions because he has personal knowledge or because the conclusion names an uncharged crime. The permitted function is factual explanation within the witness's demonstrated expertise. A statement that falsified filings interfere with tax administration can be evaluated as part of that explanation; an instruction that the accused thereby committed the charged conspiracy would cross the line. The proposed affirmance rests on the actual limited use, not on privileged status for an official witness.

If a particular phrase is treated as improperly conclusory, Stone should apply the criminal harmless-error standard. The documentary proof, direct testimony concerning the transactions, and instruction that law-enforcement witnesses receive neither special deference nor special suspicion support a finding that the isolated phrase did not substantially influence the verdict. The analysis must evaluate its role in this trial, not merely repeat that sufficient evidence remained. He should expressly avoid allocating to Duncan the civil litigant's burden of proving prejudice from a preserved evidentiary error.

The ex post facto objection fails because obtaining the premium through sale to the bank was a central part of the charged scheme. The April 1985 transaction and resulting distributions are not incidental aftereffects of a completed 1983 acquisition. The jury's post-enactment finding makes the temporal predicate explicit. Stone need not rely on the later agreement to continue concealment in 1990, and should not suggest that such concealment alone would indefinitely continue every fraud.

For Count One, the express bank-fraud finding supplies a valid conspiratorial basis independent of the challenged IRS object. The court can reserve the latter object's timeliness rather than mistakenly expand *Griffin* to every legally invalid alternative. That approach respects both the indictment's identified objectives and what the jury actually decided.

The leadership finding also survives. Duncan's presidency is only a starting fact. His partners described recurring planning discussions, his central role in real-estate ventures, and his involvement in specific benefits, including the sham office lease. The district court could credit that account over his description of passive participation. An appellate judge cannot substitute a different credibility choice merely because other participants also exercised authority. Affirmance leaves the judgment in place without resolving an unraised challenge to the perjury adjustment. Master Jurisprudence §§ 6, 8-9; Criminal Procedure and Punishment; Sentencing; Evidence, Proof, and the Record.

## Proposed negotiating limits

**Preferred and broadest defensible holding:** the challenged testimony is permissible factual assistance in context; independently, any isolated conclusory phrase is harmless under the criminal standard; the post-enactment execution of the scheme and explicit verdict defeat the temporal objections; the leadership finding is supported.

**Acceptable narrower proposal:** assume without deciding that the closest-to-conclusory phrase was improper and affirm only after applying Rule 52(a) and *Kotteakos* to its actual effect. The remaining holdings can stay confined to the 1985 sale, the express bank-fraud verdict, and the supported role findings.

**Non-join limits:** a law-enforcement exemption from the prohibition on legal conclusions; a civil harmless-error burden in a preserved criminal evidentiary claim; retroactive punishment for a completed pre-enactment offense; one valid conspiracy object automatically curing every legally invalid alternative; or a leadership increase from corporate title alone.
