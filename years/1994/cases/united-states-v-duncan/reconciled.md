## Wilfred Feinberg

**Reconciled disposition:** Affirm the judgment. Issue 1, no reversible error; Issue 2, affirm; Issue 3, sustain Count One on the express bank-fraud object; Issue 4, affirm.

**Ground:** Issue 1 in two steps. First, the legal line is as committed: Rules 702 and 704(a) admit explanation of records, fund flows and false filings, but labels given as characterizations of the law exceed Rule 704(a) under Scop and Bilzerian, and an investigator's role is no exemption. Applied to the actual testimony, the admission was within discretion, adopting history's reading of a transcript that its panel saw and no simulation stage has. Second, and alternatively, any error in the discrete labels was harmless under Rule 52(a) and Kotteakos on whole-record circumstances (isolation, objection, curative instruction, undisputed transactions, cooperating witnesses and documents on knowledge), not on sufficiency and not on Duncan's failure to show prejudice. Issues 2 and 3 as committed: the April 1985 sale to the bank is an execution of the scheme, the jury found an overt act after October 12, 1984, and Griffin is no general cure. Issue 4: clear error under Castillo's rule, resting on planning, concealment from other directors, the Taft Group and the sham lease, if the district court so found.

**Remedy:** Affirm; no new trial, no resentencing. The committed fallback (new trial limited to infected counts if characterization was pervasive and uncured) is not reached.

**Join limits:** Unchanged. No investigator exemption from Scop; no harmlessness from sufficiency alone; no Griffin cure for a legally invalid object; no Issue 2 rationale on concealment or lingering effects; no Issue 4 rationale on title or receipt of proceeds alone. Applied to history's alternative discussion, a civil-rule standard would let the government rest on Duncan's failure to show prejudice, which the commitment excludes.

**Basis for any departure from the commitment or from history:** (1) The commitment assumed error only because the transcript was unavailable, and it was expressly conditional. The record premise survives, and history is stronger evidence of what Mulligan said because the panel that read it included Feinberg. The no-error ground is therefore added ahead of the harmlessness ground; the legal line and the harmlessness ground are kept. (2) The Rule 61 alternative is not adopted. The changed premise is the governing-law frame in the packet, and his own harmlessness opinions apply whole-record criminal analysis; joining an alternative discussion not needed for the result is weak evidence he would adopt its standard. (3) Castillo does not change his outcome. It makes the standard and the government's preponderance burden binding and bars proceeds and title as bases, which his Rodriguez-Gonzalez reasoning (negotiated and directed another) already satisfies.

## Lawrence W. Pierce

**Reconciled disposition:** Affirm on all four issues, as for Feinberg.

**Ground:** Issue 1 in the same two steps. The Scop holding he joined governs the line: Rule 704 does not admit opinions embodying legal conclusions, and participation in the investigation does not cure. On the actual testimony, the admission was within discretion, per history. Alternatively, error to the extent the labels characterized the law was harmless under Rule 52(a) and Kotteakos in his Castro formulation (government's burden; highly probable the error did not contribute), with Teitler's substantial-and-injurious-effect inquiry. The labels are discrete and the transactions undisputed, but knowledge and intent were contested, so the conclusion rests on influence, not sufficiency. Issue 2: Torres-style strictness is satisfied by the express post-October 12, 1984 finding and the April 1985 execution. Issue 3: sustain Count One on the express bank-fraud finding. Issue 4: Pitre clear-error review, applying Castillo.

**Remedy:** Affirm; no remand. The committed fallbacks stay unreached: vacatur and remand for findings only if the role finding rested on title or proceeds alone; a limited new trial only if the labels were pervasive or tied to Duncan.

**Join limits:** Unchanged. Hesitation to extend Scop's credibility holding (not at issue); no treatment of Schwartz as admitting agent legal characterizations without context review; no harmlessness from sufficiency; no Griffin reading that ignores legal invalidity; no role holding on title or proceeds. A civil-rule harmlessness standard conflicts with his committed allocation of the burden to the government.

**Basis for any departure from the commitment or from history:** (1) Same as Feinberg on the no-error ground. (2) He authored the historical opinion containing the Rule 61 alternative, so this difference is specific to him. No changed fact explains it; the changed premise is legal, since the packet fixes the criminal standard and the government's burden. His own Castro and Teitler opinions are the stronger basis, history's alternative was unnecessary to the result, and its content is known only from a one-line summary. The disposition is unaffected. (3) Castillo confirms his Pitre approach and gives binding support to his conditional vacatur branch; it does not change the outcome.
