## Record and issues

**Material record:** Vinal S. Duncan was a director of Security Savings & Loan Association and a member of its loan committee. In 1983, he and other committee members arranged to purchase two Meriden Road parcels through a nominee while concealing their beneficial ownership from the remaining directors. They used a bank-financed mortgage, leased property to the bank, and subsequently approved sales to the bank without disclosing their interests. The first parcel sold for $200,000 in October 1984 and the second for $135,000 in April 1985. The transactions generated $135,000 above the initial purchase amount, and the beneficial owners received distributions through checks of less than $10,000.

The government also prosecuted a separate political-corruption arrangement. Duncan, Richard Barbieri, and John Corpaci formed the Taft Group in 1986. The group provided benefits to Waterbury Mayor Joseph Santopietro and associates in return for favorable official treatment. Barbieri and Corpaci, who pleaded guilty and cooperated, described Duncan's knowledge and participation. One transaction involved $63,000 paid through a lease of uninhabitable office space. Duncan disputed guilty knowledge and intent, rather than denying that the transactions and corrupt payments occurred.

IRS Agent Thomas Mulligan testified about the financial records, the tax system, false returns, and movement of funds. Duncan objected that portions of this expert testimony supplied legal conclusions. The agent had participated in the investigation and reviewed the records; he was not simply assessing the credibility of other witnesses from their trial testimony.

The jury convicted Duncan on Count One, a conspiracy with IRS, bank-regulatory, and bank-fraud objects; Count Two, bank fraud; Count Four, conspiracy to make corrupt payments; and Count Five, corrupt payments under 18 U.S.C. § 666(a)(2). The verdict specifically identified a bank-fraud conspiratorial object and an overt act after October 12, 1984. The court imposed concurrent prison terms, the longest sixty-five months, applying a four-level leadership adjustment to the guideline counts and a separate two-level perjury adjustment. The appellate sentencing challenge concerns leadership, not every adjustment or term. *Duncan*, 42 F.3d at 99-101.

### Issue 1: Investigating-agent expertise, legal conclusions, and criminal harmlessness

**Preservation and reach:** The challenge concerns Mulligan’s identified testimony, not a blanket attack on financial expertise. Keep admissibility separate from any alternative harmlessness analysis.

**Standard of review:** Abuse of discretion for admission, independent review of the legal-conclusion boundary. Preserved nonconstitutional error is reviewed under Criminal Rule 52(a) and Kotteakos.

**Governing test and burdens:** Rules 702 and 704 permit useful expert explanation and factual conclusions, not instruction in criminal law, pronouncements of guilt, or official credibility vouching. Any preserved error requires examination of its influence in this trial under the criminal standard; sufficiency alone is not harmlessness.

Rules 702 and 704(a) permit expert assistance and opinions embracing factual issues the jury must decide. They do not permit an expert to instruct the jury on the governing law or announce that the accused committed the charged offense. *United States v. Bilzerian*, 926 F.2d 1285, 1294 (2d Cir. 1991), *United States v. Scop*, 846 F.2d 135, 140-42 (2d Cir.), modified, 856 F.2d 5 (1988), and *Hygh v. Jacobs*, 961 F.2d 359, 363-65 (2d Cir. 1992), establish that boundary.

The government's strongest argument is that Mulligan organized a complicated financial record and explained why particular filings were false, drawing on his investigation and document review. He did not state that Duncan was guilty of the charged bank fraud or conspiracies. *United States v. Schwartz*, 924 F.2d 410, 425-26 (2d Cir. 1991), recognizes the significance of an investigator also testifying about the investigation itself.

Duncan's strongest response concerns substance rather than the witness's exact vocabulary. References to money laundering and to filings obstructing the IRS can carry an official legal judgment even without the sentence “the defendant is guilty.” The fact that a label describes an uncharged offense does not automatically make it admissible. Nor does personal participation in an investigation authorize a witness to assess other witnesses' credibility or apply the criminal law for the jury. The court must examine the particular testimony in context, not award law-enforcement witnesses an exemption from *Scop*.

**Historical Supreme Court authority:** Kotteakos v. United States, 328 U.S. 750 (1946), supplies the substantial-influence inquiry for nonconstitutional criminal error.

**Other governing authority:** Fed. R. Evid. 702, 704(a); Fed. R. Crim. P. 52(a); United States v. Bilzerian, 926 F.2d 1285, 1294 (2d Cir. 1991); United States v. Scop, 846 F.2d 135, 140-42, modified, 856 F.2d 5 (2d Cir. 1988); Hygh v. Jacobs, 961 F.2d 359, 363-65 (2d Cir. 1992); United States v. Schwartz, 924 F.2d 410, 425-26 (2d Cir. 1991).

**Applicable simulation holding:** None supplied or verified. No proposed position in these briefs is an adopted holding.

**Open question or conflict:** The actual function of the challenged phrases, rather than a categorical exemption for investigating agents or statements about uncharged crimes.

**Appellant's strongest supported position:** Labels concerning money laundering and obstruction of the IRS conveyed authoritative legal judgments; omission of the exact word guilty does not cure the intrusion.

**Appellee's strongest supported position:** The agent explained documentary transactions and false filings using investigative knowledge, without instructing the jury on charged elements or announcing Duncan’s guilt. Any isolated overstatement was nonprejudicial in context.

### Issue 2: Prospective application of the bank-fraud statute to the charged scheme

**Preservation and reach:** Duncan argues that the material conduct was completed before § 1344 became effective. The jury expressly found the relevant post-enactment continuation.

**Standard of review:** Independent review of ex post facto and statutory scope; evaluate the verdict and evidence without substituting a different factual theory.

**Governing test and burdens:** The government may not punish an offense completed before enactment. A genuinely continuing scheme executed after enactment may be reached, but later concealment or the lingering effects of a completed wrong do not alone extend the crime.

The Ex Post Facto Clause forbids application of § 1344 to an offense completed before that statute took effect on October 12, 1984. *United States v. Torres*, 901 F.2d 205, 226 (2d Cir. 1990), and *United States v. Ferrara*, 458 F.2d 868, 874 (2d Cir. 1972), permit application to criminal activity continuing after the relevant enactment. *United States v. Fletcher*, 928 F.2d 495, 500 (2d Cir. 1991), and *United States v. Knuckles*, 581 F.2d 305, 313 (2d Cir. 1978), explain that an agreement whose object includes receipt of anticipated profits may continue until that object is realized.

Duncan characterizes the wrong as appropriation of a corporate opportunity in 1983. The prosecution's charged theory was broader in a legally material way: purchase through a concealed nominee followed by profitable resale to the bank. The 1985 sale was execution of that intended extraction of bank funds, not merely a later effort to conceal an already completed crime. That difference permits prospective application without holding that any subsequent concealment indefinitely prolongs liability.

**Historical Supreme Court authority:** The Ex Post Facto Clause governs; no more specific Supreme Court holding is necessary to replace the date-eligible circuit continuation cases on this record.

**Other governing authority:** U.S. Const. art. I, § 9, cl. 3; 18 U.S.C. § 1344, effective October 12, 1984; United States v. Torres, 901 F.2d 205, 226 (2d Cir. 1990); Ferrara, 458 F.2d 868, 874 (2d Cir. 1972); Fletcher, 928 F.2d 495, 500 (2d Cir. 1991); Knuckles, 581 F.2d 305, 313 (2d Cir. 1978).

**Applicable simulation holding:** None supplied or verified. No proposed position in these briefs is an adopted holding.

**Open question or conflict:** The legally material scope and completion point of the charged scheme, not the power to criminalize completed pre-enactment conduct.

**Appellant's strongest supported position:** The corporate-opportunity appropriation was accomplished in 1983; later transactions and concealment cannot retroactively make that conduct a new federal offense.

**Appellee's strongest supported position:** The charged object included secret nominee acquisition followed by profitable resale to the bank. The April 1985 execution and receipt of profits were intended parts of the scheme, not mere aftereffects.

**Additional material position:** No independently verified additional amicus position is supplied. Counsel or amicus names alone are not evidence of an argument.

**Error and remedy constraints:** Preserved nonconstitutional trial error takes Criminal Rule 52(a) and Kotteakos; forfeited objections take Rule 52(b) and Olano where applicable, as identified above. Constitutional trial error has its own harmlessness rule; a structural defect is not excused by a sufficiency assessment. Relief must match the particular proven error; insufficiency is not presumed from trial error.

### Issue 3: The challenged IRS conspiracy object and the express bank-fraud verdict

**Preservation and reach:** The limitations objection concerns the IRS object of Count One. The verdict separately identifies the bank-fraud object and its timing; do not treat it as a wholly silent general verdict.

**Standard of review:** Independent review of the legal effect of the identified alternative bases and limitations issue.

**Governing test and burdens:** An evidentially deficient alternative is not the same as a legally unavailable basis. A demonstrably independent, valid basis identified by the jury can support the count without assuming how a silent verdict was reached.

*Griffin v. United States*, 502 U.S. 46 (1991), distinguishes an evidentially unsupported alternative from a legally impermissible basis for conviction. It should not be summarized as a rule that one good object always cures another object that is legally unavailable. Here, the verdict's express identification of bank-fraud conspiracy and its timing finding make the relevant valid basis ascertainable without merely presuming that a silent general verdict selected it. The asserted infirmity of the IRS object therefore need not invalidate the separately established basis for Count One.

**Historical Supreme Court authority:** Griffin v. United States, 502 U.S. 46 (1991), distinguishes factual insufficiency from legal invalidity and is not a universal one-valid-object cure.

**Other governing authority:** 18 U.S.C. § 371 and the charged bank-fraud object; the specific verdict and timing finding, rather than a new rule about every possible limitations period.

### Issue 4: The organizer-or-leader adjustment

**Preservation and reach:** Duncan challenges his leadership role, not the numerosity or extensiveness predicate. An unraised perjury-adjustment issue is outside this component.

**Standard of review:** Clear error for supported role findings; independent interpretation of the guideline. United States v. Garcia, 936 F.2d 648, 656 (2d Cir. 1991).

**Governing test and burdens:** Section 3B1.1(a) requires organizer or leader responsibility in criminal activity involving five or more participants or otherwise extensive activity. Corporate title alone cannot substitute for actual authority, planning, or direction; more than one participant can exercise leadership.

Section 3B1.1(a) supplies the four-level increase for an organizer or leader of sufficiently numerous or extensive criminal activity. Duncan did not challenge the size or extensiveness predicate, but denied being a leader. *United States v. Garcia*, 936 F.2d 648, 656 (2d Cir. 1991), requires clear-error review of the role finding. Corporate title alone is insufficient; planning, authority, and actual participation in directing the criminal operation must support the conclusion.

**Historical Supreme Court authority:** No directly controlling Supreme Court holding on this particular role finding is identified.
