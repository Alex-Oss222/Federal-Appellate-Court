# United States v. Knoll

**Status:** Version 1; user-approved. **Baseline:** February 14, 1994 packet. **Approval reference:** User instruction of 2026-10-12 approving all eighteen Cycle 001 briefs as written ("all ... approved - change"), subject to each brief’s stated conditions. **Conditions:** Resubmission checks above. **Approved fallback or separate writing:** None.

**Component: Count four.**  
Under § 1291 and de novo statutory review, propose reversal and dismissal for both defendants. Section 3284’s continuing-offense treatment should remain confined to the offense it names. The government’s evidence cannot enlarge a completed false-declaration charge into a different continuing offense.

**Component: Knoll’s suppression claim.**  
Propose a limited remand while preserving the supported finding that officials did not instigate the burglary. An official may join an unconstitutional search after its private beginning. The proposed holding would require findings about what remained unexamined when the prosecutor encouraged further production, rather than treating possession of stolen files as permission to inspect every enclosed paper. Relief must then follow the exclusionary rule and the causal record, not the seriousness of the intrusion alone. ([OpenJurist](https://openjurist.org/16/f3d/1313))

**Component: Gleave and remaining issues.**  
Propose rejection of Gleave’s claim as to the business communications and bank records actually identified, without a general rule eliminating privacy in client files. Reject the reported sufficiency, comment, evidentiary, self-representation, and speedy-trial grounds where the record supports the described rulings. Do not turn a limited suppression remand into a final determination of Knoll’s surviving conviction. This follows Stone’s distinction between error, prejudice, and the particular remedy authorized.
