On the plaintiff-favorable view, the evidence (known broken pins, persistent pain, the disclosure and referral sequence, delayed response) permitted a finding of deliberate indifference despite some treatment, and the immunity argument did not require judgment for Foote. Further trial proceedings remained possible; no liability or damages were found. Altimari wrote and McLaughlin joined. Jacobs dissented, stressing the treatment given, the specialist decisions, and the line between disagreement over care and the mental element. Mandate status is unknown.

## Frank X. Altimari

**Reconciled disposition:** Take jurisdiction only over the qualified-immunity denial as a question of law, on Hathaway's evidence and reasonable inferences. Affirm the denial insofar as it rests on immunity. Dismiss any remainder that seeks Rule 50 sufficiency review apart from immunity or that depends on crediting Foote's account. Foote's actual awareness is for the jury.

**Ground:** Mitchell (472 U.S. at 530) and Moffitt limit the reach to the purely legal question. On Hathaway's version, the undisclosed broken-pin finding, his July 1981 willingness to have surgery and the promised referral, and the late 1982 referral permit a rational jury to find actual awareness and disregard under Estelle, Wilson and Farmer. Conservative-care and specialist evidence is a competing inference for the jury. Notice comes from Estelle, Williams v. Vincent and Todaro, for a narrowly defined prohibition (consciously disregarding a known serious medical need by persistent inaction), applied through the Jermosen method as of 1980-82. Farmer states the present merits standard and creates no notice. Hathaway I (1988) postdates the conduct and cannot supply notice.

**Remedy:** Affirm in part, dismiss in part, remand for retrial. No damages ruling. The other defendants' judgments are not before the court.

**Join limits:** Unchanged. He would not join a rationale that decides Foote's knowledge or credibility, uses Farmer or the 1988 mandate to create notice, states the right as a general medical-care duty, or reviews Rule 50 sufficiency apart from immunity. He would not join a categorical expert-proof rule either way. He would join full dismissal only if the record shows the Rule 50 ruling reserved or conditioned immunity.

**Basis for any departure from the commitment or from history:** None on disposition, ground or remedy. Three differences are noted. (a) Label: history.md says "affirmed insofar as reviewable" and does not say that the remainder was formally dismissed or that the case was remanded. The commitment's dismissal of the remainder follows from the reach limit that history's own phrase implies. No changed premise explains the label, so the commitment stays. If history only declined to reach the remainder, the gap is formal. (b) Framing: history states the holding as what the evidence permitted. The commitment's phrase "no judgment for Foote" has to be read as the constitutional-violation prong of immunity (Siegert), consistent with his own limit (1) and not as independent Rule 50 review. Because history.md does not show how the historical opinion framed it, the join limit is kept. (c) Whalen is a changed premise. It matters little here. Its scope is a without-prejudice summary-judgment denial and does not reach a Rule 50 ruling on a tried record after a mistrial. The commitment already follows it as consistent circuit law and distinguishes it. History's reaching of immunity is some evidence the order was not treated as a reservation, not proof.

## Joseph M. McLaughlin

**Reconciled disposition:** The same components. Reach only the legal immunity question on Hathaway's version, affirm the denial insofar as it rests on immunity, dismiss the remainder, and leave Foote's knowledge and the reason for the delay for retrial. He remains the stricter policeman of arguments that depend on Foote's contrary account.

**Ground:** Mitchell (528 n.9, 530), Moffitt, Siegert and Elder, de novo. The plaintiff-favorable evidence permits an inference of actual awareness and disregard under Estelle, Wilson and Farmer, with competing inferences left to a jury (Hendricks). Estelle gives notice for 1980-82 conduct, supported by Kaminsky's treatment of the right and by Williams and Todaro. Farmer does not create notice.

**Remedy:** Affirm in part, dismiss the remainder for lack of appellate jurisdiction, remand for retrial. No damages determination. No review of other defendants' judgments.

**Join limits:** Unchanged. He would not join a rationale that decides Foote's knowledge or credits his explanation, reviews Rule 50 sufficiency apart from immunity, treats Farmer as creating notice, or announces a categorical expert-proof rule. He would join full dismissal if the record shows immunity was reserved.

**Basis for any departure from the commitment or from history:** None. His historical full join of Altimari's opinion is evidence that he accepts a partial-reach affirmance on this record, which is the commitment's primary path. His dismissal alternative (Kaminsky, Moffitt) stays live only on the same missing fact, whether the order was without prejudice to renewal or reserved immunity. Whalen matters to him as to Altimari: it is binding within its scope, and that scope does not cover this posture. History.md says nothing on expert proof, the role of the 1988 mandate, or the level of generality of the right, so those parts of his commitment rest on the commitment alone.

## Agreement between the two modeled judges (by component)

- **Jurisdictional reach:** Agree. Legal immunity question only, on the plaintiff's version, with the remainder dismissed. Conditional alternative of full dismissal, which both tie to the same record fact (immunity reserved or conditioned).
- **Merits threshold:** Agree. Hathaway's evidence permits a finding of actual awareness and disregard. Knowledge is for the jury.
- **Notice and immunity:** Agree on result and on the rule that Estelle supplies notice and Farmer does not. Support differs but is compatible: Altimari adds the Jermosen method, McLaughlin adds Kaminsky. A possible friction point is the level of generality. Altimari bars a general medical-care duty. Kaminsky's formulation, as the commitment reports it, is the right to be free from deliberate indifference to serious medical needs. Drafting the right at Altimari's narrower level meets both commitments, and McLaughlin states no barrier to it.
- **Expert proof:** Agree. Neither would adopt a categorical rule, and no rule is needed for a lay-comprehensible sequence.
- **Remedy:** Agree. Affirm in part, dismiss in part, remand for retrial, no damages ruling.
- **Join limits:** Compatible. Neither limit excludes a rationale the other requires. Altimari alone states a limit on use of the 1988 mandate, and McLaughlin's commitment is silent, so there is no conflict but no confirmed agreement on that point.
- **Authorship:** Neither commitment addresses it. History shows Altimari writing with McLaughlin joining, which is evidence only.

Result: the two reconciled positions agree on every component above. Two of the three votes therefore exist for the primary path, and for the full-dismissal alternative if the missing record fact is shown, with no reliance on the third seat.
