# Commitments: William Hathaway v. Thomas A. Coughlin et al. (Foote, appellant), No. 93-2732

**Stage:** neutral modeling. **Legal cutoff:** 1994-09-29. **Inputs:** neutral.md, ENGINE.md, and the public sources cited below.

## Frank X. Altimari (Active Circuit Judge)

**Disposition:** (1) Jurisdiction: exercise it only over the qualified-immunity denial as a question of law, taking Hathaway's evidence and reasonable inferences as true; dismiss for want of appellate jurisdiction any part of Foote's appeal that seeks ordinary Rule 50 sufficiency review apart from immunity, or that depends on crediting Foote's version. (2) Immunity and sufficiency on that view: no immunity and no judgment for Foote; a jury could find actual awareness and disregard rather than negligence or disagreement over treatment, and the governing right was clearly established in 1980-82. (3) Foote's actual state of mind stays with the jury.

**Ground:** Mitchell permits immediate appeal "to the extent that it turns on an issue of law," 472 U.S. 511, 530 (1985); the appealable issue is "purely legal," id. at 528 n.9 (n.9 as quoted in Mahoney v. Hankin; the 530 language as quoted in Moffitt). He sat on the panels in Mahoney, Hurlman and Moffitt, each dismissing an immunity appeal because adjudication required resolving facts, and in Washington Square Post, which reached the defense because the agents were immune "if the facts are as asserted" by the non-movant and the claimed disputes were irrelevant. Foote's appeal reaches only the second kind of question. Review of the legal question is de novo on all relevant precedent (Elder v. Holloway, 510 U.S. 510, decided Feb. 23, 1994), with the threshold question whether a constitutional violation is shown at all (Siegert v. Gilley, 500 U.S. 226, 232 (1991)). Rule 50 standard: Hendricks v. Coughlin, 942 F.2d 109 (2d Cir. 1991). His own formulation, in his Robison v. Via partial dissent, removes a claim from the jury only when he does "not believe a rational fact-finder could return a verdict" for the plaintiff. A rational jury could here: persistent pain; broken pins found July 1980 and not disclosed; Hathaway's July 1981 testimony that he would accept surgery and Foote promised a referral; no referral until late 1982, after correspondence and suit. The conservative-care and specialist evidence supplies a competing inference, and the mistrial shows it is genuine. Merits test: Estelle v. Gamble, 429 U.S. 97, 104-06 (1976) (indifference includes intentionally delaying care or interfering with prescribed treatment; negligence and medical judgment do not suffice); Wilson v. Seiter, 501 U.S. 294 (1991) (culpable state of mind); Farmer v. Brennan (June 6, 1994) (subjective knowledge, inferable from circumstances). Farmer states the standard at decision and is not needed for notice. Notice: he applies the three-part method of his Jermosen opinion (right defined with reasonable specificity; supported by Supreme Court and circuit decisional law; reasonable official would understand the conduct unlawful), as the law stood when the defendant acted. Defined as a prohibition on consciously disregarding a known serious medical need by persistent inaction, not a right to a given operation on a given date, Estelle, Williams v. Vincent (2d Cir. 1974) and Todaro v. Ward (2d Cir. 1977) supply notice. Hathaway I (1988) postdates the conduct and cannot, though he sat on that panel and it found a triable deliberate-indifference question about the delay after July 1981. Expert proof: no pre-cutoff holding requiring or excusing it was located; the lay-comprehensible sequence needs no universal expert rule. Whalen v. County of Fulton (1994-03-29) binds only within its stated scope: an immunity summary-judgment denial without prejudice, reserving the defense, is not conclusive or appealable. It does not reach a Rule 50 ruling on a tried record after a mistrial, so he would follow it as consistent circuit law (labels do not control; a functionally conclusive rejection of a purely legal defense is reviewable) and distinguish it unless the Rule 50 order reserved immunity for later decision. The plausible alternative is full dismissal on that footing, or because immunity cannot be separated from disputed facts, as in the three dismissals he joined; the missing Rule 50 papers keep it live.

**Remedy:** Affirm the denial of Rule 50 relief insofar as it rests on immunity; dismiss the remainder of the appeal; remand for retrial of the claim against Foote. No damages ruling; the other defendants' judgments are not before the court.

**Sources:** Authored: Jermosen v. Smith, 945 F.2d 547 (2d Cir. 1991) (immunity; clearly established method; narrow definition of right); Robison v. Via, 821 F.2d 913 (2d Cir. 1987) (partial dissent, rational-factfinder threshold; he joined the qualified-immunity holding). Panel member, opinion by another: Hathaway v. Coughlin, 841 F.2d 48 (2d Cir. 1988); Moffitt v. Town of Brookfield, 950 F.2d 880 (2d Cir. 1991); Hurlman v. Rice, 927 F.2d 74 (2d Cir. 1991); Mahoney v. Hankin, 844 F.2d 64 (2d Cir. 1988); Washington Square Post No. 1212 v. Maduro, 907 F.2d 1288 (2d Cir. 1990). Authorities: Mitchell; Estelle; Wilson; Farmer; Anderson v. Creighton, 483 U.S. 635, 639-40 (1987); Elder; Siegert; Hendricks; Williams v. Vincent, 508 F.2d 541 (2d Cir. 1974); Todaro v. Ward, 565 F.2d 48 (2d Cir. 1977); Whalen (supplied extract).

**Join limits:** Would not join a rationale that resolves Foote's knowledge or credibility either way, that uses Farmer or the 1988 mandate to create notice, that defines the right at the level of a general medical-care duty, or that reviews Rule 50 sufficiency apart from immunity. Would not join a holding that expert testimony is always, or never, required. Would join full dismissal only if the record shows the Rule 50 ruling reserved or conditioned immunity. Routine agreement on remand for retrial.

## Joseph M. McLaughlin (Active Circuit Judge)

**Disposition:** Same components, from his own record. (1) Jurisdiction limited to the legal immunity question on Hathaway's version; dismiss the rest. (2) On that version Foote is not entitled to immunity or judgment as a matter of law. (3) Foote's knowledge and the reason for the delay are for retrial. His sharper concern is policing fact disputes smuggled into a Mitchell appeal, so he is more likely than Altimari to insist that any argument resting on Foote's contrary account is outside jurisdiction.

**Ground:** No authored opinion on these questions was located; this rests on opinions he sat on. In Kaminsky v. Rosenblum (Cardamone, J.), an Eighth Amendment medical-care appeal on 1985-86 conduct, the court dismissed because objective reasonableness turned on disputed facts (knowledge of a hospitalization recommendation, an unexplained gap in care), and in the same opinion confirmed that the right to be free from deliberate indifference to serious medical needs was clearly established. In Prue v. City of Syracuse (June 6, 1994) he sat on a panel that accepted jurisdiction because immunity "turns solely on a question of law" and defined the right narrowly. In Golino v. City of New Haven the court took jurisdiction only "to the extent that it can be decided as a matter of law," drew inferences for the non-movant, and took pendent jurisdiction over related questions. Applied here: Foote's sufficiency argument is reviewable only as the legal question whether Hathaway's evidence shows a violation of clearly established law (Mitchell, 472 U.S. at 528 n.9, 530; Moffitt; Siegert; Elder, de novo). The same plaintiff-favorable evidence as above permits an inference of actual awareness and disregard under Estelle, Wilson and Farmer, with competing inferences left to a jury (Hendricks). Because the conduct predates Farmer, Estelle supplies notice, supported by Kaminsky's treatment of the right and by Williams v. Vincent and Todaro. Whalen: same bearing as above; he would treat its scope limit as decisive against extending it to a tried Rule 50 record, while adopting its functional approach. The live alternative is dismissal of the whole appeal under Kaminsky and Moffitt if the Rule 50 order was without prejudice to renewal, or if Foote's argument cannot be separated from his disputed account.

**Remedy:** Affirm denial insofar as it rests on immunity; dismiss the remainder for lack of appellate jurisdiction; remand for retrial. No damages determination; no review of other defendants' judgments.

**Sources:** Panel member, opinion by another: Kaminsky v. Rosenblum, 929 F.2d 922 (2d Cir. 1991); Golino v. City of New Haven, 950 F.2d 864 (2d Cir. 1991); Prue v. City of Syracuse, 26 F.3d 14 (2d Cir. 1994). Authorities: Mitchell; Moffitt; Estelle; Wilson; Farmer; Siegert; Elder; Hendricks; Williams v. Vincent; Todaro; Whalen (supplied extract).

**Join limits:** Would not join a rationale that decides Foote's knowledge or credits his explanation, that reviews Rule 50 sufficiency apart from immunity, or that treats Farmer as creating notice. Would not join a categorical expert-testimony rule. Would join dismissal if the record shows immunity was reserved. Routine agreement on retrial.
