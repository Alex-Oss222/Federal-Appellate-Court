# Hathaway v. Coughlin

**Case:** Hathaway v. Coughlin, docket 93-2732

**Historical event and date:** Panel disposition on 1994-09-29; 37 F.3d 63 (2d Cir. 1994). Only the selected event is described.

**Historical disposition:** Affirmed denial of Foote’s post-mistrial motion insofar as the qualified-immunity issue was reviewable.

**Historical panel:** Frank X. Altimari, Joseph M. McLaughlin, Dennis Jacobs.

**Opinion author and joins:** Frank X. Altimari wrote the opinion. Joined by McLaughlin.

**Historical holding and grounds:** The evidence, viewed favorably to Hathaway, permitted a finding of deliberate indifference to a serious medical need notwithstanding some treatment. Knowledge of broken pins, severe persistent pain, the course of disclosure and referral, and delayed response could support culpable disregard rather than merely medical negligence. The legal immunity argument did not require judgment for Foote on that account.

**Separate opinions:** Jacobs dissented, emphasizing the treatment provided, specialist decisions, the evidence regarding surgery, and the distinction between disagreement over medical care and the constitutional mental element.

**Remedy and mandate:** Further trial proceedings remained possible; the appellate decision did not itself find Foote liable or award damages. The separate mandate’s issuance date and any stay or recall status are not established by an independently supplied docket; they are left unknown.
