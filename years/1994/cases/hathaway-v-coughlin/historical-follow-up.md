## Dependence on the historical judgment

- **Certiorari petition (No. 94-1144).** Foote sought review as the losing party. It depended on the affirmance of the order denying his Rule 50 motions after the mistrial, and on the Second Circuit's collateral-order jurisdiction over a qualified-immunity denial. A judgment granting Foote immunity or judgment as a matter of law would leave him no petition and would make Hathaway the likely petitioner. A dismissal for want of jurisdiction would change the question presented.
- **Retrial, verdict and 1996 appeal.** The retrial against Foote depended on the earlier mistrial plus the affirmance, which left the deliberate-indifference question for a jury. Hathaway's 1996 appeal depended on a defense verdict at that retrial and on the jury charge then given. A different 1994 judgment ending the claim against Foote would remove all of these events.
- **99 F.3d 550 remand.** It is a later Second Circuit event on the same claim and presupposes a retrial held after the 1994 affirmance.
