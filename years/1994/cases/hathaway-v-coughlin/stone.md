# Hathaway v. Coughlin

**Status:** Version 1; proposed, not user-approved. **Approval reference:** None. The source brief states that every Stone position, alternative, and negotiating limit is a proposal pending approval.

## Stone's proposed judicial position

Stone should propose affirmance of the denial of qualified-immunity-based judgment as a matter of law and return the case for retrial. He should not announce that Hathaway has proved deliberate indifference. His conclusion is that a reasonable jury, crediting Hathaway's testimony and the documented sequence, could find it, and that the governing prohibition was sufficiently established by *Estelle*.

The medical details must do the work. Hathaway's earlier rejection of surgery is relevant but not permanently dispositive. The later discovery of broken pins and his testimony that he expressed willingness to proceed materially change the premise on which reliance on that refusal rests. Similarly, medication and accommodations are evidence of care, but they do not compel a conclusion that every subsequent delay reflected medical judgment. A jury could regard repeated reports of severe pain and the failure to implement a promised reassessment as evidence of knowing disregard.

Foote must remain able to show that he reasonably relied on specialist advice, understood the complaints differently, or made a genuine judgment that surgery's risks or limited likely benefits favored continued conservative treatment. Stone should not convert the eventual decision to operate into a retrospective constitutional prescription. The difference between unsuccessful treatment and deliberately withholding a response is the question for the factfinder, not a distinction abolished in favor of either party.

An expert is not invariably required to permit an inference from prolonged pain, withheld information, and unexplained failure to act on known facts. Some medical questions do require expert proof, particularly where a lay jury cannot evaluate a treatment choice or causal connection. This case should not establish that expert evidence is categorically unnecessary in prison medical litigation. The narrower conclusion is that the evidence described here can support the requisite inference without a universal expert-testimony prerequisite.

The remand should leave causation, compensable injury, and Foote's actual knowledge for determination under proper instructions. Liability must rest on his own conduct. No claim against the other officials is revived, and no damages are awarded merely because the first jury could not agree. Master Jurisprudence §§ 8-9, 18, 25; Criminal Procedure and Punishment; Personal Liberty; Remedies.

## Proposed negotiating limits

**Preferred and broadest defensible holding:** a jury may find deliberate indifference from knowing persistence in ineffective treatment and delay in responding to a materially changed, serious condition, notwithstanding some ongoing care; immunity does not require judgment for Foote on the plaintiff-favorable record.

**Acceptable narrower proposal:** affirm because the evidence concerning notification, renewed willingness to undergo surgery, and the delayed referral alone creates a triable account of knowing disregard. The opinion can reserve broader descriptions of ineffective-treatment liability and the general role of medical experts.

**Non-join limits:** an entitlement to surgery whenever an inmate requests it; constitutional liability for negligence alone; an automatic immunity rule whenever any treatment was supplied; or use of *Farmer* to impose notice that did not exist when Foote acted.
