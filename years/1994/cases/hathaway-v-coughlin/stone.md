## Stone's Position

### Component 1

**Component:** Interlocutory reach after a mistrial

**Jurisdiction and reach:** Foote appeals denial of Rule 50 relief after a deadlocked jury. The legal immunity question must be separated from an ordinary request to reweigh trial evidence. The statutory route and unsettled jurisdictional predicates in the neutral baseline remain controlling; this proposal does not assume them away.

**Proposed disposition:** Reach only the legal immunity issue on a plaintiff-favorable account; do not exercise general interlocutory sufficiency jurisdiction.

**Governing law:** Independent review of § 1291 and the collateral-order basis; plaintiff-favorable treatment of genuinely disputed evidence for a legally reachable immunity question. A denial of judgment as a matter of law after mistrial ordinarily lacks finality. Mitchell’s exception reaches a legal denial of qualified immunity, not unrestricted merits review or appellate resolution of credibility.

Mitchell v. Forsyth, 472 U.S. 511, 530 (1985), controlling at this date; no later refinement is imported. 28 U.S.C. § 1291; Moffitt v. Town of Brookfield, 950 F.2d 880, 884 (2d Cir. 1991); Fed. R. Civ. P. 50.

**Legal relationship:** Applying binding law.

**Proposed holding:** The existence of a post-mistrial Rule 50 order does not itself create finality; any present appellate power rests on the distinct legal immunity question.

**Legal reasoning:** Mitchell protects the entitlement not to stand trial when it is legally established. Its protection does not supply a power to decide whether Foote or Hathaway should be believed. Framing the evidence favorably to Hathaway permits the legal immunity issue to be tested without converting the appeal into a retrial.

**Independent or alternative considerations:** No additional rule is proposed beyond the interpretation and application stated here.

**Preferred scope:** The existence of a post-mistrial Rule 50 order does not itself create finality; any present appellate power rests on the distinct legal immunity question. The lawful outer boundary is the issue and remedial limit stated here, not a broader rule about unpresented facts.

Case-level proposed scope: a jury may find deliberate indifference from knowing persistence in ineffective treatment and delay in responding to a materially changed, serious condition, notwithstanding some ongoing care; immunity does not require judgment for Foote on the plaintiff-favorable record.

**Fallback position:** Proposed: dismiss any component that cannot be resolved as a legal immunity question.

Case-level nonjoin limits, also proposed: an entitlement to surgery whenever an inmate requests it; constitutional liability for negligence alone; an automatic immunity rule whenever any treatment was supplied; or use of *Farmer* to impose notice that did not exist when Foote acted.

**Remedy and remand:** Limit the appellate judgment to the legal defense; leave ordinary factual questions for retrial.

**Reserved questions:** Any nonfinal issue outside the collateral order and unrelated defendants’ judgments.

**Separate writing:** Proposed only. It would explain why the authorities and record support the disposition stated above and answer the contrary position addressed in the legal reasoning. ### Component 2

**Component:** Deliberate indifference, expert proof, and historically established immunity law

**Jurisdiction and reach:** Foote disputes both constitutional sufficiency and immunity. The standard available at decision must be distinguished from the law giving notice during the earlier treatment. The statutory route and unsettled jurisdictional predicates in the neutral baseline remain controlling; this proposal does not assume them away.

**Proposed disposition:** Affirm denial of qualified-immunity-based judgment and return the case for retrial.

**Governing law:** View evidence and reasonable inferences favorably to Hathaway under Hendricks; apply the legal culpability and immunity rules independently without deciding credibility. A serious medical need and actual awareness and disregard are required; negligence or disagreement over treatment does not suffice. Knowledge may be inferred from the actual medical sequence. Qualified immunity concerns clearly established law at the time of conduct, not notice created by this appeal.

Estelle v. Gamble, 429 U.S. 97, 104-06 (1976); Wilson v. Seiter, 501 U.S. 294 (1991); Farmer v. Brennan, 511 U.S. 825, 837, 842-45 (1994). Farmer articulates the current merits standard but does not retrospectively create notice. 42 U.S.C. § 1983; Hendricks v. Coughlin, 942 F.2d 109, 112 (2d Cir. 1991); the earlier Hathaway mandate at 841 F.2d 48 (1988).

**Legal relationship:** Applying binding law.

**Proposed holding:** The described combination of knowledge, prolonged pain, withheld information, and failure to respond permits a deliberate-indifference finding; treatment of some kind does not conclusively defeat it.

**Legal reasoning:** Stone should propose affirmance of the denial of qualified-immunity-based judgment as a matter of law and return the case for retrial. He should not announce that Hathaway has proved deliberate indifference. His conclusion is that a reasonable jury, crediting Hathaway's testimony and the documented sequence, could find it, and that the governing prohibition was sufficiently established by *Estelle*.

The medical details must do the work. Hathaway's earlier rejection of surgery is relevant but not permanently dispositive. The later discovery of broken pins and his testimony that he expressed willingness to proceed materially change the premise on which reliance on that refusal rests. Similarly, medication and accommodations are evidence of care, but they do not compel a conclusion that every subsequent delay reflected medical judgment. A jury could regard repeated reports of severe pain and the failure to implement a promised reassessment as evidence of knowing disregard.

Foote must remain able to show that he reasonably relied on specialist advice, understood the complaints differently, or made a genuine judgment that surgery's risks or limited likely benefits favored continued conservative treatment. Stone should not convert the eventual decision to operate into a retrospective constitutional prescription. The difference between unsuccessful treatment and deliberately withholding a response is the question for the factfinder, not a distinction abolished in favor of either party.

An expert is not invariably required to permit an inference from prolonged pain, withheld information, and unexplained failure to act on known facts. Some medical questions do require expert proof, particularly where a lay jury cannot evaluate a treatment choice or causal connection. This case should not establish that expert evidence is categorically unnecessary in prison medical litigation. The narrower conclusion is that the evidence described here can support the requisite inference without a universal expert-testimony prerequisite.

The remand should leave causation, compensable injury, and Foote's actual knowledge for determination under proper instructions. Liability must rest on his own conduct. No claim against the other officials is revived, and no damages are awarded merely because the first jury could not agree.

**Independent or alternative considerations:** No additional rule is proposed beyond the interpretation and application stated here.

**Preferred scope:** The described combination of knowledge, prolonged pain, withheld information, and failure to respond permits a deliberate-indifference finding; treatment of some kind does not conclusively defeat it. The lawful outer boundary is the issue and remedial limit stated here, not a broader rule about unpresented facts.

**Fallback position:** Proposed, not approved: affirm because the evidence concerning notification, renewed willingness to undergo surgery, and the delayed referral alone creates a triable account of knowing disregard. The opinion can reserve broader descriptions of ineffective-treatment liability and the general role of medical experts. The case-level nonjoin limits in Component 1 apply; no trade in accuracy or jurisdiction is authorized.

**Remedy and remand:** Try actual knowledge, culpability, causation, and injury with proper instructions. Do not award damages or revive claims against other officials.

**Reserved questions:** No constitutional right to a particular surgery on a fixed date and no general rule eliminating the need for medical experts.
