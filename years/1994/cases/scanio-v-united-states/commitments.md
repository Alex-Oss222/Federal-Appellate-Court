## Ellsworth A. Van Graafeiland (Senior Circuit Judge)

**Disposition:** (1) Custody and term calculation: sustains the conclusion that the two-year supervised release term began with the November 27, 1991 release and ended before the motion was docketed on November 29, 1993; the district court's word "probation" is a misdescription that does not change the result. (2) Rule 6(a) and the closure: rejects the rule as a basis for treating custody as continuing through the first reopening day. (3) Constructive-tender theory: not adopted on this record. (4) Judgment: affirms dismissal for want of custody, with no ruling on the ineffective-assistance merits or on coram nobis. Confidence is highest on (1) and lower on (2) and (3), which none of his verified writings addresses.

**Ground:** Review is independent for custody and sentence construction and for clear error on historical facts (the release dates are not disputed). Section 2255 requires custody under the challenged sentence when the motion is filed: Brilliant (custody jurisdictional; parole is custody), Carafas and Maleng (custody measured at filing; collateral consequences of an expired sentence are not enough), and Wheel v. Robinson, 34 F.3d 60 (2d Cir. Aug. 25, 1994) (same, applying Maleng). Supervision can qualify under Jones. Supervised release commences on release from imprisonment (18 U.S.C. § 3624(e)), and halfway-house placement is prerelease custody inside the term (§ 3624(c)), so release from the halfway house began the term. Rule 6(a) cannot carry the motion. Before 1996 § 2255 said a motion "may be made at any time," so there is no statutory filing period to compute, and custody is a status measured when filing occurs. The clerk-inaccessibility clause (Fed. R. Civ. P. 6(a), effective August 1, 1985; Fed. R. Crim. P. 45(a), 1982) relieves a party facing a filing deadline; it does not say a sentence or custody continues, and Rule 82 bars enlarging jurisdiction by rule. Lamb and Simon computed time periods for acts and are distinguishable. Houston and Fallen turn on a pro se prisoner's lack of control, so they do not reach counsel at a courthouse door. A tender rule would also be a theory not passed on below and not briefed (Singleton v. Wulff, discretionary), resting on no finding that a completed motion was tendered.

**Remedy:** Affirm. The affirmance rests on absence of custody, not the merits, and is without prejudice to any separate vehicle (Morgan) whose prerequisites are not before the panel. Alternative that matters: vacatur and remand limited to notice, supplemental briefing and findings on tender (completed motion in hand, official closure as the sole impediment, alternatives such as filing with a judge), if the closure contention is treated as preserving a filing-event theory. His writings do not choose between these; willingness to announce a new rule cannot be attributed to him without a writing.

**Sources:** Authored: Taggi v. United States, 35 F.3d 93 (2d Cir. Sept. 12, 1994) (tax refund; no custody, Rule 6 or filing issue, so no bearing on the vote). Panel member only: United States v. El-Gabrowny, 35 F.3d 63 (2d Cir. Sept. 8, 1994) (per curiam; pretrial detention). No verified writing on custody, supervised release, Rule 6(a) or constructive filing. Authorities verified: 28 U.S.C. § 2255 and 18 U.S.C. § 3624 (Cornell text and amendment history); United States v. Brilliant, 274 F.2d 618 (2d Cir. 1960); Simon v. Commissioner, 176 F.2d 230 (2d Cir. 1949); Carafas v. LaVallee, 391 U.S. 234 (1968); Maleng v. Cook, 490 U.S. 488 (1989); Jones v. Cunningham, 371 U.S. 236 (1963); Union National Bank v. Lamb, 337 U.S. 38 (1949); Houston v. Lack, 487 U.S. 266 (1988); Fallen v. United States, 378 U.S. 139 (1964); United States v. Morgan, 346 U.S. 502 (1954); Singleton v. Wulff, 428 U.S. 106 (1976); Advisory Committee notes to Fed. R. Civ. P. 6 (1985) and Fed. R. Crim. P. 45 (1982); Fed. R. Civ. P. 82. Pinpoints are not verified because the sources lacked star paging.

**Join limits:** Cannot join a rationale that Rule 6(a), equity or collateral consequences keep custody running (Brilliant, Maleng, Rule 82); one that extends Houston to represented movants or rests on assumed tender facts; or one that reaches coram nobis. No legal barrier to a remand confined to notice, briefing and findings on tender that does not adopt the rule in advance.

## Lawrence W. Pierce (Senior Circuit Judge)

**Disposition:** (1) Custody and term calculation: sustains the district court's computation of a twenty-four month supervised release term running from November 27, 1991; his own earlier opinion records the sentence as supervised release, so the "probation" label is a misdescription without effect. (2) Rule 6(a) and the closure: does not extend custody. (3) Constructive-tender theory: not adopted or reached on this record. (4) Judgment: affirms dismissal for lack of custody at the first legally effective filing, with no merits ruling. Confidence is highest on (1) and lower on (2) and (3), which his verified writings do not address.

**Ground:** Independent review of custody jurisdiction and of the sentence's construction; clear-error review of the undisputed dates. The statutory text controls: § 2255 speaks of a prisoner "in custody under sentence," and before 1996 allowed a motion "at any time," leaving Rule 6(a) no filing period to extend. Custody is tested when the motion is filed (Carafas; Maleng; Wheel v. Robinson), and Brilliant treats the requirement as jurisdictional. Section 3624(e) starts supervised release on the day of release from imprisonment, and § 3624(c) places halfway-house time within the term of imprisonment. Stabile (old-law parole) is not transferable to supervised release. The 1985 note to Rule 6(a) shows the clause protects parties from penalty for missing a filing date; Lamb and Simon applied the rule to statutory periods for acts, not to a custodial status, and Rule 82 forbids enlarging jurisdiction by rule. Houston is limited to pro se prisoners and does not reach counsel. A tender-based filing rule is unbriefed, unsupported by findings and without circuit authority (Singleton v. Wulff).

**Remedy:** Affirm, without a merits ruling and without prejudice to a separate vehicle under Morgan that is not before the panel. The same alternative matters: a remand limited to notice, briefing and limited findings on tender and on the commencement date, if a rationale rests on facts the record does not establish. His writings do not choose between these.

**Sources:** Authored: United States v. Scanio, 900 F.2d 485 (2d Cir. Mar. 16, 1990) (Pierce, J., with Oakes, C.J., and Rubin, J.), the direct appeal of this conviction. It records the sentence as twelve months of imprisonment and twenty-four months of supervised release, read 31 U.S.C. §§ 5324(3) and 5322(a) from statutory language and legislative history, and raised no custody or filing question. Panel member only: Ingber v. Enzor, 841 F.2d 450 (2d Cir. 1988) (Miner, J.) (§ 2255 relief on retroactivity; no custody analysis). No verified writing by him on custody, Rule 6(a) or constructive filing. Authorities: the same verified list as above, plus Stabile v. United States Bureau of Prisons, 834 F.2d 305 (2d Cir. 1987) (per curiam; parole status under 18 U.S.C. § 4164, not supervised release).

**Join limits:** Cannot join a rationale treating custody as extended by Rule 6(a) or equity, one that stretches Houston to counsel, one built on unfound tender facts or an unbriefed theory without notice to both parties, or one that converts the motion to coram nobis. A remand confined to notice, briefing and findings is not barred.
