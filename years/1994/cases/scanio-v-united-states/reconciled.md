## Historical comparison

History.md reports a panel disposition on 1994-10-11 (37 F.3d 858) affirming dismissal of the § 2255 motion for lack of custody at filing. The replaced judge (Altimari) wrote for the majority, joined by Van Graafeiland. Pierce dissented, questioning the custody calculation and the rejection of the holiday/closure computation principle. The dissent concerned access to the § 2255 forum, not the ineffective-assistance merits. The stated majority grounds were: the supervised-release term expired before the November 29, 1993 filing; Rule 6(a)'s computation provisions cannot extend custody or enlarge § 2255 jurisdiction; collateral consequences do not satisfy the initial custody requirement. Dismissal stayed in place and no hearing was ordered.

The historical outcome depended on the same record, argument date (May 26, 1994), preserved issues and two retained judges that this simulation has. The retained pair split one to one, and the replaced seat supplied both the majority's author and its second vote. The only changed premise is who occupies that seat, and this document does not model the new occupant. History.md summarizes the opinions briefly, so it does not show how the majority treated Houston, tender, coram nobis or the sentence arithmetic, or the dissent's detailed reasoning and remedy.

## Ellsworth A. Van Graafeiland

**Reconciled disposition:** Affirm the dismissal for want of custody at filing. By component: (1) the supervised-release term began with the November 27, 1991 release and ended before the motion was docketed; "probation" is an immaterial misdescription; (2) Rule 6(a) and the closure do not keep custody running; (3) no constructive-tender rule is adopted on this record; (4) affirmance with no ruling on the ineffective-assistance merits or on coram nobis.

**Ground:** Custody is tested when the motion is filed (Brilliant, Carafas, Maleng, with Jones for non-physical restraint). Section 3624(e) starts supervised release on release from imprisonment, and § 3624(c) places halfway-house time inside the term of imprisonment. Rule 6(a) computes periods for acts, § 2255 then had no filing period to compute, and Rule 82 bars enlarging jurisdiction by rule. Lamb and Simon concern time periods, not a custodial status. Collateral consequences of an expired sentence do not suffice.

**Remedy:** Affirm; no hearing. His qualifier that the affirmance is without prejudice to a separate Morgan vehicle remains his position, though history does not show whether the majority said so. The commitment's limited-remand alternative on tender is not carried forward as a live preference, because on the same record he joined an outright affirmance.

**Join limits:** Unchanged from the commitment. He cannot join a rationale that Rule 6(a), equity or collateral consequences keep custody running, one that extends Houston to represented movants or rests on assumed tender facts, or one that reaches coram nobis. A remand confined to notice, briefing and findings on tender, without adopting a rule in advance, is not barred but is not his first choice.

**Basis for any departure from the commitment or from history:** None. Every component of the commitment agrees with the historical majority position he joined, and no premise has changed for him. History corroborates the commitment and raises confidence on components (1), (2) and (4).

## Lawrence W. Pierce

**Reconciled disposition:** Does not join an affirmance. Vacate the dismissal and remand. By component: (1) does not sustain the district court's custody calculation as adequately established; (2) does not accept the rejection of the holiday/closure computation principle, and would give the closure effect in deciding whether the motion was filed while the term was running; (3) no constructive-tender rule is adopted or reached on this record (commitment retained); (4) no appellate ruling on the ineffective-assistance merits, and none on coram nobis.

**Ground:** The two grounds history attributes to the dissent: the calculation of the supervised-release term and the closure computation principle. Either, if accepted, defeats the stated basis for dismissal, so I treat each as sufficient to prevent affirmance. History does not say whether the dissent treated them as independent or cumulative, or whether either alone supported a direct holding of § 2255 jurisdiction rather than a remand. These commitment points do not conflict: custody is jurisdictional (Brilliant) and tested at filing, Stabile (old-law parole) does not transfer automatically to supervised release, and Houston is limited to pro se prisoners.

**Remedy:** Vacatur of the dismissal and remand to the district court, with no appellate ruling on the ineffective-assistance claim. The scope of the remand is uncertain: limited findings on the legal commencement and expiration of the term and the filing date, or consideration of the § 2255 motion on a filing treated as made within the term. History does not choose between these.

**Join limits:** Cannot join an affirmance that holds Rule 6(a) computation cannot reach the closure, an opinion that sustains the district court's calculation without findings, a ruling on the ineffective-assistance merits, or a conversion of the motion to coram nobis. Can join a remand limited to notice, briefing and findings, and a resolution by computation that announces no tender rule. Retained with lower confidence, because history is silent: reluctance to join a rule extending Houston to represented counsel, or a rationale built on unfound tender facts or an unbriefed theory without notice to both parties, and a refusal to let equity alone or collateral consequences supply custody. Dropped: the commitment's bar on any rationale that treats Rule 6(a) as extending custody, which cannot stand beside a dissent from the majority's rejection of the closure computation principle.

**Basis for any departure from the commitment or from history:** The commitment departs from history on disposition, ground, remedy and one join limit. No changed legal, factual, procedural or remedial premise explains the difference: the record, argument, issues and judge are the same. The commitment was an inference from text and structure, built on the absence of any verified Pierce writing on custody, Rule 6(a) or filing. Even its highest-confidence component, the calculation, rests on his 1990 opinion, which records only the sentence imposed and says nothing about commencement. History is direct evidence of his vote on this record and these issues, so it is stronger than the commitment's basis, and the reconciled position follows history. The change in the third seat does not alter any premise of his dissent.
