## Dependence on the historical judgment

- **Any certiorari petition.** Only Scanio could have sought review of an affirmance of the dismissal. It would have turned on the custody question: supervised release ended at midnight 1993-11-26, the section 2255 motion was filed 1993-11-29, and the majority held that Rule 6(a) computation could not extend custody and that collateral consequences do not supply initial custody. Judge Pierce's dissent on the computation is the feature a petition would have used.
- **Different judgment.** A reversal holding that custody existed at filing would send the ineffective-assistance claim back to the district court (W.D.N.Y., Chief Judge Telesca dismissed on 1993-12-06). The United States, not Scanio, would then be the likely petitioner on the custody question, and the district court's merits ruling would become a new later event. An affirmance on a different ground (for example the merits) would change the question any petition presented.
- **Peck citation.** It depends only on the 1994 holding that expired supervised release ends custody; a contrary holding would change how later cases cite the case, though not the course of this litigation.
