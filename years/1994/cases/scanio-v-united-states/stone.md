# Scanio v. United States

**Status:** Version 1; proposed, not user-approved. **Approval reference:** None. The source brief states that every Stone position, alternative, and negotiating limit is a proposal pending approval.

## Stone's proposed judicial position

Stone should propose a limited vacatur and remand directed to the jurisdictional facts and the asserted pre-expiration attempt to file. He should not declare that Rule 6(a) prolongs custody until the next business day. That would answer the government's strongest objection by denying the difference between a person's status and a deadline. His proposed route instead asks whether the application was constructively lodged while the jurisdictional status still existed.

The rule he should propose is narrow: when counsel actually presents at the court's designated filing location during an otherwise available filing day with the completed application for filing, and an official closure alone prevents its receipt, filing at the first reopening may establish a constructive lodging at that earlier tender. The applicant must prove those predicates. Preparation at home, an uncommunicated intention to file, an ordinary mailing, or a visit without the completed application would not suffice. This is an extension by analogy from established filing principles, not a holding already supplied by *Houston*, *Lamb*, or § 2255.

The distinction performs real legal work. The court does not say that Scanio remained supervised on Monday. It identifies Friday as the filing event if an actual tender of the completed application was frustrated by the court's own closure. That preserves the statutory requirement of simultaneous custody and filing. It also prevents the government's exclusive control over receipt from making the otherwise available statutory procedure disappear before custody ends. The rationale comes from the administration of filing and the existing collateral-review channel, not from a freestanding constitutional power to overlook jurisdiction.

The reported visit to the courthouse provides a concrete reason to examine that question, but an appellate opinion should not invent findings about the completed papers, precisely what counsel did, or available means of lodging them. The parties should first be heard on the proposed legal characterization and its preservation. The district court should then determine any genuinely disputed predicate facts and explain the legal commencement and expiration of supervised release. An unsupported suspicion about a halfway house's authority would not justify extending custody; the actual legal arrangement must be established.

If the court concludes that the proposed filing rule is unavailable under governing law or that its factual predicates are not proved, and the sentence had fully expired before the first effective filing, dismissal follows. Stone cannot replace that conclusion with equitable sympathy. If an effective filing occurred during custody, later release does not defeat the proceeding under *Carafas*, and the district court may address the motion's remaining procedural requirements and merits. Nothing here determines the effectiveness of trial counsel or promises collateral relief. Master Jurisprudence §§ 3, 5, 8-9, 18; Courts, Jurisdiction, and Justiciability; Collateral Review.

## Proposed negotiating limits

**Preferred and broadest defensible holding:** recognize a carefully bounded constructive-lodging rule for a proved, completed application actually brought for filing before custody ended and prevented solely by official closure; remand for the necessary facts and any required adversarial briefing.

**Acceptable narrower proposal:** remand for an adequate explanation of the custody calculation and findings concerning the attempted filing, expressly reserving whether constructive lodging is legally available. This fallback does not predetermine jurisdiction after remand.

**Non-join limits:** treating collateral consequences alone as custody; extending supervised release by judicial equity; applying a later statutory limitation period; assuming that a visit without an application constitutes filing; or adopting the new theory without a fair opportunity for the government to address it.
