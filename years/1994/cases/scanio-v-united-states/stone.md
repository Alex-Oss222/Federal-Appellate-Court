## Stone's Position

### Component 1

**Component:** Custody at commencement and legal expiration of supervised release

**Jurisdiction and reach:** The district court dismissed on custody grounds. The record’s “probation” label and physical release date do not conclusively determine the legal term. The statutory route and unsettled jurisdictional predicates in the neutral baseline remain controlling; this proposal does not assume them away.

**Proposed disposition:** Propose a limited remand to establish the relevant jurisdictional facts without assuming continuing custody.

**Governing law:** Independent review of custody jurisdiction and sentence construction; clear-error review of supported historical findings. Section 2255 requires custody under the challenged sentence when the proceeding begins. Supervised release can qualify; collateral consequences of a fully expired sentence cannot. A properly commenced case survives later release.

Jones v. Cunningham, 371 U.S. 236 (1963), restraint outside prison can be custody; Carafas v. LaVallee, 391 U.S. 234, 238 (1968), preserves jurisdiction after release in a properly filed case; Maleng v. Cook, 490 U.S. 488, 491-92 (1989), excludes mere collateral consequences after full expiration. 28 U.S.C. § 2255 as then in force; United States v. Brilliant, 274 F.2d 618, 620 (2d Cir. 1960); Stabile v. United States Bureau of Prisons, 834 F.2d 305 (2d Cir. 1987), concerning parole in its own setting; 18 U.S.C. § 3624(e) requires attention to the legal commencement of supervised release.

**Legal relationship:** Applying binding law.

**Proposed holding:** The statutory status must be established by the sentence and legal release arrangement, not by an inaccurate label or unexamined physical-release inference.

**Legal reasoning:** Stone should propose a limited vacatur and remand directed to the jurisdictional facts and the asserted pre-expiration attempt to file. He should not declare that Rule 6(a) prolongs custody until the next business day. That would answer the government's strongest objection by denying the difference between a person's status and a deadline. His proposed route instead asks whether the application was constructively lodged while the jurisdictional status still existed.

Brilliant and Maleng make custody a real jurisdictional limit. Carafas is not an exception to initial custody; it explains the consequence of a timely commencement. The court therefore must identify when imprisonment legally ended and supervision began before deciding that the action arrived too late.

**Independent or alternative considerations:** No additional rule is proposed beyond the interpretation and application stated here.

**Preferred scope:** The statutory status must be established by the sentence and legal release arrangement, not by an inaccurate label or unexamined physical-release inference. The lawful outer boundary is the issue and remedial limit stated here, not a broader rule about unpresented facts.

Case-level proposed scope: recognize a carefully bounded constructive-lodging rule for a proved, completed application actually brought for filing before custody ended and prevented solely by official closure; remand for the necessary facts and any required adversarial briefing.

**Fallback position:** Proposed: affirm lack of § 2255 custody if the proper calculation and effective-filing date establish expiration.

Case-level nonjoin limits, also proposed: treating collateral consequences alone as custody; extending supervised release by judicial equity; applying a later statutory limitation period; assuming that a visit without an application constitutes filing; or adopting the new theory without a fair opportunity for the government to address it.

### Component 3

**Component:** Proposed constructive lodging and the need for briefing and findings

**Jurisdiction and reach:** The record describes a courthouse visit on November 26, but does not establish all predicates of completed-paper tender. Constructive lodging is a proposed additional theory, not a verified party submission. The court must determine reach and allow both parties to address it before adoption. The statutory route and unsettled jurisdictional predicates in the neutral baseline remain controlling; this proposal does not assume them away.

**Proposed disposition:** Propose limited further proceedings on actual pre-expiration tender, subject to briefing, legal authority, and factual proof.

**Governing law:** Independent review of legal filing rules; no appellate creation of disputed tender facts. Any remand requires materiality and a properly reachable legal ground. Existing filing principles recognize that the legally operative filing event need not always equal later docket entry. Houston is confined to its pro se prisoner setting. Any extension to actual counsel tender blocked by official closure must be justified openly and must preserve simultaneous custody and filing.

Houston v. Lack, 487 U.S. 266 (1988), is analogous rather than controlling for represented counsel at a courthouse. Carafas governs survival of a case if a valid in-custody filing is established. United States v. Morgan, 346 U.S. 502 (1954), concerns a distinct coram nobis remedy and does not authorize automatic relabeling. § 2255; the rules governing receipt of court filings in this period. The actual closure order, completed application, tender conduct, and alternative filing availability are necessary source gaps.

**Legal relationship:** Proposing a new legal rule within the court’s authority, conditional on determining preservation and source support.

**Proposed holding:** Proposed new application: a completed petition actually tendered at the designated filing location while custody remains, rejected solely because of official closure and filed at the first reopening, may be treated as lodged at that tender. A visit, intention, preparation at home, or ordinary mailing alone is insufficient.

**Legal reasoning:** The rule he should propose is narrow: when counsel actually presents at the court's designated filing location during an otherwise available filing day with the completed application for filing, and an official closure alone prevents its receipt, filing at the first reopening may establish a constructive lodging at that earlier tender. The applicant must prove those predicates. Preparation at home, an uncommunicated intention to file, an ordinary mailing, or a visit without the completed application would not suffice. This is an extension by analogy from established filing principles, not a holding already supplied by *Houston*, *Lamb*, or § 2255.

The distinction performs real legal work. The court does not say that Scanio remained supervised on Monday. It identifies Friday as the filing event if an actual tender of the completed application was frustrated by the court's own closure. That preserves the statutory requirement of simultaneous custody and filing. It also prevents the government's exclusive control over receipt from making the otherwise available statutory procedure disappear before custody ends. The rationale comes from the administration of filing and the existing collateral-review channel, not from a freestanding constitutional power to overlook jurisdiction.

The reported visit to the courthouse provides a concrete reason to examine that question, but an appellate opinion should not invent findings about the completed papers, precisely what counsel did, or available means of lodging them. The parties should first be heard on the proposed legal characterization and its preservation. The district court should then determine any genuinely disputed predicate facts and explain the legal commencement and expiration of supervised release. An unsupported suspicion about a halfway house's authority would not justify extending custody; the actual legal arrangement must be established.

If the court concludes that the proposed filing rule is unavailable under governing law or that its factual predicates are not proved, and the sentence had fully expired before the first effective filing, dismissal follows. Stone cannot replace that conclusion with equitable sympathy. If an effective filing occurred during custody, later release does not defeat the proceeding under *Carafas*, and the district court may address the motion's remaining procedural requirements and merits. Nothing here determines the effectiveness of trial counsel or promises collateral relief.

A visit, intention, preparation at home, or ordinary mailing alone is insufficient. The lawful outer boundary is the issue and remedial limit stated here, not a broader rule about unpresented facts.
