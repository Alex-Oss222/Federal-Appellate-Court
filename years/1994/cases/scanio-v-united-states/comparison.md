# Comparison: Scanio v. United States, No. 94-2002

## Departure from history

Historically the panel of Van Graafeiland, Pierce and Altimari affirmed the dismissal of the section 2255 motion for want of custody, 2-1, with Altimari writing and Pierce dissenting; the simulated panel of Van Graafeiland, Pierce and Stone vacated the dismissal and remanded, 2-1, with Stone writing, Pierce in the majority on the vacatur, and Van Graafeiland dissenting from the judgment. The Rule 6(a) holding is the same in substance (Rule 6(a) does not continue an expired sentence), but the simulated opinion did not accept the district court's custody calculation and held that the record does not establish when the supervised-release term began and ended, so the judgment, the custody reasoning and the remedy all differ (remand for a supported calculation and, if the term had expired, notice and findings on counsel's attempted filing, instead of dismissal left in place).

## Historical comparison extract

On October 11, 1994, the Second Circuit (Van Graafeiland, Pierce, Altimari) affirmed dismissal of Scanio's section 2255 motion for lack of custody at filing, 2-1. Judge Altimari wrote, joined by Judge Van Graafeiland; Judge Pierce dissented, questioning the custody calculation and the rejection of the holiday closure computation principle. The majority treated the supervised-release term as expired before the November 29, 1993 filing and held that Rule 6(a) computation cannot extend custody or enlarge section 2255 jurisdiction. No hearing was ordered.

The simulated judgment changes the outcome: it vacates and remands rather than affirms. The Rule 6(a) rule is essentially unchanged, so the changed judgment rests on the custody calculation, held unsupported on this record, and on the remedy, not on a different view of Rule 6(a).

Source: Scanio v. United States, 37 F.3d 858, 858-65 (2d Cir. 1994).
