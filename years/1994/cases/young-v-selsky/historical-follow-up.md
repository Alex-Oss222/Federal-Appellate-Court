## Supreme Court merits decision, if any

None. The Court denied certiorari on 1995-05-01 without opinion, leaving the Second Circuit's reversal of the absolute-immunity dismissal in place. A denial is not approval of the reasoning. The LOC full-text index of the U.S. Reports for 1994 to 1997 matches "Selsky" only in the 514 U.S. table of cases and orders, so no rehearing petition or second petition appears in that index.

## Dependence on the historical judgment

- **Certiorari petition (No. 94-1393):** Sought by Selsky, the party that lost on appeal. It depends on a Second Circuit judgment that reversed the dismissal and denied absolute immunity to Selsky, on the functional analysis that his administrative appellate role lacked the independence and safeguards of a judicial role, and on the remand to address qualified immunity. If a differently decided judgment affirmed absolute immunity, Selsky would have won, Young would be the aggrieved party, and a Selsky petition would have no object. If the court reversed on narrower grounds (for example, qualified immunity only), the petition's question would need to be re-examined.
- **Respondent's IFP motion:** Depends only on Young's status as a respondent defending the reversal; it would not attach if Young had lost.
- **Remand proceedings:** Depend on the remand "for further proceedings consistent with this opinion", specifically the open qualified-immunity question the district court never reached. An affirmance would leave no remand.
