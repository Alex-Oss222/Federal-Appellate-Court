# Commitments: Young (Ramadan) v. Selsky, No. 93-2567 (2d Cir.)

**Stage:** neutral modeling. **Legal cutoff:** 1994-11-21. **Inputs:** neutral.md, ENGINE.md, and the public sources cited below.

Judges are modeled separately; the third panel member is not modeled.

## Wilfred Feinberg (Senior Circuit Judge)

**Disposition:** One separable component, the dismissal of the damages claims against Selsky on absolute-immunity grounds. Reverse that ruling. On this record Selsky has not shown that his review of disciplinary appeals is functionally comparable to adjudication, so only qualified immunity is available at this stage. No view on liability, damages, or any of the five sanctions.

**Ground:** The immunity classification is a legal question reviewed independently (neutral.md). Absolute immunity is functional and the proponent bears the burden of justifying it: Butz v. Economou, 438 U.S. 478 (1978) (official "must bear the burden of showing that public policy requires an exemption of that scope"); Burns v. Reed, 500 U.S. 478 (1991) (burden on the official; qualified immunity presumptively sufficient); Antoine v. Byers & Anderson, Inc., 508 U.S. 429 (1993) ("The proponent of a claim to absolute immunity bears the burden of establishing the justification for such immunity"). The Butz factors, as restated in Cleavinger v. Saxner, 474 U.S. 193 (1985), are protection from harassment, safeguards reducing the need for damages suits, insulation from pressure, precedent, adversary character, and correctability on appeal. Cleavinger denied absolute immunity to a prison committee whose members were not "independent," were subordinates of the warden who reviewed them, and had few procedural safeguards, and it did not treat multi-level administrative review as enough. Taking Selsky's own materials at face value, centralization and caseload bear mainly on harassment, which Cleavinger found overstated and which qualified immunity already addresses. The state has not shown insulation from departmental pressure, adversary presentation, or precedent at the review stage, and the record includes evidence that he advised hearing officers and sat within the correctional hierarchy. The result rests on the failure of the burden, not on a finding that the advisory-role evidence is true. Distinguished: Dorman v. Higgins (probation officer appointed and removable by the court, an arm of the court, with adversary and judicial-review safeguards); Shelly v. Johnson (Michigan hearing officers were statutorily independent of the warden, with prescribed procedures, written findings, and judicial review); Richardson v. Selsky (decided on qualified immunity, no categorical rule). Walker v. Bates (sanitized extract): binding only on the cure question, that administrative reversal after punitive confinement began is no defense to a section 1983 claim for punishment already served. It does not address absolute immunity. It bears indirectly: administrative correction does not compensate for served punishment, so the correctability factor carries less weight as a substitute for damages review. Selsky affirmed here, so no cure question is presented.

**Remedy:** Reverse and remand for further proceedings. Qualified immunity, personal involvement, causation, the Wolff witness claims, any protected-interest question, and relief are for the district court. No damages directed. Absolute damages immunity would not in any event bar non-damages relief (Heimbach, below), so any contrary path must be confined to damages.

**Sources:** Own writings: Hodge v. Police Officers, 802 F.2d 58 (2d Cir. 1986) (Feinberg, C.J.; appointment of counsel for indigent civil rights plaintiff; no immunity content); Rhem v. Malcolm, 507 F.2d 333 (2d Cir. 1974) (Feinberg, J.; prison conditions; remand for tailored remedy; dated, little weight). Joined panel opinions: Heimbach v. Village of Lyons, 597 F.2d 344 (2d Cir. 1979) (per curiam; absolute immunity for the justice's judicial act, qualified immunity for other officials, injunctive relief not barred); Barbera v. Smith, 836 F.2d 96 (2d Cir. 1987) (Pierce, J.; absolute immunity denied for investigative acts, qualified immunity applied); Gill v. Mooney, 824 F.2d 192 (2d Cir. 1987) (Van Graafeiland, J.; pro se prisoner pleading, personal involvement). Governing: Bradley v. Fisher, 80 U.S. (13 Wall.) 335 (1872); Pierson v. Ray, 386 U.S. 547 (1967); Wolff v. McDonnell, 418 U.S. 539, 566 (1974); Dorman v. Higgins, 821 F.2d 133 (2d Cir. 1987) (Kearse, J.); Richardson v. Selsky, 5 F.3d 616 (2d Cir. 1993) (Pierce, J.); Shelly v. Johnson, 849 F.2d 228 (6th Cir. 1988) (per curiam); Young v. Hoffman, 970 F.2d 1154 (2d Cir. 1992) (per curiam); 42 U.S.C. § 1983 and 28 U.S.C. §§ 46, 1291 as in force in 1994 (the 1996 injunctive-relief amendment to § 1983 is not in force). No Feinberg-authored opinion applying Butz or Cleavinger to a prison or administrative adjudicator was located.

**Join limits:** Can join a narrow burden-and-factors rationale. Barriers to other rationales: (a) one resting on appellate fact-finding from testimony in other lawsuits, which the parties' consent to consideration does not authorize; (b) a categorical rule that no reviewing official in any prison appeals system can have absolute immunity, which exceeds the need and conflicts with the structure-specific inquiry in Dorman and Shelly; (c) first-instance appellate decision of qualified immunity or the merits on this incomplete record; (d) reading Walker v. Bates beyond its cure scope.

## Ellsworth A. Van Graafeiland (Senior Circuit Judge)

**Disposition:** Same single component. Reverse the absolute-immunity dismissal of the damages claims against Selsky, because the burden of justifying absolute immunity for this function was not carried on this record. No view on liability or damages.

**Ground:** Independent review of the legal classification; function over title; burden on the official (Butz; Burns; Antoine; Cleavinger as above). His joined opinions apply immunity by function and confine absolute immunity to the protected function: Ying Jing Gan v. City of New York, 996 F.2d 522 (2d Cir. 1993) (Kearse, J.; immunity "depends principally on the nature of the function performed, not on the office itself"; absolute for the charging decision, qualified for investigative and other acts) and Heimbach. Applied here, a reviewer who is a commissioner's designee inside the department, advised hearing officers, and works without shown adversary process or precedent does not match the court-like conditions Butz and Cleavinger require. The state's best factor for this judge is correctability through Article 78 and administrative review, which fits his joined Marino v. Ameruso, 837 F.2d 45 (2d Cir. 1988) (Winter, J.; Article 78 as an adequate corrective for ALJ evidentiary error). Marino decided a due-process question, not immunity, and Cleavinger refused to treat warden and multi-level review as sufficient, so correctability is one factor that cannot carry the burden alone. Dorman, Shelly, and Richardson are distinguished as above. Walker v. Bates: same limited bearing as for Judge Feinberg (cure question only; nothing on absolute immunity).

**Remedy:** Reverse and remand. Qualified immunity and personal involvement are reserved to the district court, consistent with his authored Gill v. Mooney (no supervisory liability without personal involvement). Circuit law on supervisors' notice and failure to remedy is Wright v. Smith, 21 F.3d 496 (2d Cir. 1994) (Newman, C.J.), which is not decided now. No damages directed.

**Sources:** Own writing: Gill v. Mooney, 824 F.2d 192 (2d Cir. 1987) (Van Graafeiland, J.; Feinberg, C.J., and Pierce, J., on panel). Joined: Heimbach v. Village of Lyons, 597 F.2d 344 (2d Cir. 1979); Ying Jing Gan v. City of New York, 996 F.2d 522 (2d Cir. 1993); Russell v. Coughlin, 910 F.2d 75 (2d Cir. 1990) (Pierce, J.; New York keeplock rules create a liberty interest under Hewitt v. Helms, 459 U.S. 460 (1983); qualified immunity denied where law was clear); Marino v. Ameruso, 837 F.2d 45 (2d Cir. 1988). Governing: the Supreme Court and circuit authorities and statutes listed under Judge Feinberg, plus Kentucky Dep't of Corrections v. Thompson, 490 U.S. 454 (1989). No Van Graafeiland-authored opinion applying Butz or Cleavinger to a prison or administrative adjudicator was located.

**Join limits:** Can join a narrow burden-and-factors rationale. Barriers: (a) appellate fact-finding from other lawsuits' testimony; (b) a rationale that dismisses Article 78 and administrative correction as irrelevant rather than weighing them and finding them insufficient alone; (c) a categorical rule for all reviewing officials; (d) deciding personal involvement, causation, qualified immunity, or the merits on this record; (e) extending Walker v. Bates past the cure question.
