## Supreme Court merits decision, if any

None in this case; certiorari was denied on 1995-01-23. Related, different case: Zicherman, 516 U.S. 217 (decided 1996-01-16), on whether Warsaw Convention Article 17 permits loss-of-society damages for a death on the high seas. It held that Article 17 allows compensation only for legally cognizable harm and leaves that to the domestic law selected by the forum's choice-of-law rules; the Death on the High Seas Act supplied the law there and bars nonpecuniary damages. The Second Circuit later read it as rejecting Lockerbie II's uniform federal damages rule (Pescatore). Its effect on the Pagnucco remand is not established. Source: [Cornell](https://www.law.cornell.edu/supct/html/94-1361.ZO.html).

## Dependence on the historical judgment

- **Certiorari petition.** Defendants sought review of the affirmed liability verdicts (willful misconduct, causation, trial conduct) and the damages holdings. It depended on the affirmance of liability. A reversal on liability would have left the plaintiffs as petitioners.
- **Remand.** It depended on vacatur of the Pagnucco society award (adult children, dependency not shown) and of all three parental-care awards. The Bainbridge and Porter society awards stood, so no remand attached to them.
- **Zicherman.** It reaches the damages-law premise (Warsaw Convention plus federal common law), not liability.
