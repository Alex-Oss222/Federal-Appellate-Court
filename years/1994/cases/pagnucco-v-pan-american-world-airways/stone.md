## Stone's Position

### Component 1

**Component:** Regulatory communications, culpable understanding, and alternative defenses

**Jurisdiction and reach:** The specific Cozzi proffer and restriction on alternative regulatory defenses supply the preserved grounds. The uncertain Sonesen proffer cannot be treated as equally preserved or supported. The basic willful-misconduct instruction was not challenged. The statutory route and unsettled jurisdictional predicates in the neutral baseline remain controlling; this proposal does not assume them away.

**Proposed disposition:** Vacate willful-misconduct liability and order a new trial on the preserved evidentiary ground.

**Governing law:** Abuse of discretion for evidentiary rulings, independent review of the legal relevance theory, and civil substantial-rights review under Rule 61 and § 2111. The estates must prove the treaty’s willful-misconduct standard and causal relation to the accident. An ineffective oral waiver cannot legalize noncompliance; the distinct effect of a reported regulatory communication on culpable understanding may nevertheless be relevant. Ordinary foundation, hearsay, and prejudice controls still apply.

Cheek v. United States, 498 U.S. 192 (1991), and Ratzlaf v. United States, 510 U.S. 135 (1994), concern particular criminal willfulness statutes, not a universal knowledge-of-illegality element for Warsaw liability. Mathews v. United States, 485 U.S. 58, 64 (1988), supplies persuasive support for alternative defenses in its criminal setting. Warsaw Convention arts. 17 and 25; Montreal Agreement; Fed. R. Evid. 105, 403; former Fed. R. Civ. P. 8(e)(2), 61. Grey v. American Airlines, 227 F.2d 282, 285 (2d Cir. 1955); Berner, 346 F.2d 532, 537 (2d Cir. 1965); Ospina, 975 F.2d 35, 37 (2d Cir. 1992); Vinieris, 731 F.2d 1061, 1064 (2d Cir. 1984). Duggan and Durrani address legal authorization, not every state-of-mind use.

**Legal relationship:** Applying binding law; developing an independent evidentiary rationale within existing rules.

**Proposed holding:** A regulatory communication’s inability to create legal authorization does not make its effect on the decisionmaker’s culpable understanding categorically irrelevant.

**Legal reasoning:** Stone should propose a new trial on willful-misconduct liability because the preserved exclusion of Cozzi's mental-state evidence rested on an improper equivalence between legal authorization and the defendant's actual understanding. His opinion should expressly reject a defense of binding oral waiver. Pan Am remained subject to the written security requirements. The question is whether the people directing its operations possessed the culpable state of mind required to remove the Convention's liability limit.

A jury could disbelieve Cozzi, conclude that reliance was contrived, or find that even an honestly understood communication did not overcome knowledge of probable injury. Those are reasons to test the evidence, not to declare it legally irrelevant. Evidence that a responsible official instructed personnel to consult the regulator, received a report of approval, and proceeded on that understanding has some tendency to distinguish a consciously reckless operational choice from a mistaken judgment. Rule 105 permits the court to tell the jury that no legally effective waiver occurred and that the evidence bears only on the appropriate mental-state question. Rule 403 remains available for a particularized objection; it cannot sustain an exclusion whose stated foundation denied the distinction altogether.

The error's consequence must be considered against plaintiffs' substantial warning evidence. Stone should not minimize prior bomb incidents, the shortcomings of x-ray detection, or the alleged failure to respond adequately to specific warnings. Yet the issue is not whether that evidence would permit another jury to find willful misconduct. It is whether exclusion of the defense's account of its own decisionmaking could materially have affected this jury's resolution of the central disputed mental element. A trial built around management's knowledge and disregard is not fairly assessed by counting the remaining adverse incidents. The Cozzi exclusion, reinforced by the improper restriction on alternative regulatory defenses, warrants retrial under the civil substantial-rights standard.

**Independent or alternative considerations:** No additional rule is proposed beyond the interpretation and application stated here.

**Preferred scope:** A regulatory communication’s inability to create legal authorization does not make its effect on the decisionmaker’s culpable understanding categorically irrelevant. The lawful outer boundary is the issue and remedial limit stated here, not a broader rule about unpresented facts.

Case-level proposed scope: a preserved and prejudicial mental-state exclusion requires a new liability trial; actual legal authority and evidence relevant to culpable understanding must be separately analyzed. The opinion should also state the correct society and parental-care rules governing the remand.

**Fallback position:** Proposed, not approved: a new trial based specifically on the Cozzi proffer and the restriction on alternative defenses, without deciding the disputed adequacy of other offers or announcing a general rule admitting regulatory communications. A remand confined to particular damages categories is acceptable only if Stone independently concludes that the liability error was harmless; that conclusion is not approved merely as a bargaining alternative.

Case-level nonjoin limits, also proposed: an effective oral waiver contrary to the governing regulatory procedure; automatic willful misconduct from any violation; exclusion of all state-of-mind evidence because it cannot establish authorization; appellate adoption of an unproved bomb route; unrestricted adult-child society awards; or an assumption that the Convention's loss of a limitation authorizes punitive damages.

**Remedy and remand:** Permit properly founded state-of-mind evidence with a limiting instruction denying any binding oral-waiver effect, and allow lawful alternative defenses. Evaluate each other proffer separately.

**Reserved questions:** No holding that an oral waiver existed, that reliance was genuine, or that knowledge of illegality is invariably an element of treaty willful misconduct.

**Separate writing:** Proposed only. A separate opinion would defend or delimit this proposition: A regulatory communication’s inability to create legal authorization does not make its effect on the decisionmaker’s culpable understanding categorically irrelevant. It would explain why the authorities and record support the disposition stated above and answer the contrary position addressed in the legal reasoning. ### Component 3

**Component:** Loss-of-society damages

**Jurisdiction and reach:** The estates’ damages trials and the carrier’s challenges to the permissible beneficiaries and categories are before the court. Dependency is a factual predicate, not presumed for every adult child. The statutory route and unsettled jurisdictional predicates in the neutral baseline remain controlling; this proposal does not assume them away.

**Proposed disposition:** Recognize the period’s legally available spouse and dependent-child society claims, but require proof rather than presumed dependency for adult children.

**Governing law:** Independent review of the legally recoverable category; factual dependency and amount remain for the appropriate factfinder. The then-binding circuit federal-common-law framework permits qualifying society damages while excluding punitive recovery. Maritime analogies must be applied in their own statutory settings rather than importing a high-seas limitation mechanically into this over-land crash.

Sea-Land Services, Inc. v. Gaudet, 414 U.S. 573 (1974), and American Export Lines, Inc. v. Alvez, 446 U.S. 274 (1980), support society damages in their settings. Mobil Oil Corp. v. Higginbotham, 436 U.S. 618 (1978), and Miles v. Apex Marine Corp., 498 U.S. 19 (1990), require attention to enacted limits. In re Air Disaster at Lockerbie, 928 F.2d 1267, 1278-81 (2d Cir. 1991), binding circuit framework; Warsaw Convention; no subsequent Supreme Court treatment is applied.

**Legal relationship:** Applying binding law.

**Proposed holding:** The circuit framework allows the identified compensatory category; it does not authorize unrestricted adult-child recovery or punitive damages.

**Legal reasoning:** The prior Lockerbie decision is binding circuit law at this stage. Gaudet and Alvez support the category within that framework, while Miles and Higginbotham warn against overriding statutes that actually govern. Because this is an over-land accident, the necessary statutory connection must be established rather than presumed. Dependency remains a material limit that the appellate court cannot supply.

### Component 4

**Proposed disposition:** Require corrected parental-care instructions and a separate basis for claimed post-majority pecuniary contributions.

**Governing law:** Independent category and instruction review; civil substantial-rights analysis; factual valuation to the factfinder. Ordinary parental-care expectations are limited to minority. A specifically proved post-majority pecuniary contribution may be compensable, but companionship cannot be relabeled guidance to duplicate society recovery.

The compensatory and statutory-discipline principles of Miles and Higginbotham apply by their proper scope; no directly controlling Supreme Court holding fixes these particular parental-care awards. First National Bank in Greenwich v. National Airlines, 288 F.2d 621, 624 (2d Cir. 1961), binding circuit authority; Rule 61; the existing Lockerbie compensation framework.

**Legal relationship:** Applying binding law.

**Proposed holding:** Ordinary parental-care damages cannot be presumed indefinitely; particular later economic contributions require proof and no double counting.

**Legal reasoning:** Stone should resolve the recurring damages issues necessary to guide further proceedings. Under the governing circuit framework, the spouse and dependent-child society claims are legally available; Pagnucco's adult children's dependency cannot be assumed. Parental-care instructions require the minority limitation and a separate evidentiary basis for identifiable post-majority pecuniary contributions. Any new damages proceedings must avoid double counting. The district court should determine which unchallenged and genuinely separable components can remain fixed, subject to the new liability determination, rather than automatically reopening every dollar or treating all existing awards as collectible despite vacated liability.
