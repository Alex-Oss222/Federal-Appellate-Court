## Departure from history

The simulated decision vacated the willful-misconduct and causal-connection determination and ordered a new trial, by a 2-1 vote (Stone-Khoury, joined by Van Graafeiland, with Cardamone dissenting), where the historical decision affirmed liability 2-1 (Cardamone, joined by Altimari, with Van Graafeiland dissenting); authorship moved from Cardamone to Stone-Khoury. The damages judgments and reasoning are materially the same (Pagnucco society award and all three parental-care components vacated, Porter and Bainbridge society components not disturbed on the category challenge), but the remedy is broader: a new trial with separable awards to conform to its result, instead of a limited damages-only remand with liability left in place.

## Historical comparison extract

On 1994-09-12 the Second Circuit (Van Graafeiland, Cardamone, Altimari) issued an amended opinion on rehearing, written by Cardamone and joined by Altimari; Van Graafeiland dissented on liability. It affirmed the willful-misconduct liability verdicts and causation, affirmed the Bainbridge and Porter society awards, vacated the Pagnucco society award (adult children's dependency unproved) and the parental-care awards in all three cases (no minority limitation), and ordered a limited damages remand.

The simulated judgment issued the same day. It changes the liability judgment: the willful-misconduct and causal-connection determination is vacated for a new trial, while Pan Am's request for judgment on causation is still denied. On damages the judgments match, with materially similar reasoning on dependency and the minority limit. Only the remedy's scope differs.

Source: Pagnucco v. Pan American World Airways, Inc. (In re Air Disaster at Lockerbie), 37 F.3d 804, 814-32, esp. 828-30 (2d Cir. 1994).
