## Ellsworth A. Van Graafeiland (Senior Circuit Judge, senior status 1985-05-11)

**Disposition:** A: error as to the preserved Cozzi proffer and the condition placed on the British regulatory evidence; the Sonesen proffer is not treated as equally preserved. If the Cozzi proffer is as the packet describes, the exclusion prejudiced the contested mental-state element and the willful-misconduct determination is vacated. B: causation sufficiency affirmed (evidence viewed favorably to the verdict); x-ray demonstration exclusion upheld as cumulative of the stipulation; unsupported alternative-bombing scenarios stay out absent a Rule 702 foundation; expert testimony stating legal conclusions is error if preserved; unpreserved objections get plain-error review only and are not pooled. None of B is an independent ground for reversal, but B rulings guide the retrial. C: society damages affirmed only for categories the governing circuit framework recognizes (spouse, dependent children); no presumption of adult-child dependency; any society component resting on assumed dependency is vacated for redetermination. D: an instruction permitting presumed lifetime parental benefits is error; vacate the parental-care component only.

**Ground:** Willful misconduct under Warsaw art. 25 requires conscious intent, or realization of the probability of injury with disregard of the consequences (Grey; Berner; Perera). Because that is a subjective-state test, a ruling that an oral FAA communication could not be a valid waiver answers legal permission but not what management actually understood and why it acted; Duggan and Durrani (actual authority) do not reach the state-of-mind use. Review: abuse of discretion, with a legally mistaken premise reviewed independently; Rule 61 and 28 U.S.C. § 2111 prejudice. Conditioning one defense on abandoning another is unsupported under former Rule 8(e)(2) (Mathews, 485 U.S. at 64, as cited by the packet, unverified pinpoint). The Cheek and Ratzlaf line does not add a knowledge-of-illegality element; the proper course is admission with a limiting instruction (Rule 105) that belief in approval does not excuse noncompliance.

**Remedy:** New trial on willful misconduct and its causal connection, confined to that issue if separable. The packet does not say whether liability within the Montreal limit was contested, so the exact scope is flagged. Over-limit damages awards fall with the vacated finding; C and D rulings guide the remand, and unchallenged financial and service components are not reopened. Plausible alternative that matters: affirm on harmlessness if Cozzi's account duplicated admitted evidence of Pan Am's understanding; the transcripts are missing, so this turns on the proffer's actual content.

## Richard J. Cardamone (Senior Circuit Judge, senior status 1993-11-13)

**Disposition:** A: the exclusion rested on a mistaken legal premise as to the preserved Cozzi proffer, and the condition on the British regulatory evidence was error; the Sonesen proffer and the rejected curative-instruction point do not support reversal. Prejudice found on the mental-state element if the proffer is as described; vacate the willful-misconduct determination. B: no abuse of discretion in the x-ray, cumulative, and foundation rulings; sufficiency affirmed; unpreserved objections are not reviewed as preserved; legal-conclusion expert testimony cannot stand if preserved. C: society damages recoverable as compensatory loss only for spouse and dependent children on proof; vacate any component built on presumed dependency. D: parental-care instruction that allowed assumed post-majority benefits is error; redetermine that component only.

**Ground:** Rein adopted substantive federal common law as the law of the Convention cause of action, read art. 17 as compensatory only, and treated lifting the limit under art. 25 as the Convention's sole response to willful misconduct. Willful misconduct is therefore a high-consequence finding that must rest on a properly tried mental-state record. Evidentiary rulings are ordinarily reviewed deferentially (Malarkey: reversed only if "manifestly erroneous"), but a ruling resting on a legal error is not within that deference. Prejudice follows his Colombo test: whether the error could have "substantially influence[d] the jury's verdict." Damages instruction defects that misstate the recoverable measure are reversible where they taint the award (Metz). Adult-child parental guidance requires specific proof of pecuniary value (First National Bank in Greenwich, a DOHSA case that reserved the minor-child rule; the packet's description as limiting care "to minority" is looser than the opinion).

**Remedy:** New trial on willful misconduct and its causal connection, confined to that issue if separable (scope flagged below); redetermination of the parental-care component and any unsupported society component; unchallenged components stand; punitive recovery excluded. Metz (liability conceded; retrial scope not expressly limited) is the closest analogue for a damages-only retrial.

**Sources:** In re Air Disaster at Lockerbie (Rein v. Pan American World Airways, Inc.), 928 F.2d 1267 (2d Cir. 1991) (Cardamone, J.; panel Cardamone, Miner, Pollack, D.J.): "we adopt substantive federal common law as the law governing the cause of action under the Warsaw Convention" (Part IV.A); "Article 17 contemplates monetary or compensatory damages only" (Part V.A.2); art. 24(2) does not preserve punitive damages; the passages retrieved do not discuss loss of society by name. Malarkey v. Texaco, Inc., 983 F.2d 1204 (2d Cir. 1993) (Cardamone, J.). Metz v. United Technologies Corp., 754 F.2d 63 (2d Cir. 1985) (Cardamone, J.; Van Graafeiland, J., on the panel): failure to give a required damages instruction was plain error and required a new trial, while speculative lost-profit and uncorroborated income evidence was properly excluded. United States v. Colombo, 909 F.2d 711 (2d Cir. 1990) (Cardamone, J.; Winter, J., dissenting): preserved Rule 403 error not harmless. Authorities as listed for the other judge, plus Mathews v. United States, 485 U.S. 58 (1988) (inconsistent civil defenses under Rule 8(e)(2) noted; confirmed). No Cardamone writing on parental-care damages or Rule 8(e)(2) was found.

**Join limits:** Would not join a rationale that measures damages by anything other than compensatory loss, or that presumes adult-child dependency. Would not join reliance on the Sonesen proffer or on objections not preserved in the form now asserted. Would not join a rationale treating belief in FAA approval as a categorical defense to art. 25. Routine agreement otherwise.

## Open points and source gaps

- Rein's text, as retrieved, does not address loss of society; the packet's attribution of a society-damages framework to 928 F.2d at 1278-81 is unverified, as is that pinpoint. Rein also quotes the view that the Convention leaves the measure of damages to internal law; the court's own reading treats that reference as concerning compensatory calculation only. Which body of law supplies beneficiary and dependency rules remains open.
- Duggan's holding was not reached in the retrieved text; only Durrani's description of Duggan (actual authority required) is verified.
- First National Bank in Greenwich arose under DOHSA; its use for Warsaw damages is by analogy.
- FRAP 40 as in force on 1994-09-12 (the rehearing-stage standard) was not retrieved; these commitments model merits positions, not rehearing-stage discretion. Law-of-the-case or mandate effects of earlier versions of this appeal were not assessed because those versions are off-limits.
- Exact Cozzi proffer, objection transcripts, and the instructions on parental care are not in the packet; the prejudice and redetermination positions are conditional on them.
- The scope of any partial retrial (separability from damages and from art. 17 liability within the Montreal limit) is unresolved on this record.
