# United States v. Anderson

**Status:** Version 1; proposed, not user-approved. **Approval reference:** None. The source brief states that every Stone position, alternative, and negotiating limit is a proposal pending approval.

## Stone's proposed judicial position

Stone should propose vacatur of the seventeen-month sentence and remand for resentencing without using additional incarceration to obtain rehabilitation. He should accept the advisory character of Chapter Seven. These conclusions are complementary: the court has genuine discretion outside the policy-statement range, but not discretion to use an objective Congress has excluded from the decision to imprison.

The opinion should begin with the actual sentencing choice. Anderson was not merely directed toward treatment while otherwise serving an independently selected term. The judge used the need for a structured program as a reason for choosing seventeen months. The inquiry therefore cannot be resolved by observing that treatment is desirable or that § 3553(a)(2)(D) is relevant somewhere in the proceeding. It is relevant to the available measures Congress authorized for that purpose.

Section 3583(e)'s treatment cross-reference has substantial work to do even if prison duration cannot be increased for rehabilitation. Conditions can require treatment; the period and structure of supervision can reflect treatment needs; a court selecting lawful custody for other reasons can recommend available care. The construction Stone proposes preserves all of those functions. The government's alternative, in contrast, makes a change in the label of the sentencing proceeding sufficient to restore imprisonment's rehabilitative rationale, despite § 3582(a)'s direct instruction about imprisonment itself.

Stone should not announce a constitutional right to treatment outside prison or hold that rehabilitation is irrelevant to revocation as a whole. Nor should he direct a twelve-month sentence. Poor compliance, protection of the public, deterrence, and other incorporated lawful considerations may support a term above the Chapter Seven recommendation. The proper mandate requires the district court to exercise that discretion again, explain the permissible reasons, and refrain from selecting extra months because a particular program requires them.

This is an interpretation of the statutes available in 1994, informed by *Maier* and the distinction between imprisonment and other sentencing measures. It does not depend on the later Supreme Court treatment of rehabilitation. The Master Jurisprudence's Sentencing domain supports full use of lawful discretion, but § 3582(a), not Stone's preference, supplies the proposed limit.

## Proposed negotiating limits

**Preferred and broadest defensible holding:** the statutory prohibition on using prison to promote rehabilitation applies to the selection and duration of custody following revocation; vacate and remand while recognizing the advisory Chapter Seven range.

**Acceptable narrower proposal:** decide only that this sentence cannot stand where the court expressly selected its additional duration to secure institutional treatment, reserving incidental treatment recommendations and mixed records not presented here.

**Nonjoin limit:** affirming on hypothetical permissible reasons without confronting the stated rationale, making the Chapter Seven range mandatory, or forbidding all consideration of treatment in the administration of supervised release.
