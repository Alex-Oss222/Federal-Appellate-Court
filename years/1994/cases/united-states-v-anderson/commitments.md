## James L. Oakes (Senior Circuit Judge)

**Disposition:** Issue 1: sentence vacated. Choosing the 17-month duration partly to obtain structured treatment and an institutional program rested on an impermissible objective. The violation findings and the power to imprison for some period are undisturbed. Issue 2: the claim that 6 to 12 months is a mandatory ceiling is rejected. Seventeen months is under the 24-month ceiling and is not unlawful merely because it exceeds the recommendation. Net: revocation stands; term vacated and remanded for resentencing.

**Ground:** Statutory meaning and the force of policy statements are reviewed de novo; supported findings stand (18 U.S.C. § 3742(e)). Section 3582(a) directs the court, in "determining the length of the term" of imprisonment, to recognize that imprisonment is not an appropriate means of promoting correction and rehabilitation. Revocation imprisonment under § 3583(e)(3) is a term of imprisonment, so § 3582(a) applies of its own force. The government's point that § 3583(e) does not cross-reference it fails because § 3582(a) is a general command, not a § 3553(a) factor omitted from a list, and it asks for the § 3553(a) factors only "to the extent that they are applicable." Section 3553(a)(2)(D), which § 3583(e) incorporates, keeps treatment relevant to nonprison choices such as modifying or enlarging conditions under § 3583(e)(2). That reading fits 28 U.S.C. § 994(k), *Mistretta*, and *Maier*. Poor cooperation with supervision can justify custody but cannot be assumed to explain its length. Issue 2: *Bermudez* binds the panel; Chapter Seven statements are advisory but must be considered (§ 3553(a)(4) and (5), incorporated by § 3583(e)). *Williams* and *Stinson* concern policy statements tied to an applicable guideline and authoritative commentary and do not make Chapter Seven mandatory. Oakes's own *Johnson* opinion placed policy statements above legislative history but below guidelines, not to be given undue weight.

Alternative path that matters: affirm on the government's reading. *Darmand* shows Oakes drawing inferences from differences in drafting, which could support a negative inference from § 3583(e)'s silence about § 3582(a). I rate it weaker, because § 3582(a) speaks directly to "the length of the term" and § 994(k) states the policy.

**Remedy:** Vacate the sentence only; remand to the District of Connecticut for resentencing. No direction to impose 12 months or release, and no appellate fact-finding. The error cannot be called harmless on this record: the court expressly emphasized treatment, and nothing in the packet shows it would impose 17 months on permissible grounds alone (*Williams*; compare *Johnson*). That judgment belongs to the district court. Resentencing considers the policy statements in effect on the resentencing date (*Bermudez*). *Koehler* and *Bermudez* bar a new supervised-release term after revocation imprisonment, so no remand instruction may rely on one to supply treatment.

**Sources:** *United States v. Smith*, 982 F.2d 757 (2d Cir. 1992) (Oakes, J.) Grasso*, 6 F.3d 87 (2d Cir. 1993) (per curiam; Oakes on panel). Authorities: 18 U.S.C. §§ 3553(a), 3582(a), 3583(e), 3742; 28 U.S.C. § 994(a), (k); *United States v. Bermudez*, 974 F.2d 12 (2d Cir. 1992) (per curiam); *United States v. Koehler*, 973 F.2d 132 (2d Cir. 1992); *United States v. Maier*, 975 F.2d 944 (2d Cir. 1992); *Mistretta v. United States*, 488 U.S. 361 (1989); *Williams v. United States*, 503 U.S. 193 (1992); *Stinson v. United States*, 508 U.S. 36 (1993). Persuasive only: *United States v. Lee*, 957 F.2d 770 (10th Cir. 1992) (Chapter Seven advisory but must be considered); *United States v. Harris*, 990 F.2d 594 (11th Cir. 1993) (§ 994(k) bars lengthening initial imprisonment for drug treatment).

**Join limits:** He would not join a rationale making the 6 to 12 month range binding or requiring departure-style findings to exceed it (*Bermudez*; *Johnson*). *Grasso*, on which he sat, applied departure-review vocabulary to the Revocation Table without citing *Bermudez*; it governs reviewability of refusals to depart, so a join must not call it superseded or treat the range as legally meaningless. He would not join a holding that treatment is irrelevant to every § 3583(e) choice (*Smith*; § 3553(a)(2)(D)). He could not join an affirmance resting only on the cooperation ground without a finding that the term was not imposed as a result of the treatment rationale. Routine agreement with a text-based § 3582(a) rationale is expected.

## Amalya L. Kearse (Active Circuit Judge)

**Disposition:** Same components as above. Issue 1: sentence vacated for reliance on rehabilitation to fix prison length. Issue 2: mandatory-range claim rejected under *Bermudez*. Net: revocation stands; term vacated and remanded for resentencing.

**Ground:** Her demonstrated method is plain text first, with lenity as a reinforcing ground and a refusal to let policy statements or policy arguments override a statute. In *Koehler* (she sat) the court read the subdivisions of § 3583(e) by their words, applied lenity to any ambiguity that would enlarge punishment, treated Chapter Seven § 7B1.3 as nonbinding and unable to override the statute, and said broader flexibility was for Congress. Applied here, § 3582(a) governs the length of any imprisonment term, and the government's reading would allow longer custody for a purpose the statutes disclaim (§ 994(k); *Maier*, on which she sat, read § 994(k) as rejecting imprisonment for rehabilitation, not rehabilitation as a goal). If § 3583(e) were thought ambiguous about § 3582(a), lenity favors the defendant (*Koehler*; *Alese*). Treatment stays available through § 3553(a)(2)(D) in conditions. Her *Restrepo* opinion declined to adjust prison length for matters the Bureau of Prisons controls and required a departure to rationally remedy the identified problem; by analogy only, it cuts against lengthening a term to reach an institutional program. Issue 2: *Bermudez* controls. *Alese* used Chapter Seven only as interpretive context, and *Koehler* treated its statements as nonbinding.

Alternative path that matters: affirm on the government's textual point that § 3583(e) incorporates (a)(2)(D) and not § 3582(a). Her strict textualism in *Koehler* makes it conceivable, but I rate it weaker for the reasons given for Oakes.

**Remedy:** Same as Oakes. In addition, *Mayo* (her opinion) shows she will treat a sentencing error as harmless where the district court said it would impose the same sentence regardless; no such statement appears here, so remand for the district court to decide. No instruction may rely on a new supervised-release term (*Koehler*; *Bermudez*).

**Sources:** *United States v. Renaud*, 999 F.2d 622 (2d Cir. 1993) (Kearse, J.) (Rule 11 and § 3583(b); harmless-error remedy; no § 3582 analysis). *United States v. Restrepo*, 999 F.2d 640 (2d Cir. 1993) (Kearse, J.). *United States v. Mayo*, 14 F.3d 128 (2d Cir. 1994) (Kearse, J.). Panels she joined: *Koehler* (Mahoney, J.); *Alese*, 6 F.3d 85 (2d Cir. 1993) (per curiam); *Maier* (Newman, J.). Authorities as listed for Oakes, plus *Lee* for persuasive weight.

**Join limits:** She would not join a rationale resting on policy or legislative-history grounds without a textual footing (*Koehler*), or one that treats Chapter Seven as binding (*Bermudez*). She would not join any remand instruction presupposing a new supervised-release term after prison. Routine agreement with a text-based disposition and a plain vacate-and-remand remedy is expected.
