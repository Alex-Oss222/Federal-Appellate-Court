## Departure from history

The simulated panel vacated the seventeen-month term and remanded for resentencing, where the historical panel affirmed the sentence with no resentencing. The vote stayed two to one but the sides reversed: Kearse (author) and Stone-Khoury formed the majority and Oakes dissented, whereas historically Oakes (author) and Altimari formed the majority and Kearse dissented. The reasoning on rehabilitation changed (lengthening revocation imprisonment to promote rehabilitation is barred, not permitted), while the conclusion that the Chapter Seven range is advisory and not a twelve-month ceiling is the same.

## Historical comparison extract

On February 17, 1994, a Second Circuit panel of Oakes, Kearse and Altimari affirmed the seventeen-month revocation sentence in United States v. Anderson, No. 93-1097. Judge Oakes wrote the opinion, joined by Judge Altimari; Judge Kearse dissented on the rehabilitation question. The majority treated the Chapter Seven recommendations as advisory and held that treatment and rehabilitation considerations could support the length of a revocation prison term, distinguishing the bar on imprisonment for rehabilitation at an initial sentencing. The simulated panel (Oakes, Kearse, Stone-Khoury) decided the same day and reached a different judgment: vacatur and remand for resentencing, two to one, Kearse writing with Stone-Khoury and Oakes dissenting. The judgment changed because the reasoning on rehabilitation under 18 U.S.C. §§ 3582(a) and 3583(e) changed. On Chapter Seven the reasoning is the same: advisory, no twelve-month ceiling. Source: United States v. Anderson, 15 F.3d 278, 281-86 (2d Cir. 1994), https://law.justia.com/cases/federal/appellate-courts/F3/15/278/536351/.
