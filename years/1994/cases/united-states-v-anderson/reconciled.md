## Historical comparison

The historical panel (Oakes, Kearse, Altimari) affirmed the 17-month revocation sentence on 1994-02-17, 15 F.3d 278. Oakes wrote; Altimari joined; Kearse dissented from allowing rehabilitation to determine the length of revocation imprisonment, reading the statutory prohibition and related sentencing provisions together. The majority treated Chapter Seven as advisory and held that treatment and rehabilitation considerations could support the choice of term, distinguishing the revocation framework from the prohibition on imprisonment for rehabilitation at an initial sentencing. The term was within the 24-month ceiling. Kearse's objection did not make the 6 to 12 month range a binding ceiling. No resentencing; mandate status unknown.

## James L. Oakes

**Reconciled disposition:** Issue 1: sentence affirmed; revocation findings and the 17-month term stand. Issue 2: the claimed mandatory 6 to 12 month ceiling is rejected. Net: affirm.

**Ground:** Chapter Seven statements are advisory under *Bermudez* and must be considered, but do not bind, and the term is under the statutory ceiling. On Issue 1, section 3583(e) is a distinct revocation framework that expressly incorporates the treatment consideration of section 3553(a)(2)(D) and does not cross-reference section 3582(a); the prohibition on using imprisonment to promote rehabilitation addresses the initial decision to imprison and does not bar treatment considerations in choosing the revocation term. This is stated at the generality history.md reports. The textual points (the silence of section 3583(e) as to section 3582(a), a drafting-difference inference as in *Darmand*) are consistent with history but are my inference, not reported grounds.

**Remedy:** None. The sentence stands; no resentencing. Mandate timing is not established.

**Join limits:** Would not join a rationale making the range binding or requiring departure-style findings to exceed it (*Bermudez*; *Johnson*), or one treating the range as legally meaningless; a join must not describe *Grasso*, on which he sat, as superseded. Would not join a holding that treatment is irrelevant to every section 3583(e) choice. Would not join a vacatur resting on section 3582(a) governing revocation prison length. The commitment's limit on affirming through the cooperation ground alone was tied to the vacatur path and is withdrawn as a barrier; history does not show whether he would join a narrower affirmance that avoids the rehabilitation question, so that is unresolved.

**Basis for departure from the commitment:** The commitment's vacatur rested on inference from method; it found no writing of his on section 3582(a) and itself named affirmance on the government's reading as the plausible alternative.
