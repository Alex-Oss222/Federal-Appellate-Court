## J. Edward Lumbard (Senior Circuit Judge)

**Disposition:** Affirm the judgment of conviction in full, by component. (1) Juror claim: no abuse of discretion in denying the Rule 33 motion. The first McDonough requirement fails because counsel conceded an honest omission, and the known relationship (uncle of the wife of an assistant U.S. attorney uninvolved in the case) does not itself establish actual or legally implied bias. (2) Overt-act response and unanimity charge (conspiracy count): no error, and so no plain error. (3) Remaining charge objections (description of the alleged falsehoods; mailing stipulation) on the ten mail-fraud counts: no plain error. (4) Sentence: not independently challenged, left undisturbed.

**Ground:** For the juror claim, McDonough Power Equipment, Inc. v. Greenwood, 464 U.S. 548, 556 (1984), requires a materially dishonest answer and a truthful answer that would have supported cause. Langford, 990 F.2d 65 (2d Cir. 1993), did not drop the first element, and Colombo, 869 F.2d 149 (2d Cir. 1989), confined its concern to relatives of attorneys actually involved in the investigation and prosecution. Independent bias remains a separate claim, but it must be proved; counsel waived the only chance to develop it, and the family tie to an uninvolved lawyer is not a close connection to the prosecuting team. Review is for abuse of discretion, with the legal tests reviewed independently. For the charge, Rules 30 and 52(b) and United States v. Olano, 507 U.S. 725 (1993), govern. The whole charge is read. Schiff, 801 F.2d 108 (2d Cir. 1986), and Harris, 8 F.3d 943 (2d Cir. 1993), treat a general unanimity instruction as ordinarily sufficient, and the court twice required unanimity on material falsity. The jury's question asked whether every overt act must be proved, not which act occurred. The court need not decide whether agreement on a specific overt act is required, because the charge is adequate even if it is. Because there is no error, the later Olano steps are not reached.

**Remedy:** Affirm. No remand and no further juror inquiry, since counsel declined to question the juror.

**Sources:** Own writings (all located through CourtListener metadata and case.law text; no reporter pagination was available, so no pinpoints are given for them):
- United States v. Blume, 967 F.2d 45 (2d Cir. 1992) (Lumbard, J.): wide discretion over juror matters, resolved on "special facts"; a tardily raised juror claim rejected on waiver grounds.
- United States v. Sacco, 923 F.2d 970 (2d Cir. 1991) (Lumbard, J.): juror challenge waived for lack of timely objection absent a showing of prejudice. The rehearing opinion, 927 F.2d 726 (2d Cir. 1991) (Lumbard, J.), changed only the conspiracy ruling and does not address that point.
- United States v. Boothe, 994 F.2d 63 (2d Cir. 1993) (Lumbard, J.): unobjected-to instruction reviewed only for plain error, correction reserved for errors that seriously affect fairness, integrity or public reputation; instruction found proper.
- United States v. Tortora, 30 F.3d 334 (2d Cir. 1994) (Lumbard, J.): challenged instruction upheld on the record; no unanimity or plain-error discussion.
- United States v. Ruiz, 894 F.2d 501 (2d Cir. 1990) (Lumbard, J.): "especial deference" to the trial judge's voir dire findings (Batson setting; analogy only).
- United States v. Castillo, 14 F.3d 802 (2d Cir. 1994) (Lumbard, J., dissenting): found limiting instructions inadequate. He therefore does not defer to charges reflexively, but that dissent concerned instructions defense counsel helped frame, which is unlike unobjected charges here.

Authorities: McDonough; Olano (Rule 52(b) text and the waiver/forfeiture distinction verified); Schiff; Harris; Peterson, 768 F.2d 64 (2d Cir. 1985) (unobjected charge forfeited unless the error was so plain that the judge and prosecutor were derelict in countenancing it); Natelli, 527 F.2d 311 (2d Cir. 1975) (calls unanimity on a particular specification "sound practice" for multiple specifications; its reach is limited by Schiff and Harris); Remington, 191 F.2d 246 (2d Cir. 1951); Schmuck v. United States, 489 U.S. 705 (1989) (mailing must be incident to an essential part of the scheme); 18 U.S.C. §§ 371, 1341, 1291.

**Join limits:** He can join any affirmance that keeps independent actual or implied bias available (McDonough's concurring opinions and Colombo bar a holding that an honest answer immunizes all bias). He cannot join a rationale that treats a relative of any office lawyer as disqualified. Plausible variation: he may rest the factual side of the bias claim on counsel's waiver (Blume, Sacco), reaching only the legal question of implied bias, with the same result. He would not join a holding that settles the abstract overt-act unanimity question.

## Frank X. Altimari (Active Circuit Judge)

**Disposition:** Affirm in full, by the same components. (1) Juror claim: no abuse of discretion; the honest omission fails McDonough and the record shows neither actual nor impliedly presumed bias. (2) Overt-act response and unanimity charge: no error, and no plain error. (3) Remaining charge objections: no plain error. (4) Sentence: undisturbed.

**Ground:** Juror claim: same governing tests (McDonough at 556; Langford; Colombo). His closest writing, United States v. Garcia, 936 F.2d 648 (2d Cir. 1991), declined to imply bias from jurors' prior service at a trial with a common government witness, holding that challenges there "must be based on a showing of actual bias," and it upheld the trial court's actual-bias findings under "broad discretion." That decision supports requiring proof beyond a bare relationship, though it did not address relatives of government counsel or McDonough. With counsel's waiver, no actual-bias showing exists. Charge: the instruction is read as a whole and a defendant must show the charge misled or prejudiced (Valdez, 16 F.3d 1324 (2d Cir. 1994); Anderson v. Branen, 17 F.3d 552 (2d Cir. 1994) (civil case: failure to object waives, subject to plain error that goes to the essence of the case)). Under Schiff, Harris and Olano, a general unanimity charge plus the two explicit unanimity-on-falsity directions suffices, and the answer to the overt-act question did not disturb that command. He has also affirmed without deciding an abstract instruction question where no prejudice was shown (Teicher, 987 F.2d 112 (2d Cir. 1993) (harmless beyond doubt)), which fits the packet's note that the overt-act question need not be settled. He would add, as a fallback only, that the record shows no effect on substantial rights.

**Remedy:** Affirm. No remand or evidentiary hearing.

**Sources:** Own writings (no pinpoints verified; case.law text lacks pagination):
- United States v. Garcia, 936 F.2d 648 (2d Cir. 1991) (Altimari, J.), as above.
- Anderson v. Branen, 17 F.3d 552 (2d Cir. 1994) (Altimari, J.): charge reviewed as a whole; waiver by failure to object.
- United States v. Valdez, 16 F.3d 1324 (2d Cir. 1994) (Altimari, J.): requester must show the charge given prejudiced the fair trial.
- United States v. Johnson, 994 F.2d 980 (2d Cir. 1993) (Altimari, J.): charge sufficient if it adequately apprises the jury of the crime and defense.
- United States v. Townsend, 987 F.2d 927 (2d Cir. 1993) (Altimari, J.): charge read as a whole.
- United States v. Teicher, 987 F.2d 112 (2d Cir. 1993) (Altimari, J.): defect assumed, harmless.
No Altimari opinion located on specific unanimity or on a juror related to a government lawyer.

Authorities: same as above, including Olano, McDonough, Schiff, Harris, Peterson, Colombo (opinion by Winter, J.), Langford (opinion by Kearse, J.).

**Join limits:** Routine agreement with an affirmance on the same grounds. He could not join a rationale that holds relationship to any office prosecutor disqualifying as a matter of law, or one that makes McDonough the exclusive route to a bias claim (Garcia treats actual bias as independently available). He would also resist deciding the overt-act unanimity question where the charge suffices on either view.
