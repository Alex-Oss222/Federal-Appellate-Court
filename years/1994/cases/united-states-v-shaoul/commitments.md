## J. Edward Lumbard (Senior Circuit Judge)

**Disposition:** (A) Affirm denial of a new trial. (B) Affirm; no plain error.

**Ground:** (A) *McDonough Power Equipment, Inc. v. Greenwood*, 464 U.S. 548, 556 (1984), requires a dishonest answer and a correct answer that would have supported a cause challenge; counsel's express concession of good faith defeats the first element, and *United States v. Langford*, 990 F.2d 65 (2d Cir. 1993), does not remove it. Implied bias fails on this record. The prosecutor-relative was uninvolved and the tie runs through a spouse's uncle. *United States v. Colombo*, 869 F.2d 149 (2d Cir. 1989), points at attorneys "actually involved in this investigation and prosecution." *Dennis v. United States*, 339 U.S. 162 (1950), refuses to presume bias from government connection alone. *United States v. Brown*, 644 F.2d 101 (2d Cir. 1981), declined to presume bias from a juror's employment by the robbed bank. Actual bias is unproved because counsel declined to question the juror. Lumbard's *Boothe* treats a deliberate strategic choice as a waiver ("waived his right to complain"), and his *Calbas* leaves post-verdict juror inquiry to the trial court's "wide discretion." The legal claim stays reviewable, but the evidentiary gap is counsel's own. (B) The unobjected charge is reviewed under Rule 52(b), as in *Boothe*. The general unanimity charge plus two express unanimity requirements on material falsity is ordinarily enough (*United States v. Schiff*, 801 F.2d 108 (2d Cir. 1986); *United States v. Harris*, 8 F.3d 943 (2d Cir. 1993); *United States v. Peterson*, 768 F.2d 64 (2d Cir. 1985); *United States v. Natelli*, 527 F.2d 311 (2d Cir. 1975)). The jury note, read as asking whether every act must be proved, matches *United States v. DiLapi*, 651 F.2d 140 (2d Cir. 1981). The proposed overt-act agreement requirement can be assumed without decision. The stipulation sentence is read within the whole charge, and no error is plain. *Olano* is the operative test.

**Remedy:** Affirm the judgment and the Rule 33 denial. No remand for a juror hearing, since the opportunity was offered and relinquished.

**Sources:** *United States v. Calbas*, 821 F.2d 887 (2d Cir. 1987) (Lumbard, J.; extra-record juror information, objective prejudice, trial-court discretion over inquiry); *United States v. Boothe*, 994 F.2d 63 (2d Cir. 1993) (Lumbard, J.; unobjected charge reviewed only for plain error under Rule 52(b), citing *United States v. Young*, 470 U.S. 1, 15 (1985); deliberate strategic choice waived complaint); *United States v. Tortora*, 30 F.3d 334 (2d Cir. 1994) (Lumbard, J.; challenged charge upheld). Authorities above, plus *Smith v. Phillips*, 455 U.S. 209 (1982) (O'Connor, J., concurring), and *Garland v. United States*, 182 F.2d 801 (4th Cir. 1950) (persuasive only).

**Join limits:** Would not join a per se disqualification of any relative of any office lawyer (inconsistent with *Colombo* as read and *Dennis*). Would not join a rationale making honesty under *McDonough* dispensable, or one ordering a juror hearing despite counsel's waiver. Would not join a holding that a particularized unanimity or overt-act charge is required (*Natelli*, *Peterson*, *Schiff*). Routine agreement with an affirmance on narrower waiver, no-error or assumed-error grounds.

## Frank X. Altimari (Active Circuit Judge)

**Disposition:** (A) Affirm denial of a new trial. (B) Affirm; no error, and no plain error.

**Ground:** (A) Same *McDonough*/*Langford* analysis: honest omission conceded, so the nondisclosure route fails. On independent bias, his *United States v. Garcia*, 936 F.2d 648 (2d Cir. 1991), requires proof: "actual bias must be proved, not implied" for jurors with prior exposure to a witness, and a trial judge's finding of impartiality stands absent "clear abuse." Applied here, the indirect, uninvolved relationship is not within *Colombo*'s concern, and *Dennis* and *Haynes* (*United States v. Haynes*, 398 F.2d 980 (2d Cir. 1968)) refuse to infer bias from status. The record holds no finding or testimony of partiality. Whether the juror claim is also waived need not be resolved. (B) *United States v. Locascio*, 6 F.3d 924 (2d Cir. 1993), reads the charge as a whole, applies Rule 30 where there was no specific objection, and reverses on Rule 52(b) only for a miscarriage of justice (citing *United States v. Frady*, 456 U.S. 152, 163 n.14 (1982)). Under the same authorities as above, the charge as a whole required unanimity on falsity and contained no error, so the plain-error steps are not reached; if reached, no substantial-rights showing exists (*Olano*: defendant's burden). The *Collins* approach (below) does not change this, because no error is conceded.

**Remedy:** Affirm. No remand; the district court's discretion on the record presented was not abused.

**Sources:** *United States v. Garcia*, 936 F.2d 648 (2d Cir. 1991) (Altimari, J.; implied versus actual bias, deference to trial judge); *United States v. Locascio*, 6 F.3d 924 (2d Cir. 1993) (Altimari, J.; unobjected charge, whole-charge review, Rule 52(b)); *United States v. Collins*, 957 F.2d 72 (2d Cir. 1992) (Altimari, J.; unobjected omission of an element, conceded error, resolved as harmless under *Chapman*/*Yates v. Evatt* without a Rule 52(b) analysis). Shared authorities as above. He wrote no pre-cutoff opinion found on nondisclosure by a juror related to a prosecutor.

**Join limits:** Would not join an implied-bias rule keyed only to kinship or office-wide employment (*Garcia*; *Dennis*; O'Connor, J., concurring in *Smith v. Phillips*, which reserves implied bias for extreme situations). Would not join a rationale that dispenses with *McDonough*'s honesty element. Would resist a holding that specific unanimity is mandatory, since his *Locascio* analysis turns on the charge as a whole. Ordinary joins on narrower or alternative affirmance grounds.

## Open points and source gaps

- Neither judge has a located pre-cutoff opinion on a juror related to a prosecutor, on specific overt-act unanimity, on constructive amendment, or on a mailing stipulation. Those components rest on circuit law and the record only; no judicial philosophy is inferred.
- Lumbard's *Boothe* waiver involved a witness question, not a juror. The analogy to counsel's waiver of juror examination is an inference, not a holding. A narrower panel path, resting on waiver or on failure to prove bias without reaching the broader implied-bias question, is plausible and would matter to a joint rationale.
- The static.case.law texts carry no page markers. Pinpoints in neutral.md (*Colombo* at 152, *Langford* at 68, *Schiff* at 114-15, *Harris* at 945, *Natelli* at 325, *Remington* at 250, *Peterson* at 68, *Garland* at 802) are unverified here. *Schiff*, as retrieved, speaks of complexity or other factors creating a danger of jury confusion; the phrase "unusual complexity" in neutral.md was not found verbatim.
