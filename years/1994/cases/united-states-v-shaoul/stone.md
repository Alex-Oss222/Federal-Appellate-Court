## Stone's Position

### Component 1

**Component:** Juror nondisclosure and an indirect relationship to an uninvolved prosecutor

**Jurisdiction and reach:** Counsel expressly conceded an honest omission and relinquished questioning the juror. The legal claim of disqualification from the known relationship remains; the waived factual opportunity cannot be described as mere forfeiture. The statutory route and unsettled jurisdictional predicates in the neutral baseline remain controlling; this proposal does not assume them away.

**Proposed disposition:** Affirm denial of a new trial on this record.

**Governing law:** Abuse of discretion in denying a new trial, with independent review of the legal nondisclosure and implied-bias tests. McDonough requires a materially dishonest answer and a truthful answer that would have supported cause. Independent actual or implied bias is a distinct claim; an honest answer is not immunity from demonstrated bias. The claimant must establish the relevant factual or categorical ground.

McDonough Power Equipment, Inc. v. Greenwood, 464 U.S. 548, 556 (1984), supplies the two-part nondisclosure test. Fed. R. Crim. P. 33; United States v. Langford, 990 F.2d 65, 68 (2d Cir. 1993); United States v. Colombo, 869 F.2d 149, 152 (2d Cir. 1989). Garland v. United States, 182 F.2d 801, 802 (4th Cir. 1950), is persuasive only and does not erase every familial-bias concern.

**Legal relationship:** Applying binding law.

**Proposed holding:** The conceded honest omission defeats the McDonough claim, and this particular indirect relationship to an uninvolved prosecutor does not alone establish disqualifying implied bias.

**Legal reasoning:** Stone should propose affirmance. The right to an impartial jury is fundamental, but the application must begin with what is established about this juror, not a speculative connection to the prosecutor's office. Counsel conceded an honest omission and elected not to develop an evidentiary record when the juror was available. Stone should enforce that choice while deciding the asserted per se legal rule on its merits.

The proposed rule should be no broader than necessary: this indirect relationship to an uninvolved prosecutor does not, standing alone, establish disqualifying implied bias. A different relationship, participation in the case, exposure to information about the prosecution, or actual evidence of partiality could require a different inquiry. Stone should not equate office-wide professional association with participation in a particular criminal case, but neither should he endorse *Garland* as eliminating family-relationship objections altogether.

Stone should not rely on the apparent strength of the fraud case to excuse an actually biased jury. The juror claim fails because the relevant form of dishonesty or bias was not established, not because the photographs and witnesses would probably have produced the same verdict before another tribunal. The instructional claim likewise should be resolved by the charge and the governing error standard, rather than a general declaration that a defendant against whom the evidence is strong needs fewer procedural protections.

**Independent or alternative considerations:** No additional rule is proposed beyond the interpretation and application stated here.

**Preferred scope:** The conceded honest omission defeats the McDonough claim, and this particular indirect relationship to an uninvolved prosecutor does not alone establish disqualifying implied bias. The lawful outer boundary is the issue and remedial limit stated here, not a broader rule about unpresented facts.

Case-level proposed scope: the conceded honest nondisclosure does not satisfy *McDonough*; the particular indirect relationship to an uninvolved prosecutor does not independently establish automatic disqualification; the actual charge presents no reversible unanimity error.

**Fallback position:** Proposed: reject only the particular categorical relationship theory and the conceded-honesty nondisclosure claim, reserving the wider law of implied bias.

Case-level nonjoin limits, also proposed: family ties to a prosecutor never matter; honest nondisclosure immunizes actual bias; strength of guilt evidence cures a structurally partial tribunal; or every conspiracy case requires a newly prescribed special verdict absent governing authority.

**Remedy and remand:** Leave the judgment intact without an evidentiary hearing on the questioning counsel expressly declined.

**Reserved questions:** Closer relationships, participation in the prosecution, access to case information, and actual evidence of partiality may require a different result. Strength of evidence is not a cure for an actually biased jury.

**Separate writing:** Proposed only. A separate opinion would defend or delimit this proposition: The conceded honest omission defeats the McDonough claim, and this particular indirect relationship to an uninvolved prosecutor does not alone establish disqualifying implied bias. It would explain why the authorities and record support the disposition stated above and answer the contrary position addressed in the legal reasoning. A concurrence in the judgment is proposed only if the court adopts an independently accepted narrower ground; a dissent or partial dissent would be appropriate only if the eventual judgment or reasoning conflicts with the stated legal limits. No panel alignment, author assignment, or agreement to a particular separate opinion is established.

### Component 2

**Component:** Unanimity, the overt-act response, and the remaining unpreserved charge objections

**Jurisdiction and reach:** The original charge and supplemental answer were not objected to when correction was possible. Apply Rule 52(b), not preserved-error presumptions. The statutory route and unsettled jurisdictional predicates in the neutral baseline remain controlling; this proposal does not assume them away.

**Proposed disposition:** Reject the unpreserved charge challenges and affirm.

**Governing law:** Plain-error review: error, plainness, effect on substantial rights, and the discretionary fairness/integrity/public-reputation inquiry. A general unanimity instruction ordinarily suffices; unusual complexity or a genuine danger of incompatible factual bases may require specificity. Read the whole charge and response. A constructive broadening is different from clarification of charged falsehoods, and a mailing stipulation does not eliminate the mailing’s necessary nexus to the scheme.

United States v. Olano, 507 U.S. 725 (1993), supplies the governing plain-error framework. No additional directly controlling Supreme Court holding on the precise overt-act charge is identified. Fed. R. Crim. P. 30, 52(b); United States v. Schiff, 801 F.2d 108, 114-15 (2d Cir. 1986); United States v. Harris, 8 F.3d 943, 945 (2d Cir. 1993); Natelli, 527 F.2d 311, 325 (2d Cir. 1975); Remington, 191 F.2d 246, 250 (2d Cir. 1951); Peterson, 768 F.2d 64, 68 (2d Cir. 1985).

**Legal relationship:** Applying binding law.

**Proposed holding:** The general and specific unanimity directions, read with the accurate supplemental answer, do not establish the alleged reversible plain error in this trial.

**Legal reasoning:** The instruction challenge fails under the existing unanimity precedents and the plain-error posture. The charge addressed each count, directed unanimity, and specifically emphasized agreement on material falsehoods. The supplemental response corrected a misunderstanding about how many overt acts the government had to prove; it did not authorize a verdict assembled from mutually exclusive individual findings. There is no demonstrated complexity or contradictory submission sufficient to displace the ordinary rule on this record.

The opinion may explain why a specific unanimity instruction often provides useful clarity, but it should distinguish recommended trial practice from a new mandatory appellate rule. Affirmance leaves the criminal judgment intact. No separate evidentiary hearing is required to explore a matter counsel expressly declined to develop without alleging bad faith or another factual basis for bias.

**Independent or alternative considerations:** No additional rule is proposed beyond the interpretation and application stated here.

**Preferred scope:** The general and specific unanimity directions, read with the accurate supplemental answer, do not establish the alleged reversible plain error in this trial. The lawful outer boundary is the issue and remedial limit stated here, not a broader rule about unpresented facts.

**Fallback position:** Proposed: assume the agreement requirement asserted by the defendant and hold the actual charge adequate under the plain-error posture. The case-level nonjoin limits in Component 1 apply; no trade in accuracy or jurisdiction is authorized.
