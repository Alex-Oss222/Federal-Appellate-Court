# United States v. Shaoul

**Status:** Version 1; proposed, not user-approved. **Approval reference:** None. The source brief states that every Stone position, alternative, and negotiating limit is a proposal pending approval.

## Stone's proposed judicial position

Stone should propose affirmance. The right to an impartial jury is fundamental, but the application must begin with what is established about this juror, not a speculative connection to the prosecutor's office. Counsel conceded an honest omission and elected not to develop an evidentiary record when the juror was available. Stone should enforce that choice while deciding the asserted per se legal rule on its merits.

The proposed rule should be no broader than necessary: this indirect relationship to an uninvolved prosecutor does not, standing alone, establish disqualifying implied bias. A different relationship, participation in the case, exposure to information about the prosecution, or actual evidence of partiality could require a different inquiry. Stone should not equate office-wide professional association with participation in a particular criminal case, but neither should he endorse *Garland* as eliminating family-relationship objections altogether.

The instruction challenge fails under the existing unanimity precedents and the plain-error posture. The charge addressed each count, directed unanimity, and specifically emphasized agreement on material falsehoods. The supplemental response corrected a misunderstanding about how many overt acts the government had to prove; it did not authorize a verdict assembled from mutually exclusive individual findings. There is no demonstrated complexity or contradictory submission sufficient to displace the ordinary rule on this record.

Stone should not rely on the apparent strength of the fraud case to excuse an actually biased jury. The juror claim fails because the relevant form of dishonesty or bias was not established, not because the photographs and witnesses would probably have produced the same verdict before another tribunal. The instructional claim likewise should be resolved by the charge and the governing error standard, rather than a general declaration that a defendant against whom the evidence is strong needs fewer procedural protections.

The opinion may explain why a specific unanimity instruction often provides useful clarity, but it should distinguish recommended trial practice from a new mandatory appellate rule. Affirmance leaves the criminal judgment intact. No separate evidentiary hearing is required to explore a matter counsel expressly declined to develop without alleging bad faith or another factual basis for bias. Master Jurisprudence §§ 4, 8-9; Criminal Procedure and Punishment; Evidence, Proof, and the Record.

## Proposed negotiating limits

**Preferred and broadest defensible holding:** the conceded honest nondisclosure does not satisfy *McDonough*; the particular indirect relationship to an uninvolved prosecutor does not independently establish automatic disqualification; the actual charge presents no reversible unanimity error.

**Acceptable narrower proposal:** affirm on the conceded failure of the nondisclosure claim and the absence of a demonstrated independent bias basis, while limiting the kinship discussion to these facts. The court may assume without deciding the asserted specific-predicate unanimity requirement because the instructions adequately protected it here.

**Non-join limits:** family ties to a prosecutor never matter; honest nondisclosure immunizes actual bias; strength of guilt evidence cures a structurally partial tribunal; or every conspiracy case requires a newly prescribed special verdict absent governing authority.
