## Frank X. Altimari

**Reconciled disposition:** By component. (1) Authority to entertain the motion after the mandate and recall denial: yes. (2) Rule 60(b)(6): reverse the reopening. (3) Rule 60(b)(5): not available. (4) Independent action: not a ground of the order under review; he would not rest on it.

**Ground:** Standard Oil settles power only; Eutectic and Fine bar relitigating what the court actually decided. Entitlement under (b)(6) requires extraordinary circumstances, and a later state-court correction of a fully litigated and reviewed federal prediction is not, without more, extraordinary (Sarkisian, Moitie, Bank of New York; Matarese's narrow special-circumstances door is not met; Pierce is distinguished on its same-accident posture). The ground is finality, not forfeiture. On (b)(5), the prior-judgment clause is not met because Guggenheim is not a judgment on which this one rests, and the prospective clause reaches executory or supervised decrees only (Swift; Twelve John Does, persuasive).

**Remedy:** Reverse and restore the earlier final judgment; remand only for ministerial implementation, not to reweigh equities.

**Join limits:** As committed. He cannot join a holding that the mandate or recall denial removed power; a per se rule that disapproval of a federal prediction is extraordinary, or that Pierce is circuit law; a flat rule that a decisional change is never cognizable (Matarese cannot be overruled by a panel); or a reading that makes a completed property judgment "prospective" under (b)(5).

**Basis for any departure from the commitment or from history:** None. Disposition, authority, (b)(6) result and (b)(5) result each match history, and no changed premise gives a reason to depart. History is reported only in summary, so it does not show whether the majority leaned on a deliberate-choice (Ackermann) rationale. ## John M. Walker, Jr.

**Reconciled disposition:** By component. (1) Authority: yes, under Standard Oil. (2) Rule 60(b)(6): reverse the reopening. (3) Rule 60(b)(5): not available. (4) Independent action: not reached on this record.

**Ground:** Guggenheim is accepted as controlling New York law, but Rule 60 is a separate federal question about a final judgment. Without a statute, (b)(6) needs extraordinary circumstances (Ackermann, Matarese, Sarkisian). A mistaken prediction that was fully reviewed, followed by denied certiorari and no deliberate forfeiture, is a legal error corrected later, which Moitie, Sarkisian and Eutectic treat as insufficient. Review is for abuse of discretion, with the rule's meaning, the mandate's scope and the state-law framework reviewed independently. On (b)(5), no executory or supervised obligation exists (Swift, Twelve John Does, Sarkisian n.4; Rufo is for institutional decrees), and the reversed-prior-judgment clause does not fit.

**Remedy:** Reverse and restore the earlier final judgment; remand for ministerial steps; no remand on laches or diligence, which the 1987 panel did not reach.

**Join limits:** As committed. He cannot join a holding that the district court lacked power; he resists deciding laches, diligence, prejudice or the independent-action question on this record; and he cannot join a per se rule in either direction (Erie always compels reopening, or Rule 60 is never available after a change in decisional law). Matarese and Sarkisian are read together.

**Basis for any departure from the commitment or from history:** None. History independently shows him as the author of the reversal on the same three premises. That corroborates the commitment, whose stated basis was general method and circuit law because he has no authored opinion on (b)(5) or a post-judgment change in state decisional law. It supplies no new basis and requires no change.
