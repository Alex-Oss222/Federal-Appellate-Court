## Supreme Court merits decision, if any

None. Certiorari was denied on 1994-11-14 without opinion. A denial is not approval of the Second Circuit's reasoning. No rehearing of the denial and no second petition appear: the LOC full-text index of the U.S. Reports matches "DeWeerth" only in 486 U.S. (1988) and 513 U.S. (1994).

## Dependence on the historical judgment

- **Certiorari petition (No. 94-550):** Sought by DeWeerth, the party that lost the Rule 60(b) relief on appeal. It depends on a Second Circuit reversal against her, and on the questions the panel decided: whether a later New York high court decision (Guggenheim) was an extraordinary circumstance under Rule 60(b)(6), and whether the judgment had "prospective application" under Rule 60(b)(5). If a differently decided judgment affirmed the Rule 60 relief, the losing side would be Baldinger and Wildenstein, and a DeWeerth petition would have no object.
- **Timing caution (inference only):** The denial came 18 days after the amended opinion, and the docket number sits among petitions denied the same day. Ordinary distribution timing suggests the petition was filed before 1994-10-27 and so addressed the 1994-05-16 judgment, not the amended opinion. This is not verified. A later check should treat the petition as attached to the reversal outcome, not to the amended text.
- **Rehearing petition:** Depends on the 1994-05-16 reversal, sought by the losing party; the amendment's content is not described in the reported text.
