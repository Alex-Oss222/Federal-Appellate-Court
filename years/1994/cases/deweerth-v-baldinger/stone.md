## Stone's Position

### Component 1

**Component:** Authority to entertain Rule 60 relief after an appellate mandate

**Jurisdiction and reach:** Baldinger challenges the district court’s authority after the original appeal and the unexplained denial of recall. Authority to entertain the motion is distinct from the propriety of granting it. The statutory route and unsettled jurisdictional predicates in the neutral baseline remain controlling; this proposal does not assume them away.

**Proposed disposition:** Reject a categorical lack-of-authority theory and address whether Rule 60 permits the relief granted.

**Governing law:** Independent review of the mandate’s legal scope and the district court’s authority; the ultimate Rule 60 decision remains discretionary within lawful bounds. A district court may consider a Rule 60 motion based on later circumstances without appellate leave. It may not relitigate a matter the mandate actually disposed of. An unexplained denial of recall does not necessarily settle every ground later presented.

Standard Oil Co. of California v. United States, 429 U.S. 17, 18-19 (1976), permits consideration of a later Rule 60 motion without prior appellate permission. Fed. R. Civ. P. 60; Eutectic Corp. v. Metco, Inc., 597 F.2d 32 (2d Cir. 1979); Fine v. Bellefonte Underwriters Insurance Co., 758 F.2d 50 (2d Cir. 1985); Doe v. New York City Department of Social Services, 709 F.2d 782, 788 (2d Cir. 1983).

**Legal relationship:** Applying binding law.

**Proposed holding:** The district court possessed authority to consider the later state decision; that authority did not itself authorize vacatur of the final judgment.

**Legal reasoning:** Standard Oil separates the existence of an appellate mandate from the treatment of later events. Eutectic and Fine protect decisions actually made, not a blanket rule immunizing every judgment from the Rules. Because the recall denial supplies no explanation establishing the necessary identity of grounds, the proper inquiry remains whether this motion satisfies Rule 60. This approach preserves both the mandate and the limited residual power the Rule expressly leaves.

**Independent or alternative considerations:** No additional rule is proposed beyond the interpretation and application stated here.

**Preferred scope:** The district court possessed authority to consider the later state decision; that authority did not itself authorize vacatur of the final judgment. The lawful outer boundary is the issue and remedial limit stated here, not a broader rule about unpresented facts.

Case-level proposed scope: Rule 60(b)(6) does not reopen this final diversity judgment solely because a later authoritative state decision rejects its legal prediction; Rule 60(b)(5) does not apply merely because the prevailing party continues possessing the disputed property.

**Fallback position:** Proposed: assume only the narrow later-event authority clearly recognized by Standard Oil, without reopening anything actually settled by the mandate.

Case-level nonjoin limits, also proposed: federal predictions overriding a state's highest court in pending litigation; consent to erroneous substantive law inferred from choosing federal court; an absolute prohibition on all extraordinary Rule 60 relief; or treating every lasting property consequence as an ongoing equitable decree.

**Remedy and remand:** Decide the merits of the Rule 60 grounds rather than dismissing the motion for want of authority.

**Reserved questions:** The court need not infer reasons for the unexplained recall denial or declare that all repeat motions remain entertainable.

**Separate writing:** Proposed only. A separate opinion would defend or delimit this proposition: The district court possessed authority to consider the later state decision; that authority did not itself authorize vacatur of the final judgment. It would explain why the authorities and record support the disposition stated above and answer the contrary position addressed in the legal reasoning. A concurrence in the judgment is proposed only if the court adopts an independently accepted narrower ground; a dissent or partial dissent would be appropriate only if the eventual judgment or reasoning conflicts with the stated legal limits. No panel alignment, author assignment, or agreement to a particular separate opinion is established.

### Component 2

**Component:** Extraordinary circumstances under Rule 60(b)(6) and authoritative state-law correction

**Jurisdiction and reach:** The appeal directly challenges reopening based on Guggenheim’s rejection of the prior federal prediction. The original ownership and limitations judgment is not again on direct review. The statutory route and unsettled jurisdictional predicates in the neutral baseline remain controlling; this proposal does not assume them away.

**Proposed disposition:** Propose reversal of Rule 60(b)(6) relief while recognizing Guggenheim as authoritative New York law.

**Governing law:** Abuse of discretion, including correction of erroneous legal premises; independent review of the state-law and Rule 60 framework. Erie and York require state substantive law in pending diversity cases. Rule 60(b)(6) separately requires extraordinary circumstances for reopening a completed federal judgment. The moving party must establish those circumstances; legal error or a later corrective precedent does not automatically meet that burden.

Erie Railroad Co. v. Tompkins, 304 U.S. 64 (1938); Guaranty Trust Co. v. York, 326 U.S. 99, 109 (1945); Ackermann v. United States, 340 U.S. 193, 199 (1950); Klapprott v. United States, 335 U.S. 601 (1949). Fed. R. Civ. P. 60(b)(6); Matarese v. LeFevre, 801 F.2d 98, 106 (2d Cir. 1986); Travelers Indemnity Co. v. Sarkisian, 794 F.2d 754, 757 (2d Cir. 1986); Solomon R. Guggenheim Foundation v. Lubell, 77 N.Y.2d 311, 317-21 (1991), controlling New York substantive law; Pierce v. Cook & Co., 518 F.2d 720, 723 (10th Cir. 1975) (en banc), persuasive and transaction-specific.

**Legal relationship:** Applying binding law.

**Proposed holding:** The later authoritative rejection of a state-law prediction, without an independently extraordinary circumstance on this record, does not justify reopening the fully litigated final judgment.

**Legal reasoning:** Stone should propose reversal of the Rule 60 relief while expressly recognizing that *Guggenheim* states controlling New York substantive law. His conclusion should not defend the federal court's authority to make state policy. It should identify the different question now before the court: whether an authoritative correction of the legal premise of completed litigation, without an additional exceptional circumstance, permits reopening under the governing federal rule.

The answer should be no on this record. DeWeerth had litigated ownership and timeliness, obtained a favorable trial decision, received appellate review, and sought Supreme Court review. The alleged defect is not a denial of a hearing, fraud in obtaining the judgment, or a continuing decree operating unlawfully in changed circumstances. It is an erroneous state-law prediction. The importance of the painting and the strength of the state's later rejection do not change that category by themselves.

This conclusion should not be expressed as a waiver of correct state law through selection of a federal forum. A diversity litigant is entitled to state substantive law and does not consent to judicial invention. Nor should Stone fabricate proof that Baldinger made particular expenditures or transactions in reliance on the judgment. The finality interest is concrete in the completed adjudication of competing property claims and the legal settlement of entitlement, not in invented later conduct. Federal procedural finality can preserve a completed judgment even though a pending case would now be decided differently.

Stone should give the district court's equitable judgment its proper respect, but discretion cannot turn the ordinary occurrence of later corrective precedent into an exceptional circumstance solely by describing it as a severe legal error. *Matarese* preserves real residual authority. The opinion should leave open circumstances involving an independently extraordinary defect, a materially different relationship among judgments, or another recognized Rule 60 ground. It need not announce that a later state decision can never contribute to extraordinary circumstances.

The earlier failure to obtain an authoritative state answer remains a legitimate institutional lesson. Stone's preference for certification on an unsettled, determinative state-law issue would guide a case still open to decision. It is not a power to reopen this one outside Rule 60.

**Independent or alternative considerations:** No additional rule is proposed beyond the interpretation and application stated here.

**Preferred scope:** The later authoritative rejection of a state-law prediction, without an independently extraordinary circumstance on this record, does not justify reopening the fully litigated final judgment. The lawful outer boundary is the issue and remedial limit stated here, not a broader rule about unpresented facts.

**Fallback position:** Proposed: resolve only this completed litigation under Ackermann and Matarese, leaving materially different same-transaction or independently extraordinary cases open. The case-level nonjoin limits in Component 1 apply; no trade in accuracy or jurisdiction is authorized.

**Remedy and remand:** Reinstate the final judgment displaced by the Rule 60 order; do not redecide the original title or limitations merits.

**Reserved questions:** A later state decision may contribute to a different extraordinary-circumstances case. No rule that choosing federal court waives correct state law; no fabricated reliance expenditures.

**Separate writing:** Proposed only. A separate opinion would defend or delimit this proposition: The later authoritative rejection of a state-law prediction, without an independently extraordinary circumstance on this record, does not justify reopening the fully litigated final judgment. It would explain why the authorities and record support the disposition stated above and answer the contrary position addressed in the legal reasoning. A concurrence in the judgment is proposed only if the court adopts an independently accepted narrower ground; a dissent or partial dissent would be appropriate only if the eventual judgment or reasoning conflicts with the stated legal limits. ### Component 3

**Component:** Prospective operation under Rule 60(b)(5)

**Jurisdiction and reach:** The alternative ground for reopening concerns the prospective-effect clause. Continued possession is established; ongoing judicial supervision is not. The statutory route and unsettled jurisdictional predicates in the neutral baseline remain controlling; this proposal does not assume them away.

**Proposed disposition:** Reject Rule 60(b)(5) as an independent basis for the relief granted.

**Governing law:** Independent interpretation of prospective application; abuse of discretion within the correctly identified category. The prospective-application clause reaches an executory or ongoing equitable decree, not every completed judgment whose effects continue. The movant must identify qualifying prospective operation before changed circumstances justify relief.

United States v. Swift & Co., 286 U.S. 106, 114 (1932), illustrates modification of continuing injunctive obligations, not routine reconsideration of settled property entitlements. Fed. R. Civ. P. 60(b)(5); Twelve John Does v. District of Columbia, 841 F.2d 1133, 1139 (D.C. Cir. 1988), persuasive distinction between continued consequences and prospective application.

**Legal relationship:** Applying binding law.

**Proposed holding:** Continuing possession under this completed judgment is not prospective judicial administration within the rule.

**Legal reasoning:** The alternative prospective-effect theory fails independently. Baldinger's continued possession is a consequence of an adjudication of title-related claims, not an ongoing injunction requiring the court to administer future conduct. Treating that consequence as prospective would subject final property and money judgments to continuing substantive reconsideration. The remedy is reinstatement of the final judgment, not a new appellate decision on laches, the adequacy of the original search, or the identity of the wartime thief.

**Independent or alternative considerations:** No additional rule is proposed beyond the interpretation and application stated here.

**Preferred scope:** Continuing possession under this completed judgment is not prospective judicial administration within the rule. The lawful outer boundary is the issue and remedial limit stated here, not a broader rule about unpresented facts.

**Fallback position:** Proposed: confine the holding to completed property adjudication rather than all judgments involving possession. The case-level nonjoin limits in Component 1 apply; no trade in accuracy or jurisdiction is authorized.

**Remedy and remand:** Reverse this alternative ground along with the unsupported Rule 60(b)(6) relief.

**Reserved questions:** No decision on a genuinely ongoing injunction, independent laches adjudication, or identity of the original wartime wrongdoer.
