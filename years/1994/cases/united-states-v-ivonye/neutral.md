## Record and issues

Ivonye carried 336.5 grams of heroin concealed in his shoes through JFK Airport. He asserted that he believed he was carrying 250 grams of cocaine. The district court calculated the guideline range from the heroin actually carried, producing a range of 30 to 37 months, and imposed the bottom of that range. The sentencing judge understood that a sufficiently extraordinary discrepancy might permit a departure but declined to depart. Ivonye’s claimed belief must not be converted into an undisputed finding beyond what the sentencing record establishes. ([Justia Law](https://law.justia.com/cases/federal/appellate-courts/F3/30/275/471763/))

**Jurisdiction and reach:** Section 3742 supplies the sentencing-appeal route. A claim that the guideline was misinterpreted is reviewable; a discretionary refusal to depart, made with knowledge of authority, ordinarily is not. **Preservation:** Both calculation and departure arguments were presented. **Missing material:** Plea allocution, presentence report, complete sentencing transcript, and notice date. No separate conviction challenge is identified.

## Law entering the event

**Cutoff:** July 8, 1994. **Simulation holdings:** None supplied.

### Issue 1: Actual drugs or reasonably expected drugs

**Review and governing law:** Guideline interpretation is reviewed de novo; factual findings receive clear-error review, with the deference then prescribed for application. Sections 1B1.3 and 2D1.1 distinguish personally undertaken conduct from conduct attributable through joint activity. *United States v. De Velasquez*, 28 F.3d 2 (2d Cir. 1994), governs the actual drugs personally carried. *Stinson v. United States*, 508 U.S. 36 (1993), addresses authoritative guideline commentary. ([Justia Law](https://law.justia.com/cases/federal/appellate-courts/F3/30/275/471763/))

**Positions:** Ivonye urges limitation to the substance and quantity he expected. The government argues that foreseeability restrictions applicable to others’ conduct do not reduce the quantity personally imported. The court must avoid transporting a joint-activity limitation into a different relevant-conduct category.

**Paths:** Sustain a calculation based on actual personal possession if the governing provisions so require. Remand for recalculation only if the district court applied the wrong category or made a material factual error.

### Issue 2: Departure and appellate review

**Governing authority:** *United States v. Imariagbe*, 999 F.2d 706 (2d Cir. 1993), recognizes the possible significance of an extraordinary disparity between expected and actual drugs. *United States v. Whittaker*, 999 F.2d 38 (2d Cir. 1993), limits appellate review of a knowing discretionary refusal to depart. ([Justia Law](https://law.justia.com/cases/federal/appellate-courts/F3/30/275/471763/))

**Positions and paths:** Ivonye argues that the difference creates sufficiently unusual unfairness. The government emphasizes the judge’s acknowledged authority and discretionary rejection. Review a legal misunderstanding of authority, but do not reweigh an informed departure refusal merely because another sentence was possible. No directly controlling Supreme Court decision requires a departure for this discrepancy.
