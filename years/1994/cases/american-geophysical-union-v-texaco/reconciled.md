## Historical comparison

The historical event (December 23, 1994 amended version, 37 F.3d 881, on denial of rehearing) affirmed the interlocutory rejection of fair use for the stipulated copying. Newman wrote the majority opinion and Winter joined it. Jacobs dissented, stressing the scientific purpose, the character of an individual researcher's files, and the circularity risk of letting an emerging permission market defeat fair use. The majority assessed the complete retained articles under all four § 107 factors and gave weight to their substitute function and the evidence of an institutional copying-license market. It did not decide every scientific photocopy, damages, or the injunction. The limited determination was affirmed and the remaining district-court proceedings were left open. Mandate status is unknown.

The historical outcome depended on the same stipulated eight-article record, the same § 1292(b) posture, the same reproduction-licensing evidence, and law available by late 1994. Those premises survive in the simulation. The changed premises are three. First, the dissenting seat is held by a different person. Second, the neutral packet frames the event as a merits determination on resubmitted papers, a disclosed scheduling premise, whereas history shows an amendment on denial of rehearing. Third, history.md reports no factor-by-factor allocation, only that all four factors were assessed.

Neither retained judge's commitment rests on the dissenter's presence. Each is built from that judge's own writings and the record, and each already answers the circularity point (the market must be a mechanism actually operating for this use, not an announced fee). ## Jon O. Newman

**Reconciled disposition:** Not fair use as to the eight retained complete copies. Affirm the limited interlocutory ruling.

**Ground:** The four factors are weighed together, with Texaco bearing the burden. Factor four decides. The district court found a convenient, reasonably priced licensing mechanism for this kind of institutional copying. That is the owners' ordinary reproduction market and a potential market under Campbell, and the retained copies serve the same reading and reference function as authorized copies. Factor one leans modestly to Texaco because research is a listed purpose and the commercial setting does not disqualify, but it is tempered because the copies are non-transformative working duplicates. Factor two favors Texaco (factual works). Factor three favors the publishers (each article is a separately copyrighted whole). Thin subscription-loss proof does not matter. Sony and Williams & Wilkins are distinguished.

**Remedy:** Affirm and remand for matters not submitted. No injunction directed. No ruling that a license fee measures damages for earlier conduct.

**Join limits:** He will not join a rationale that an available or announced fee alone proves harm, a categorical rule for corporate research copying, or any ruling on damages or unsubmitted copying. He can join a narrower factor-four rationale tied to the functioning mechanism, with a research-friendlier weighing of factor one.

**Basis for any departure from the commitment or from history:** None. The commitment matches history on disposition, scope limits, and remedy. History's authorship by Newman is consistent with the commitment, but authorship is an assembly question and is not decided here. History.md is silent on the factor-one allocation, so it neither confirms nor contradicts the commitment's modest lean to Texaco. The commitment stands, and the point affects opinion text, not the judgment.

## Agreement between the two modeled judges (by component)

- **Disposition:** Agree. Both hold the eight retained copies not fair use and affirm the limited interlocutory ruling.
- **Sufficient ground:** Agree at the level that matters for a majority proposition. Both rest the result on factor four, the operating licensing mechanism for this use, with factor two for Texaco and factor three for the publishers. Both treat factor one as modestly for Texaco. They differ in emphasis: Newman expressly tempers factor one for non-transformative working duplicates, and Winter does not say so. The difference is one of weight, not result.
- **Remedy:** Agree. Affirm, remand, reserve damages, injunction scope, other copying, and earlier-period market conditions.
- **Join limits:** Compatible, with a shared zone. An opinion limited to retained reference copies on the stipulated record, resting factor four on the operating mechanism, with no fee-proves-harm reasoning, no categorical rule, and no ruling on damages or unsubmitted copying, satisfies both.

Conclusion: two of the three votes for the judgment exist without the third judge. A two-judge majority proposition also exists at the shared-zone level. If an opinion goes beyond the zone (circularity-based reasoning, a bright-line rule, or a damages ruling), neither judge is committed to join it, and Winter would concur in the judgment or write separately.
