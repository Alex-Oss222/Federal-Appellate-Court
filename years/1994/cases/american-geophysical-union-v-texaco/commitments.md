# Commitments: American Geophysical Union v. Texaco, Inc. (2d Cir. No. 92-9341)

**Stage:** neutral modeling. **Legal cutoff:** 1994-12-23. **Inputs:** neutral.md, ENGINE.md, and the public sources cited below.

## Jon O. Newman (Chief Judge, active circuit service)

**Disposition:** (1) Ultimate fair-use question for the eight retained complete copies: not fair use; affirm the interlocutory ruling as to that practice on the stipulated record. Purpose and character leans modestly to Texaco (research is a listed purpose; commercial setting is not disqualifying) but is tempered because the copies are non-transformative working duplicates. Nature of the work favors Texaco (factual). Amount favors the publishers (each article is a separately copyrighted whole). Market effect favors the publishers and decides the balance. Confidence is moderate; factors one and four are the close points. (2) Scope: the holding reaches only copies retained as reference duplicates, as here; copying for immediate use in a particular experiment is not before the court. (3) Damages, injunction scope, other photocopying, and earlier-period market conditions: not decided.

**Ground:** Fair use is a mixed question; the ultimate conclusion gets full review and subsidiary findings are reviewed for clear error (Twin Peaks, which he wrote, quoting Harper & Row; Harper & Row also allows decision as a matter of law when findings suffice). Texaco carries the burden on the defense (Campbell). The factors are weighed together, not scored (Campbell; his Salinger opinion concludes "On balance"). His own cases treat research and scholarship categories as favoring a commercial user without special latitude (Salinger), treat commercial purpose as "not an all-or-nothing matter" (Twin Peaks), and ask about the "potential market" (Salinger) and markets in which the owner has a "legitimate interest" (Twin Peaks). Campbell removes any commercial-use presumption of harm, so harm must come from the record. The district court found that the Copyright Clearance Center arrangements (TRS and AAS) offered convenient, reasonably priced licensing of this kind of institutional copying; he would review that finding for clear error. That is the owners' ordinary market for reproduction, not a fee invented to suppress criticism, so it falls within the potential markets Campbell recognizes (those creators would develop or license). The retained copies serve the same reading and reference function as an authorized copy, and Harper & Row's test (whether widespread use would adversely affect the potential market) points to substitution for licensed copies. Thin subscription-loss proof does not matter because factor four reaches the licensing market. Sony is distinguished (private, noncommercial, no shown harm). Williams & Wilkins is persuasive only (1909 Act, government libraries with copying controls, no proven harm, equally divided affirmance). Circularity is answered by tying the market to a mechanism actually operating for this use, not an announced fee.

**Remedy:** Affirm the limited determination and remand for matters not submitted. No injunction directed; his New Era dissent from denial of rehearing en banc says 17 U.S.C. § 502 leaves injunctions discretionary and the public interest relevant. No ruling that a license fee measures damages for earlier conduct.

**Join limits:** He would not join a rationale that an available or announced license fee, standing alone, proves market harm, or a categorical rule either way for corporate research copying (his New Era dissent resists per se rules). He would not join anything deciding damages or reaching unsubmitted copying. A narrower factor-four rationale tied to the functioning licensing mechanism, with a research-friendlier weighing of factor one, is compatible. A fair-use result becomes the plausible alternative only if a colleague's analysis makes the licensing market non-cognizable; his own writings do not supply that premise.

## Ralph K. Winter, Jr. (Active Circuit Judge)

**Disposition:** (1) Same direction, lower confidence: not fair use as to the eight retained copies, affirming on a narrow factor-four ground, with factor two for Texaco, factor three for the publishers, and factor one close and modestly for Texaco. (2) Scope limited to this stipulated record and retained reference copies. (3) Damages, injunction, other copying: reserved. The fair-use result is a genuine alternative for him and matters here: his writings demand proven rather than assumed harm, and subscription-loss proof is thin, so the outcome turns on whether the licensing market is cognizable.

**Ground:** Same standard of review. He joined Twin Peaks, which supplies full review of the ultimate question, so his 1982 DC Comics statement that the factors "raise essentially factual issues" is no barrier on a developed written record. His own writings support three points. First, harm must be shown: Diamond v. Am-Law (a concession on market harm was dispositive) and Business Trends v. Freedonia (rejecting a "speculative and artificial" hypothetical-sale measure). Second, licensing is a recognized ownership interest: DC Comics states that "one of the benefits of ownership of copyrighted material is the right to license its use for a fee" and that the owner is best placed to weigh licensing revenue against other benefits; Legi-Tech treats lost retransmission revenue as the measure of a free-rider's cost. Third, factual works receive narrow protection (Key Publications; Feist; Maxtone-Graham, which he joined). Business Trends is distinguishable: the infringing study there competed for no sale, whereas here an operating channel for this very reproduction exists and the district court found it convenient and reasonably priced. With Campbell (no presumption of harm; potential markets are those owners would develop or license) and Harper & Row's widespread-use test, the found licensing market and complete non-transformative copies kept as working duplicates support unfairness on this record. Williams & Wilkins and Sony are distinguished as for Newman.

**Remedy:** Affirm the limited ruling and remand. Any opinion should reserve damages: Business Trends rejected a "value of use" award measured by a fictive purchase price under § 504(b), which signals caution about hypothetical license-fee damages, and Bourne Co. v. Tower Records shows delay and acquiescence bear on irreparable harm for any later injunction. Neither is before the court.

**Sources:** DC Comics Inc. v. Reel Fantasy, Inc., 696 F.2d 24 (2d Cir. 1982) (Winter, J.); Diamond v. Am-Law Publishing Corp., 745 F.2d 142 (2d Cir. 1984) (Winter, J.); Legi-Tech, Inc. v. Keiper, 766 F.2d 728 (2d Cir. 1985) (Winter, J.; First Amendment case, copyright only by analogy); Business Trends Analysts, Inc. v. Freedonia Group, Inc., 887 F.2d 399 (2d Cir. 1989) (Winter, J.; damages); Key Publications, Inc. v. Chinatown Today Publishing Enterprises, Inc., 945 F.2d 509 (2d Cir. 1991) (Winter, J.; compilations); Bourne Co. v. Tower Records, Inc., 976 F.2d 99 (2d Cir. 1992) (Winter, J.). Joined, not authored: Twin Peaks, 996 F.2d 1366; Maxtone-Graham v. Burtchaell, 803 F.2d 1253 (2d Cir. 1986) (Kaufman, J.); the New Era en banc dissent, 884 F.2d 659. Supreme Court, statutory, and district court authorities are as listed for Newman.

**Join limits:** He would not join a rationale that treats the existence of a license fee as self-proving harm; his cases tie harm to evidence and actual markets. He would not join a categorical rule against complete copies (Sony forecloses it; he also joined the New Era dissent's resistance to rigid rules) or any ruling on damages. He could join any rationale that rests factor four on the operating licensing mechanism for this use. If a lead rationale rests only on circular reasoning or a bright-line rule, he would concur in the judgment or write separately.
