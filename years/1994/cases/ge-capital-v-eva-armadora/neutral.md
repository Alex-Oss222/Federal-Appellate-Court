## Record and issues

The 1986 financing agreement provided $18 million for two tankers and required an additional payment following sale or refinancing. The special-interest formula was disputed: the parties disagreed whether the deductions included previously paid principal and interest or only amounts outstanding at refinancing. Early repayment required GECC’s consent. ([Law Resource](https://law.resource.org/pub/us/case/reporter/F3/037/37.F3d.41.93-9282.1573.html))

During negotiations, GECC expressly described a formula yielding approximately $3 million. Armadora sent a draft addendum with a cover letter stating that it formalized the agreement recapitulated in GECC’s October 27, 1987 letter. The addendum itself retained the ambiguous original language. GECC then consented. Armadora later asserted substantially larger deductions. The district court construed the original contract in Armadora’s favor. The estoppel issue concerns the intervening communications and induced consent, not merely which original interpretation is more plausible. ([Law Resource](https://law.resource.org/pub/us/case/reporter/F3/037/37.F3d.41.93-9282.1573.html))

**Jurisdiction:** Section 1291 review of a final contract judgment. The original jurisdictional pleadings and citizenship allegations were not retrieved. **Preservation:** GECC’s estoppel theory is presented; the appeal is not confined to abstract interpretation. **Missing material:** Full loan and addenda, trial transcript, final judgment and fee calculation, and notice date.

### Issue 1: Contract interpretation and estoppel

**Review and law:** Legal interpretation is independent; bench-trial findings receive clear-error review. New York law controls the substantive contract and estoppel questions. *Payroll Express Corp. v. Aetna Casualty & Surety*, 659 F.2d 285 (2d Cir. 1981), recognizes the significance of misleading conduct and detrimental reliance. State appellate authority informs the prediction of New York law. No Supreme Court holding directly decides this state-law dispute. ([Law Resource](https://law.resource.org/pub/us/case/reporter/F3/037/37.F3d.41.93-9282.1573.html))

**Test and burdens:** GECC must establish the conduct creating the estoppel, the relevant knowledge and duty to correct a misleading impression, reasonable reliance, and detriment. Silence is not automatically actionable; its significance depends on the relationship, communications, and opportunity to correct an understanding being used to obtain consent.

**Positions:** GECC relies on its explicit calculation, Armadora’s confirming cover letter, and the valuable consent surrendered. Armadora relies on the addendum’s incorporation of the original contract, the lack of an express new formula, and its contention that the original agreement always governed.

**Open application and paths:** The court may preserve the district court’s interpretation of the original agreement yet find that later conduct prevents reliance on it for this refinancing. If the necessary facts are established, direct application of the relied-upon formula. If material findings are missing or conflicting, remand rather than supply them. Estoppel cannot rest solely on a conclusion that GECC deserved a better bargain.

### Issue 2: Relief and fees

**Review and constraints:** Entitlement follows the contract and established estoppel; the resulting calculation must use supported inputs. Contractual fees require reconsideration if the recovery changes. A later reservation of litigation positions does not necessarily undo reliance already induced before consent. ([Law Resource](https://law.resource.org/pub/us/case/reporter/F3/037/37.F3d.41.93-9282.1573.html))

**Positions and paths:** GECC seeks the additional special-interest payment and recalculated fees. Armadora seeks preservation of the original judgment. The mandate should identify the controlling calculation and remand numerical and fee matters not conclusively established, rather than invent a final total.
