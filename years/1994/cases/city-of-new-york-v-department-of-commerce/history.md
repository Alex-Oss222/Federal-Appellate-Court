# City of New York v. United States Department of Commerce

**Case:** City of New York v. United States Department of Commerce, docket 93-6183

**Historical event and date:** Panel disposition on 1994-08-08; 34 F.3d 1114 (2d Cir. 1994). Only the selected event is described.

**Historical disposition:** Vacated the judgment dismissing the census challenge and remanded.

**Historical panel:** William H. Timbers, Amalya L. Kearse, Pierre N. Leval.

**Opinion author and joins:** Amalya L. Kearse wrote the opinion. Joined by Leval.

**Historical holding and grounds:** The majority required more searching constitutional justification for the refusal to correct a known differential undercount affecting representational equality. On the record it required the government to justify the refusal as essential to a legitimate governmental objective rather than treating ordinary arbitrary-and-capricious review as sufficient. The remand left the district court to apply that framework; it was not an appellate calculation of new census totals.

**Separate opinions:** Timbers dissented. He would have affirmed, emphasizing the Secretary’s discretion, distributive accuracy, statistical uncertainty, and the limits of judicial substitution for the responsible census officials.
