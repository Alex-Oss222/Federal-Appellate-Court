## Supreme Court merits decision, if any

*Wisconsin v. City of New York*, 517 U.S. 1 (1996), consolidated with Nos. 94-1631 and 94-1985; argued 1996-01-10, decided 1996-03-20. The Court states no formal question presented; in substance it asked what standard of review governs the Secretary of Commerce's decision not to use a postenumeration-survey adjustment of the 1990 census, and whether the decision met constitutional and statutory limits. Holding: heightened scrutiny drawn from Wesberry and the districting cases does not apply; the *Department of Commerce v. Montana* and *Franklin v. Massachusetts* standard governs; the decision bore a reasonable relationship to an actual enumeration and was within constitutional discretion. Effect: the Second Circuit's vacatur and remand was reversed. The opinion contains no remand instruction (verified); that the district court's judgment for the Secretary (822 F. Supp. 906) thereby stands is inferred from the disposition.

## Dependence on the historical judgment

- **Certiorari petitions (Wisconsin, Oklahoma, United States):** sought reversal of the holding that heightened scrutiny applies and that the government must show non-adjustment essential to a legitimate objective. They depended on the vacatur and remand against the Secretary and intervenor States, the heightened-scrutiny standard, and the conflict with the Sixth and Seventh Circuits noted in Judge Timbers's dissent. A Second Circuit judgment affirming for the Secretary would leave these petitioners without an adverse judgment; any petition would then come from the plaintiffs, which did not occur historically.
- **Grant, divided argument and decision:** attach only to a judgment resting on the same standard-of-review ruling. The decision's effect as general law is independent: heightened scrutiny of the Secretary's census decision is unavailable after 1996-03-20.
