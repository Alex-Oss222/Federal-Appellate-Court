## Supreme Court merits decision, if any

*Wisconsin v. City of New York*, 517 U.S. 1 (1996); argued 1996-01-10, decided 1996-03-20. The Court states no formal question; in substance, what standard of review governs the Secretary of Commerce's decision not to apply a postenumeration-survey adjustment to the 1990 census, and whether the decision met constitutional and statutory limits. Holding: the heightened scrutiny drawn from Wesberry and the districting cases does not apply; the *Department of Commerce v. Montana* and *Franklin v. Massachusetts* standard governs, and the decision bore a reasonable relationship to an actual enumeration and was within constitutional discretion. Effect: the Second Circuit's vacatur and remand was reversed. The opinion has no remand instruction (verified); that the district court's judgment for the Secretary, 822 F. Supp. 906, thereby stands is inferred from the disposition.

## Dependence on the historical judgment

- **Petitions (Wisconsin, Oklahoma, United States):** sought reversal of the holding that heightened scrutiny applies and that the government must show non-adjustment essential to a legitimate objective. They depended on the vacatur and remand against the Secretary and intervenor States, on the heightened-scrutiny standard, and on the conflict with the Sixth and Seventh Circuits noted in Judge Timbers's dissent. A Second Circuit judgment affirming for the Secretary would leave these parties without an adverse judgment; any petition would come from plaintiffs, which did not occur historically.
- **Grant, divided argument, decision:** attach only to a judgment resting on the same standard-of-review ruling. As general law, the decision applies independently from 1996-03-20: heightened scrutiny of the Secretary's census decision is unavailable.
