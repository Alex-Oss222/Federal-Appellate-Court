## Historical comparison

History (34 F.3d 1114, event date 1994-08-08) vacated the judgment dismissing the census challenge and remanded. Kearse wrote the opinion, joined by Leval. Timbers dissented and would have affirmed, stressing the Secretary's discretion, distributive accuracy, statistical uncertainty, and the limits of judicial substitution. The majority required a more searching constitutional justification for refusing to correct a known differential undercount affecting representational equality. On the record it required the government to justify the refusal as essential to a legitimate governmental objective, not merely to survive arbitrary-and-capricious review. The remand left the district court to apply that framework. There was no appellate calculation of totals.

Premises that survive: the record, the 1994-01-05 argument, the 1994-08-08 legal cutoff, the party positions, and both retained judges are unchanged. The one changed premise is that the historical second majority vote (Leval) is now a seat held by a third judge about whom this document says nothing. history.md is silent on standing, 13 U.S.C. §§ 141 and 195, the *Franklin* limits on relief against the President, and the form of any remedy beyond remand to the district court. On those components history supplies no evidence and the commitments govern unchanged.

## William H. Timbers

**Reconciled disposition:** Affirm the dismissal on Issue 1. On Issue 2, no statutory command to adjust; affirm. Threshold as committed: merits reached for the governmental plaintiffs with funding and representation injuries, party-specific proof required for any other claimant and each form of relief.

**Ground:** As committed. Constitutional standard reviewed de novo, findings for clear error. The Secretary's good-faith technical judgment receives substantial but reviewable deference. Distributive accuracy and uncertainty in local estimates, with an evenly divided advisory panel, sustain the decision. The district court's finding of rational guidelines after a thirteen-day trial is not clearly erroneous. The court does not choose the better estimator. No *Karcher* burden shift to enumeration, and no racial purpose inferred from disparity. Section 195 is not decided as to whether it forbids adjustment for apportionment.

**Remedy:** None on affirmance. The conditional remedy limits stand: remand to the Secretary rather than judicial substitution of figures, limited to an identified census use, lawful defendant, and claimant with proven injury, no order at the President, attention to absent States and localities.

**Join limits:** All five stand (no *Karcher* burden shift, no racial purpose from disparity, no national adjustment or order against the President, no nonjusticiability holding contrary to *Carey*, no holding that § 195 prohibits adjustment). As I read history.md, the majority's "essential to a legitimate governmental objective" formulation is the kind of rationale limit (a) excludes. That is an inference, because history.md does not name *Karcher*.

**Basis for any departure from the commitment or from history:** None. The commitment and history agree: he dissented and would have affirmed. History also shows he did not take his commitment's stated alternative (vacatur and remand for an express standard). No changed premise bears on his position, and the panel substitution alters no legal, factual, or remedial premise he applied.

## Amalya L. Kearse

**Reconciled disposition:** Vacate the judgment of dismissal and remand on Issue 1. This departs from her committed disposition (affirm). On Issue 2, no statutory mandate to adjust, as committed. Threshold as committed: standing resolved plaintiff by plaintiff on the existing record, no credit for proof first offered on appeal, merits holding running to claimants with record-proven injury.

**Ground:** The Article I, § 2 claim requires a more searching justification than ordinary arbitrary-and-capricious review. The government must justify the refusal to correct the known differential undercount as essential to a legitimate governmental objective. A finding that the Secretary's choices were rational is therefore not sufficient to dispose of the constitutional claim. Her committed test (legitimate, genuinely held, record-supported reasons that engage the constitutional task) is retained but applied at the higher level. The legal standard is reviewed de novo, and the remand sends the application to the district court, so no finding is reversed as clearly erroneous and no estimator is chosen. Racial purpose is not inferred from disparity. The statutory component is unchanged: § 141(a) permits sampling and surveys, § 195 creates no command to adjust, and she need not decide whether the exception prohibits adjustment.

**Remedy:** Remand to the district court to apply the framework, with entitlement tailored by census use and claimant and individual issues left to the trial court. No appellate factfinding or adjusted totals, no order against the President (*Franklin*), no nationwide redistribution without the affected parties (*Carey*, 1981).

**Join limits:** Committed limits (a) through (d) stand (no appellate factfinding or estimator choice, no racial purpose from disparity, no relief without record proof of injury, no nonjusticiability contrary to *Carey*). Limit (e) is narrowed. She no longer bars a justification requirement for census nonadjustment, because history shows she wrote one. She still will not join a rationale that asserts such a requirement without explaining why it applies to enumeration. She would not join an affirmance that treats arbitrary-and-capricious review as sufficient.

**Basis for any departure from the commitment or from history:** No changed premise explains this difference. The panel substitution affects who casts the second majority vote, not her own reading of the law or record. The departure follows the commitment's own stated weakness: no census or apportionment writing of hers was found, so it was an analogy from standing, deference, and equal-protection opinions. History is her authored opinion on this very appeal, on the same record, argument date, and legal cutoff, with the same dissenting colleague. That is much stronger evidence than the analogy. The commitment's alternative (vacatur if the district court treated administrative reasonableness as conclusive) points the same way, though history is more specific.
