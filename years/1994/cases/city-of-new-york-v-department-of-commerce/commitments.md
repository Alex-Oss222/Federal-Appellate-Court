## William H. Timbers (Senior Circuit Judge)

**Disposition:** Issue 1 (constitutional claim): affirm the dismissal. Issue 2 (statutory): no statutory command to adjust; affirm. Threshold: reaches the merits for the governmental plaintiffs with funding and representation injuries (justiciability is settled in this circuit by *Carey*), and requires party-specific proof for any other claimant and for each form of relief. Whether 13 U.S.C. § 195 forbids adjustment for apportionment is not decided, because the Secretary's discretion disposes of the claim without it.

**Ground:** The governing constitutional standard is reviewed de novo and the bench-trial findings for clear error under Fed. R. Civ. P. 52(a). The Constitution constrains the census but does not demand mathematical perfection or equal accuracy everywhere. The Secretary's good-faith, reasoned technical judgment receives substantial but reviewable deference, by analogy to *Montana* (far greater deference outside the districting setting, with relative and absolute measures of inequality able to diverge) and *Franklin* (the Secretary's census judgment tested against constitutional text and the equal-representation goal, not left unreviewable). The *Karcher* justification burden is not carried over to enumeration, because no holding extends it. Unequal undercount alone does not show racial purpose (*Washington v. Davis*; *Arlington Heights*). For non-apportionment uses, review is under 5 U.S.C. § 706(2)(A) and *State Farm* (relevant factors, rational connection between facts and choice). He tests the explanation rather than accepting it, so the acknowledged probable national accuracy gain must be answered by the Secretary's stated reasons. The independent sufficient reasons are distributive accuracy and uncertainty in local estimates, with an evenly divided advisory panel. The district court's finding after a thirteen-day trial that the guidelines were rationally chosen and applied is not shown to be clearly erroneous. The court does not select the better estimator. The future-census rationale is not needed to sustain the decision. 13 U.S.C. § 141(a) permits sampling and surveys, and § 195 says the Secretary "shall, if he considers it feasible" authorize sampling, so neither compels adjustment where the Secretary reasonably declines.

**Remedy:** None on affirmance. If a defect were found, remand to the Secretary for a reasoned decision, not judicial substitution of adjusted figures, limited to the identified census use, a lawful defendant, and a claimant with proven injury, with no order directed at the President (*Franklin*) and attention to absent affected States and localities (*Carey*, 1981).

**Alternative that matters:** Vacatur and remand for an express constitutional standard, if the district opinion treated APA reasonableness as exhausting the constitutional inquiry. His own writing shows willingness to vacate when a lower court used the wrong framework, but on a fully tried record he more likely states the standard himself.

**Sources:** *Bersani v. ## Amalya L. Kearse (Active Circuit Judge)

**Disposition:** Issue 1: affirm the dismissal. Issue 2: no statutory mandate to adjust; affirm. Threshold: standing is resolved plaintiff by plaintiff on the existing record. The merits holding runs to claimants with record-proven concrete injury, which on this packet means the governmental plaintiffs' funding and representation injuries as in *Carey*, and no one is credited with proof offered for the first time on appeal.

**Ground:** Legal standard de novo; discriminatory purpose is a fact reviewed for clear error and equal protection requires purpose as well as impact (*Yonkers*, applying *Washington v. Davis* and *Arlington Heights*), so differential undercount does not itself show racial purpose. The Article I, § 2 claim is judged by whether the Secretary's actual rationale is legitimate, genuinely held, supported by the record, and engages the constitutional task. *Soto-Lopez* shows she tests each asserted purpose for fit instead of treating a stated rationale as conclusive. *Montana* (relative versus absolute deviation; the districting standard does not govern enumeration) shows that distributive accuracy is a cognizable constitutional concern and that greater aggregate accuracy does not automatically control. *Britt* supplies the APA frame for non-apportionment uses: considerable deference to discretion, no substitution of the court's judgment, and a hard look that sets aside only an arbitrary or capricious choice. On this record the Secretary's reasons, tried for thirteen days and found rational by the district court, do not show clear error or an arbitrary choice, even though adjustment probably improved accuracy for most people. Statute: § 141(a) expressly permits sampling and surveys, and § 195 withdraws only a direction to use sampling for apportionment, so the text creates no command to adjust and leaves feasibility to the Secretary; she need not decide whether the exception prohibits adjustment.

**Remedy:** None on affirmance. If a defect were found, remand for relief tailored by census use and claimant, with individual entitlement left to the trial court (the structure used in *Soto-Lopez*), no appellate factfinding, no order against the President (*Franklin*), and no nationwide redistribution without the affected parties (*Carey*, 1981).

**Alternative that matters:** Vacatur and remand for an independent constitutional determination only if the district opinion treated administrative reasonableness as conclusive; no writing of hers resolves how she reads that opinion.

**Sources:** *Sierra Club v. SCM Corp.*, 747 F.2d 99 (2d Cir. 1984) (Kearse, J.; concrete injury to the plaintiff or members required, no standing on a general interest, no supplementing the showing on appeal). *Britt v. U.S. Army Corps of Engineers*, 769 F.2d 84 (2d Cir. 1985) (Kearse, J.; Cardamone, J., dissenting; APA deference and limits). *Soto-Lopez v. N.Y.C. Civil Service Comm'n*, 755 F.2d 266 (2d Cir. 1985) (Kearse, J.; claim-by-claim rational-basis scrutiny, tailored relief with individual issues remanded). *United States v. Yonkers Board of Education*, 837 F.2d 1181 (2d Cir. 1987) (Kearse, J.; intent as a fact reviewed for clear error, impact plus purpose). Common authorities as listed for the other panelist. No census or apportionment writing by this judge was found.

**Join limits:** Will not join a rationale that (a) rests on appellate factfinding or the choice of a statistical method, (b) infers racial purpose from disparity, (c) grants relief to a claimant without record proof of injury, (d) treats the claim as nonjusticiable contrary to *Carey*, or (e) transfers the *Karcher* burden to enumeration without authority. Routinely joins a narrow decision resting on the Secretary's actual reasons and the trial findings.

## Open points and source gaps

- Neither judge has a census, apportionment, or redistricting writing in the sources found; both models reason by analogy from deference, standing, equal protection, and remedy opinions plus circuit and Supreme Court law.
- The constitutional deference owed to the Secretary (as opposed to Congress in *Montana*) and whether APA reasonableness exhausts the constitutional inquiry are unresolved by the supplied authorities and by both judges' writings.
- Fact-bound uncertainty: whether the decision holds if the future-census rationale is discounted, given the Secretary's acknowledged probable gains for about two-thirds of the population.
- Per-plaintiff standing proof, final-judgment date, and appellate timeliness are not in the packet and were not verified.
- Pinpoints for Supreme Court cases are those in neutral.md; the Cornell pages used show no reporter pagination, so they were not independently confirmed. No pinpoints are given for circuit cases. Circuit-case authorship and holdings were taken from static.case.law text; legislative history of the 1976 Census Act amendments was not examined (unverified).
- CourtListener rate-limiting cut short the searches. Every search was limited to filings before the cutoff, and none returned a decision on or after it. *City of Detroit v. Franklin* describes the district-court ruling in this case; only its separate standing and justiciability holdings were used.
