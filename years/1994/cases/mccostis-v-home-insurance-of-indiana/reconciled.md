## Historical comparison

The panel disposed of the case on 1994-08-08 (31 F.3d 110). It vacated the judgment for Home and remanded. Judge Walker wrote the opinion and Judge Pratt joined. Judge Motley, the historical third-seat occupant, dissented briefly and would have affirmed for the district court's reasons. The two judges modeled here made up the historical majority, and it was complete without the third seat.

As described in history.md, the exclusion for return or restitution of legal fees admitted more than one reasonable reading in this overbilling arrangement. The demand sought amounts beyond what McCostis personally received and was framed through broader liability theories. The court required extrinsic evidence of intended meaning, then construction against the insurer if that evidence did not resolve the meaning. It did not award indemnity or decide settlement allocation. The mandate date is unknown.

The historical outcome depended on the same parties, policy, complaint, summary-judgment posture, argument date (1994-05-02), New York law, and de novo review that this simulation preserves. The one changed premise is the occupant of the third seat. It matters to a retained judge only if that judge's vote or reasoning depended on that seat, and nothing in history or the commitments shows that it did. The commitments were formed without history, so their match with the historical lineup is independent corroboration.

Differences between commitments and history:

## George C. Pratt

**Reconciled disposition:** Vacate the summary judgment declaring that Home owes no defense, and remand. No ruling on indemnity or settlement allocation. Diversity is a threshold check, with 28 U.S.C. § 1653 available to cure any defective allegation.

**Ground:** Review is de novo and New York law governs. Ambiguity is a question of law. A defense is owed where the complaint potentially falls within coverage, and Home bears the burden on an exclusion. "Return or restitution of legal fees" can reasonably mean giving back what the insured received, and Home's economic-substance reading is also reasonable. Home therefore did not show on summary judgment that every demand is excluded, particularly the demand measured by sums others received. The order is text, then extrinsic evidence, then construction against Home as a last resort.

**Remedy:** Vacate and remand for the extrinsic-evidence inquiry. Construe against Home only if that inquiry does not resolve the ambiguity. Whether Home ultimately owes a defense is left to the remand. No direction to enter a declaration now.

**Join limits:** Unchanged. He would not join a rationale that finds ambiguity merely from tort labels, or one that holds the exclusion categorically inapplicable to racketeering, fraud or negligence counts. He would not import New Jersey reasonable-expectations doctrine or join any holding on indemnity. Either remedy branch is joinable if the legal ground is the same.

**Basis for any departure from the commitment or from history:**
- *Remedy fork (resolved, not departed from).* The commitment made the branch depend on a record fact the packet did not supply, and said either branch is joinable on the same ground. History took the remand branch on the same record, and no premise that matters to him has changed. History is strong evidence on which branch the record supports, so it is adopted. This adopts the remedy only; history.md does not say whether Home had submitted intent evidence or the evidence was simply undeveloped.
- *"Reverse" becomes "vacate."* This follows from the remand branch and is a matter of form.
- *Tort-label limit (kept).* History's ambiguity ground rests on amounts beyond McCostis's receipts together with the broader theories. That is consistent with his limit if the opinion rests on the amounts and the text, and would conflict with it if the theories were an independent ground.
- *Unaddressed components (kept).* His view that the $335,000 portion is likely excluded, at least for indemnity, is a non-operative observation that history neither confirms nor contradicts. It is not a join condition. His diversity check also stands.

## John M. Walker, Jr.

**Reconciled disposition:** Vacate the summary judgment for Home and remand. No ruling on indemnity. Diversity is a threshold matter, with § 1653 available for any curable pleading defect.

**Ground:** Ambiguity is a threshold question of law reviewed de novo. The text "return or restitution" does not unambiguously reach liability for money others retained, and Home's alternative reading is also plausible. The exclusion is reasonably susceptible to two readings, and Home has not met its "no other reasonable interpretation" burden. The sequence is textual analysis, then extrinsic evidence, then contra proferentem only as a last resort. The judgment does not rest on construction against the drafter alone.

**Remedy:** Vacate and remand so the parties may offer extrinsic evidence of intended meaning. Construe against Home only if that evidence does not resolve the ambiguity. No outright declaration at this stage.

**Join limits:** Unchanged. He would not join a rationale that construes against Home without first addressing extrinsic evidence. He would not join an outright declaration unless the opinion finds no non-conclusory intent evidence in the record. He would not join a holding that the exclusion cannot reach fee restitution pleaded as tort, or any ruling on indemnity.

**Basis for any departure from the commitment or from history:**
- *Remedy fork (resolved, not departed from).* His commitment directs vacatur and remand where intent evidence is undeveloped or contested. History's remedy matches that branch, and the record premise is unchanged.
- *"Reverse" becomes "vacate."* This is a matter of form.
- *Unaddressed components (kept).* His $335,000 observation and diversity handling stand as stated.
- *Authorship (not decided here).* History shows Walker as author, and his authored ambiguity-sequence opinions fit that role. Authorship belongs to assembly and is not fixed at this stage.

## Agreement between the two modeled judges (by component)

- **Disposition:** Agree. Both vacate the no-defense summary judgment and remand, and both leave indemnity and settlement allocation undecided.
- **Sufficient ground:** Agree on the operative proposition. The exclusion is reasonably susceptible to more than one reading as applied to demands measured by sums others received, and Home did not carry its burden on summary judgment. They differ only in emphasis: Pratt stresses Home's burden and the two reasonable readings, and Walker stresses plain text before extrinsic evidence. These are compatible.
- **Remedy:** Agree after reconciliation. Both take vacatur, an extrinsic-evidence inquiry, and construction against Home only if that inquiry fails. Walker's narrower limit means a direct-declaration remedy would have only Pratt's vote, so the remand branch is the only one both would join.
- **Join limits:** Compatible. A rationale that grounds ambiguity in the text and in sums others received, takes extrinsic evidence first, does not make tort labels alone decisive, does not hold the exclusion categorically inapplicable to tort-pleaded fee restitution, and says nothing on indemnity satisfies both.
- **Threshold (diversity):** Agree. It is checked first, with § 1653 as the cure.

The two reconciled positions agree on every component, so two of the three panel votes exist for a vacate-and-remand opinion on that ground, provided the opinion's text stays within both sets of limits. The third seat is not modeled here.
