# Commitments: Richard M. McCostis, Esq. v. Home Insurance Company of Indiana (No. 93-9283)

**Stage:** neutral modeling. **Legal cutoff:** 1994-08-08. **Inputs:** neutral.md, ENGINE.md, and the public sources cited below.

## George C. Pratt (Senior Circuit Judge; senior status since May 22, 1993)

**Disposition:** Reverse the summary judgment declaring that Home owes no defense. By component: (1) the demand for the roughly $335,000 McCostis allegedly received is the core of the fee-return exclusion and is likely excluded, at least for indemnity; (2) the demand for the more than $420,000 allegedly received by Fox and his designees, and tort or racketeering damages not confined to restoring the insured's own receipts, is not shown to fall within the exclusion; (3) because Home can escape the defense only by showing that every allegation is excluded, the duty to defend the action survives; (4) indemnity and settlement allocation are not decided. Threshold: he would confirm diversity before the merits. The packet shows no citizenship allegations, and 28 U.S.C. § 1653 allows defective allegations to be cured on appeal, so a defect would be remedied rather than treated as fatal.

**Ground:** De novo review of summary judgment and of ambiguity as a question of law; New York law governs. A defense is owed where the complaint potentially falls within coverage (*Colon*; *Villa Charlotte Bronte*). Home carries the burden on an exclusion: clear and unmistakable language, no other reasonable interpretation, and application to the particular case (*Seaboard Surety*; *Blank*). "Return or restitution of legal fees" can reasonably mean giving back what the insured received, and Home's "economic substance" reading is also reasonable, so the clause is reasonably susceptible to more than one reading, which Home cannot overcome on summary judgment (*Schering*; *Waterfront*, standard as stated there). In *Entron* he stressed that an insurer wanting to exclude a category could have said so explicitly; that was New Jersey law and reasonable-expectations doctrine, so here it is at most a drafting consideration at the last-resort stage.

**Remedy:** Follow the New York sequence (*State v. Home Indemnity*). If the record holds no non-conclusory extrinsic evidence that Home's reading was the shared intent, remand with direction to enter a declaration that Home must defend. If such evidence exists, vacate and remand for the intent determination, with construction against Home if it fails to resolve the ambiguity. Which branch applies turns on a record fact the packet does not supply.

**Sources:** *Entron, Inc. v. Affiliated FM Insurance Co.*, 749 F.2d 127 (2d Cir. 1984) (Pratt, J., author; New Jersey law; policy ambiguous, liberal construction for insured). *Cam-Ful Industries, Inc. v. Fidelity & Deposit Co. of Maryland*, 922 F.2d 156 (2d Cir. 1991) (Pratt, J., author; New York suretyship contract construed most favorably to claimant; bench trial, not insurance). Joined, not authored: *Schering Corp. v. Home Insurance Co.*, 712 F.2d 4 (2d Cir. 1983) (Kaufman, J.; New York law; summary judgment reversed where language reasonably susceptible to two meanings, extrinsic evidence of intent allowed, contra proferentem a last resort); *United National Insurance Co. v. Waterfront New York Realty Corp.*, 994 F.2d 105 (2d Cir. 1993) (Jacobs, J.; courts may not create an ambiguity; unambiguous exclusion enforced). Authorities: *Colon v. Aetna Life & Casualty Insurance Co.*, 66 N.Y.2d 6 (1985); *Villa Charlotte Bronte, Inc. v. Commercial Union Insurance Co.*, 64 N.Y.2d 846 (1985); *Seaboard Surety Co. v. Gillette Co.*, 64 N.Y.2d 304 (1984); *State v. Home Indemnity Co.*, 66 N.Y.2d 669 (1985); *State of New York v. Blank*, 27 F.3d 783 (2d Cir. June 13, 1994) (Oakes, J.); 28 U.S.C. §§ 1291, 1332, 1653, 2201.

**Join limits:** He would not join a rationale that finds ambiguity merely from tort labels or that holds the exclusion categorically inapplicable to racketeering, fraud or negligence counts; the *Waterfront* rule and Home's strongest point mean any demand that truly restores McCostis's own fees stays excluded. He would not import New Jersey reasonable-expectations doctrine. Either remedy branch is joinable if the legal ground is the same. He would not join any holding on indemnity.

## John M. Walker, Jr. (Active Circuit Judge)

**Disposition:** Reverse the summary judgment for Home on the same components as above: the $335,000 portion is likely within the exclusion; the portion measured by sums others received is not shown to be; Home therefore has not established that the entire action is excluded, and the defense duty is not negated. Indemnity is not decided. He would treat diversity as a threshold matter and use § 1653 for any curable pleading defect.

**Ground:** Ambiguity is a threshold question of law reviewed de novo (*U.S. Fire v. General Reinsurance*). Applying plain meaning, the exclusion's text speaks of "return or restitution," which does not unambiguously reach liability for money others retained, and Home's alternative reading is also plausible; the exclusion is therefore reasonably susceptible to two readings and Home has not met its "no other reasonable interpretation" burden. His sequence is textual analysis, then extrinsic evidence, with contra proferentem only as a last resort (*U.S. Fire*; *State v. Home Indemnity*). He would not rest the judgment on construction against the drafter alone.

**Remedy:** Where the record already contains the decisive extrinsic evidence, he decides without remand (*U.S. Fire*); where the intent evidence has not been developed, he vacates and remands so the parties may offer it (*Commander Oil*). Applied here: if the record has no non-conclusory intent evidence for Home, direct a declaration of a defense obligation; if the evidence was not developed or is contested, vacate and remand for it, with construction against Home as the endpoint if it does not resolve the ambiguity. The packet does not show which branch the record supports.

**Sources:** *United States Fire Insurance Co. v. General Reinsurance Corp.*, 949 F.2d 569 (2d Cir. 1991) (Walker, J., author; New York law; ambiguity de novo, extrinsic evidence admitted despite integration, contra proferentem "last resort," judgment reversed without remand on the existing record). *Commander Oil Corp. v. Advance Food Service Equipment*, 991 F.2d 49 (2d Cir. 1993) (Walker, J., author; New York law; indemnity and defense language ambiguous as a matter of law; summary judgment vacated; remand for extrinsic evidence). Authorities as for Pratt, plus *Avondale Industries, Inc. v. Travelers Indemnity Co.*, 887 F.2d 1200 (2d Cir. 1989) (Cardamone, J.; heavy insurer burden on exclusions, duty to defend decided from complaint).

**Join limits:** He would not join a rationale that construes against Home without first addressing extrinsic evidence, and would not join an outright declaration unless the opinion finds that no non-conclusory intent evidence is in the record. He would not join a holding that the exclusion cannot reach fee restitution pleaded as tort, or any ruling on indemnity. Otherwise routine agreement on reversal.

## Open points and source gaps

- The decisive record fact is whether Home submitted non-conclusory extrinsic evidence of intent. The packet does not say. Each judge's remedy is stated as a fork on that fact, not as two independent paths.
- Neither judge has an authored opinion on a fee-return or restitution exclusion or a New York duty-to-defend dispute. Pratt's closest authored insurance opinion (*Entron*) applied New Jersey law, and *Cam-Ful* is a surety case. Walker's authored opinions concern the ambiguity sequence in insurance and indemnity contracts. Pratt's participation in *Schering* and *Waterfront* is inferred from panel membership and no noted separate writing.
- Pinpoints in neutral.md are not independently verified. The case.law texts have no page markers, so no pinpoint is given for any source here.
- *Ingersoll Milling Machine Co. v. M/V Bodena*, 829 F.2d 293 (2d Cir. 1987) (Pierce, J.), applied admiralty and Illinois law, not New York law, so it is weak support for the New York sequence; *State v. Home Indemnity* is the controlling source. *Breed*'s majority enforced the exclusion after finding no ambiguity; "narrowly construed" is better supported by *Seaboard Surety*, *Avondale* and *Blank*. *Matthews v. American Central Insurance Co.*, 154 N.Y. 449 (1897), supports construction in the insured's favor of a doubtful term.
- The "entire action" formulation does not appear verbatim in *Seaboard Surety*; *Blank* states, in substance, that the insurer must show all allegations excluded. That wording is verified only by a tool summary of the text, not by direct quotation.
- Diversity (citizenship of both parties), the exact judgment date and the full policy language are unverified. Whether RICO trebling is a "statutory penalty" under the damages definition is not briefed in the packet and is not modeled.
- CourtListener search was rate-limited (HTTP 429 with a long retry) late in research, so the survey of each judge's opinions is not exhaustive. No decision dated on or after the cutoff appeared in any result, and none was opened.
