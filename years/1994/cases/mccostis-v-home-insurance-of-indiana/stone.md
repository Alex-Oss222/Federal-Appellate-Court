## Stone's Position

### Component 1

**Component:** Fee-return exclusion and potential duty to defend

**Jurisdiction and reach:** The coverage dispute was decided on summary judgment. The pleaded overbilling is allegation, not an adjudicated admission by McCostis; indemnity and unbriefed estoppel theories are not automatically part of this appeal. The statutory route and unsettled jurisdictional predicates in the neutral baseline remain controlling; this proposal does not assume them away.

**Proposed disposition:** Vacate summary judgment for Home and remand.

**Governing law:** De novo review of summary judgment and policy ambiguity, with factual disputes and permissible inferences for the nonmovant. A defense is owed where the complaint potentially falls within coverage. Construe exclusions narrowly without rewriting clear text. If the fee-return language is ambiguous, examine relevant extrinsic evidence before applying construction against the insurer where uncertainty remains.

No directly controlling Supreme Court holding interprets this New York policy. Erie’s state-substantive-law obligation supplies the federal/state allocation, not a replacement coverage rule. Colon v. Aetna Life & Casualty Insurance Co., 66 N.Y.2d 6, 8-9 (1985); Villa Charlotte Bronte, Inc. v. Commercial Union Insurance Co., 64 N.Y.2d 846, 848 (1985); Breed v. Insurance Co. of North America, 46 N.Y.2d 351, 353 (1978); State v. Home Indemnity Co., 66 N.Y.2d 669, 671 (1985), binding New York authority. Matthews v. American Central Insurance Co., 154 N.Y. 449, 456-57 (1897); Ingersoll Milling Machine Co. v. M/V Bodena, 829 F.2d 293, 306 (2d Cir. 1987).

**Legal relationship:** Applying binding law.

**Proposed holding:** The fee-return exclusion is ambiguous as applied to alleged compensatory liability for amounts received by other participants; New York’s interpretive sequence governs the defense question.

**Legal reasoning:** Stone should propose vacatur of summary judgment and remand. The exclusion cannot conclusively be applied to every liability alleged in Winston's complaint without resolving whether it reaches amounts the insured never received. The court should state the ambiguity concretely: does this policy exclude every compensatory judgment traceable to an improperly billed fee, or only the insured's restoration of fees and expenses? Both readings can be explained from the language. The distinction is not a judicial license to insure dishonest profit; it concerns the scope of the insurer's promise to defend against a complaint alleging several kinds of liability.

The district court should consider relevant extrinsic evidence of contractual intent, if available and admissible. If it establishes the parties' meaning without genuine factual dispute, the court may decide accordingly. If uncertainty remains, New York's insurer-against-drafter rule applies, together with the rule that a potentially covered theory triggers the defense obligation. The mandate should not direct findings about McCostis's actual participation in the fraud or about who ultimately retained particular amounts. Those belong to the underlying litigation unless a distinct coverage issue properly requires them.

Stone should expressly answer the argument that artful pleading cannot create coverage. That is correct but insufficient. The potential coverage here rests on the alleged allocation of money and the breadth of the damages demand, not merely on the complaint's labels. Conversely, the inclusion of negligence in one count does not automatically require a defense if every fact alleged falls wholly within an exclusion correctly construed. The task is to compare allegations and contract, not count causes of action.

The ultimate indemnity question remains open. A defense promise covering fraudulent allegations can operate before a finding of intentional wrongdoing without resolving whether public policy or another policy term would bar payment of a particular adjudicated liability. Home's initial undertaking and later withdrawal should not be turned into an unbriefed waiver or estoppel holding. The record identified in the opinion supports a contract-interpretation remand; it does not supply every element of those additional theories.

This is an ordinary state-law insurance dispute. Stone's concern with fair process reinforces attention to the defense promise but does not displace New York contract law or diminish either party's property rights.

**Independent or alternative considerations:** No additional rule is proposed beyond the interpretation and application stated here.

**Preferred scope:** The fee-return exclusion is ambiguous as applied to alleged compensatory liability for amounts received by other participants; New York’s interpretive sequence governs the defense question. The lawful outer boundary is the issue and remedial limit stated here, not a broader rule about unpresented facts.

Case-level proposed scope: the fee-return language is ambiguous as applied to liability for sums received by other participants, requiring the New York sequence of extrinsic evidence followed, if needed, by construction against the insurer.

**Fallback position:** Proposed, not approved: a remand confined to the potential coverage of the amounts beyond McCostis's alleged receipts, without defining every form of restitution or deciding indemnity. Stone could join a defense declaration after the relevant uncertainty is resolved against Home under the governing rules; that is a conditional legal consequence, not an approved present judgment.

Case-level nonjoin limits, also proposed: treating all allegations as proved, equating a defense obligation with indemnification of fraud, ordering coverage solely from a negligence label, or using generalized fairness to rewrite an unambiguous exclusion.

**Remedy and remand:** Examine admissible intent evidence, resolve any necessary factual dispute, and apply the defense promise and construction against the insurer if ambiguity remains.

**Reserved questions:** Indemnity of an adjudicated fraud, truth of the allegations, waiver or estoppel from the initial defense, and other unpresented exclusions.
