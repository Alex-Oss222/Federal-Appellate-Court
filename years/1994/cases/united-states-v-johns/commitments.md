## Frank X. Altimari (Active Circuit Judge)

**Disposition:** Issue 1 (obstruction, U.S.S.G. § 3C1.1): vacate the two-level adjustment to the extent it rests on Johns's presentence denials of his own alleged transactions, which fall within Application Note 1's exception for denial of guilt and refusal to admit guilt or provide information to a probation officer. Issue 2 (acceptance, § 3E1.1): vacate the denial of credit insofar as it rested on the same obstruction rationale or on conduct outside the offense of conviction, and remand for an independent determination. The fifty-one month sentence is vacated and the case remanded for resentencing.

**Ground:** Whether the commentary permits the enhancement is a question of guideline interpretation, reviewed de novo (Perdomo; Hudson). Under Stinson the commentary is authoritative unless it violates the Constitution or a statute or is inconsistent with or a plainly erroneous reading of the guideline, so the specific exception cannot be read away by the general example of material falsehoods to a probation officer. The two provisions are read together: the exception controls statements that are, in substance, denials of the defendant's own alleged misconduct, even when phrased as full sentences, while fabrication, false accusations against others, perjury and lies about collateral sentencing facts remain within the general example. The ground is textual, not constitutional. Rodriguez (false statements about prior convictions) is distinguishable because criminal history is not a denial of the misconduct being sentenced. Falsity and materiality are accepted premises, so they do not decide the issue. For acceptance, the district court's finding gets deference only if it rests on a correct legal premise (Austin: no foundation where the denial rested on conduct outside the offense of conviction; Reyes: a candid and full account may be required, and rejecting obstruction does not compel credit). Whether the denied transactions lie inside the conspiracy of conviction is a record question for the district court.

**Remedy:** Vacate and remand for further sentencing proceedings under 18 U.S.C. § 3742(f)(1). No appellate finding on disputed sentencing facts, no instruction to award acceptance credit, and no ruling that obstruction cannot be found on remand if independent obstructive conduct is shown. The district court redetermines acceptance on the correct rule.

**Join limits:** Will not join a Fifth Amendment or right-to-lie rationale, which has no Supreme Court support and conflicts with the logic of Rodriguez. Will not join a reading that makes the probation-officer example inoperative for fabrication or collateral lies. Will not join a directive that forecloses obstruction on remand or awards credit outright. Routine agreement with a narrower textual vacatur and remand.

**Plausible alternative path:** Affirmance of the adjustment if the court read "guilt" in the exception to mean only the offense of conviction and treated denials of other transactions as collateral material falsehoods under the probation-officer example. The accepted premise that Johns denied his own alleged misconduct, and the exception's reference to information given to a probation officer, make this the weaker path on this record. It matters only if the record shows the denials were fabrications or reached beyond Johns's own conduct.

## Joseph M. McLaughlin (Active Circuit Judge)

**Disposition:** Issue 1: vacate the obstruction adjustment as imposed, because it treated denials within Application Note 1 as ordinary material falsehoods. Issue 2: vacate the denial of acceptance credit and remand for independent determination under § 3E1.1. Sentence vacated and remanded for resentencing.

**Ground:** Guideline interpretation is reviewed de novo (Perdomo). In Cunavelis he vacated an automatic § 3C1.1 enhancement and required an independent determination at sentencing under the guideline's own standard, with the commentary's direction that defendant statements be evaluated favorably to the defendant. The same method yields here: the commentary's specific exception limits the general probation-officer example, and the district court must find willful obstructive conduct beyond the protected denials. In Acosta he read the guideline term by its text, its commentary cross-reference and its function, and rejected a literal expansion that the guideline's purpose did not support, which is consistent with harmonizing the notes rather than letting the general example swallow the exception. Stinson makes the commentary authoritative. Rodriguez and Stroud remain binding but are distinguishable. For acceptance, the inquiry is separate: obstruction and acceptance are not interchangeable, removal of an obstruction adjustment does not compel credit (Reyes), and a defendant need not volunteer conduct beyond the offense of conviction (Austin). The denial of credit is reviewed with deference to supported findings, but not where it rests on the rejected obstruction premise or on out-of-offense conduct.

**Remedy:** Vacatur and remand for resentencing, with direction that the district court make the findings the correct tests require. No appellate fact-finding, no automatic credit, no bar on a lawful obstruction finding supported by independent conduct. In Cunavelis the disposition was "vacate and remand for reconsideration of the upward adjustment."

**Sources:** United States v. Cunavelis, 969 F.2d 1419 (2d Cir. 1992) (McLaughlin, J.) (cites commentary note 1 on statements by the defendant). United States v. Acosta, 963 F.2d 551 (2d Cir. 1992) (McLaughlin, J.) (guideline text and commentary; reversed and remanded; Van Graafeiland, J., dissenting). United States v. Monk, 15 F.3d 25 (2d Cir. 1994) (McLaughlin, J.) (legal premise reviewed de novo; vacated and remanded for resentencing). Authorities as listed for Judge Altimari.

**Join limits:** Will not join a constitutional right-to-lie rationale (no Supreme Court holding supplies one). Will not join an automatic rule that any false statement to a probation officer is obstruction, which Cunavelis rejects in the analogous automatic-application setting. Will not join a rationale that removal of obstruction compels acceptance credit, or one that affirms the acceptance denial without confirming it rested on a basis independent of the rejected obstruction premise and within the offense of conviction. Routine agreement with a narrow textual vacatur and remand.
