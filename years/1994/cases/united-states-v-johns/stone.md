## Stone's Position

### Component 1

**Component:** Obstruction adjustment for presentence denial of alleged misconduct

**Jurisdiction and reach:** Replacement counsel presented the guideline question after initial Anders proceedings. Falsity and materiality are accepted premises for this appellate issue, not findings newly made here. The statutory route and unsettled jurisdictional predicates in the neutral baseline remain controlling; this proposal does not assume them away.

**Proposed disposition:** Vacate the sentence and remove the obstruction adjustment on the stated basis.

**Governing law:** De novo guideline interpretation; United States v. Perdomo, 927 F.2d 111, 118 (2d Cir. 1991). Reconcile the specific denial-of-guilt exception in § 3C1.1 Application Note 1 with the general example of material falsehoods to a probation officer. The Commission may provide protection beyond the minimum required by the Fifth Amendment.

Stinson v. United States, 508 U.S. 36, 38, 44-45 (1993), makes guideline commentary authoritative unless inconsistent with governing law or the guideline. No Supreme Court holding supplies a constitutional right to lie. U.S.S.G. § 3C1.1 and then-applicable notes; United States v. Austin, 17 F.3d 27, 31 (2d Cir. 1994); United States v. Rodriguez, 943 F.2d 215, 218 (2d Cir. 1991); United States v. Stroud, 893 F.2d 504, 507 (2d Cir. 1990).

**Legal relationship:** Applying binding law.

**Proposed holding:** The specific denial exception limits the general probation-officer-falsehood example; an unsworn denial does not lose that status merely because it uses a full sentence.

**Legal reasoning:** Stone should propose vacatur of the sentence and remand without the obstruction adjustment on the stated basis, together with reconsideration of acceptance of responsibility under the proper distinction between the offense of conviction and other alleged conduct.

The guideline's denial exception has to constrain something. Nearly every false denial during a presentence interview could be described as materially false information to the probation officer. If that description always controlled, the exception would vanish precisely where it is most likely to matter. The sound reading treats the enumerated example as subject to the express qualification, while preserving enhancement for independently obstructive conduct outside that qualification.

The government is correct that the Constitution does not authorize lying. Stone should say so. But that proposition answers a different question from whether the Commission has instructed courts to withhold this particular additional punishment for a defined class of unsworn denials. The mandatory Guidelines in 1994 must be enforced in the defendant's favor as well as the government's. Refusing to apply the commentary because its protection exceeds the constitutional minimum would substitute judicial policy for the governing sentencing source.

The line is administrable without making every interview lie immune. A defendant who merely denies his own alleged criminal conduct invokes the commentary's particular protection. A defendant who manufactures documents, falsely implicates an innocent third party, commits perjury, or conceals prior convictions engages in conduct whose obstructive character does not depend solely on denial of guilt. The opinion should not decide unpresented variations more broadly than needed, but it should identify why *Rodriguez* remains intact.

**Independent or alternative considerations:** No additional rule is proposed beyond the interpretation and application stated here.

**Preferred scope:** The specific denial exception limits the general probation-officer-falsehood example; an unsworn denial does not lose that status merely because it uses a full sentence. The lawful outer boundary is the issue and remedial limit stated here, not a broader rule about unpresented facts.

Case-level proposed scope: Note 1's denial protection limits Note 3(h); these unsworn denials cannot support the stated obstruction adjustment. Remand for recalculation and proper reconsideration of acceptance.

**Fallback position:** Proposed, not approved: confine the holding to denials of Johns's own alleged drug transactions and expressly reserve materially different affirmative deception.

Case-level nonjoin limits, also proposed: a constitutional right to lie, immunity for fabricated evidence or perjury, an automatic acceptance reduction, or treating all false information to probation as outside the commentary's express protection.

**Remedy and remand:** Recalculate the sentence without the stated obstruction basis while leaving other supported sentencing facts intact.

**Reserved questions:** Perjury, fabricated evidence, false accusation of another person, and criminal-history deception are not adjudicated as protected here.

A separate opinion would defend or delimit this proposition: The specific denial exception limits the general probation-officer-falsehood example; an unsworn denial does not lose that status merely because it uses a full sentence. ### Component 2

**Component:** Independent reconsideration of acceptance of responsibility

**Jurisdiction and reach:** The sentence denied § 3E1.1 credit; removal of the obstruction adjustment does not settle this separate ground. The statutory route and unsettled jurisdictional predicates in the neutral baseline remain controlling; this proposal does not assume them away.

**Proposed disposition:** Remand for independent reconsideration of acceptance.

**Governing law:** Apply the correct guideline interpretation independently, with appropriate deference to supported acceptance findings. The applicable version requires affirmative acceptance under its own terms. Distinguish the offense of conviction from other alleged conduct; failure of an obstruction adjustment neither proves nor disproves acceptance.

Stinson controls the authority of the relevant commentary; no Supreme Court holding requires automatic acceptance credit after removing obstruction. U.S.S.G. § 3E1.1; United States v. Reyes, 9 F.3d 275, 279 (2d Cir. 1993), binding within its scope.

**Legal relationship:** Applying binding law.

**Proposed holding:** Absence of punishable obstruction is not affirmative acceptance; the sentencing court must separately identify the relevant denied conduct and applicable § 3E1.1 criterion.

**Legal reasoning:** Acceptance of responsibility should then be reconsidered separately. A defendant may avoid an obstruction enhancement yet fail to demonstrate affirmative acceptance. The sentencing court must identify what Johns falsely denied and how that conduct relates to the offense of conviction under the guideline version governing his sentence. It cannot mechanically repeat the obstruction analysis or mechanically award the reduction because the enhancement was removed.
