## Historical comparison extract

Historically, on 1994-06-13 a Second Circuit panel of Judges Altimari, McLaughlin and Jacobs vacated Johns's 51-month sentence and remanded for resentencing. Judge Jacobs wrote the opinion, joined by Judge McLaughlin; Judge Altimari dissented, treating the statements as punishable deception rather than protected denial. The court held that the denial-of-guilt protection in the obstruction commentary covered the unsworn presentence denials despite their falsity and materiality, and that removing the obstruction basis required renewed, independent consideration of acceptance of responsibility without compelling credit. The simulated judgment is the same: vacatur and remand, a 2 to 1 vote, Altimari dissenting, and the same two holdings. The differences are in the panel and authorship only: Judge Stone-Khoury sat in place of Judge Jacobs, and Judge McLaughlin wrote with Judge Stone-Khoury joining. The judgment and remedy are unchanged, and no change of reasoning was found at the level the historical summary supplies. Source: United States v. Johns, 27 F.3d 31, 34-36 (2d Cir. 1994) (No. 92-1775).
