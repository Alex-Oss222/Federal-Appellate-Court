## Historical comparison

History records a panel disposition on 1994-06-13 that vacated the sentence and remanded for resentencing. Judge Jacobs wrote the opinion, joined by Judge McLaughlin. Judge Altimari dissented. The majority held that the denial-of-guilt protection in the obstruction commentary covered Johns's unsworn presentence denials despite their falsity and materiality. The remedy was resentencing without the rejected obstruction rationale, with acceptance reconsidered under the proper independent analysis.

## Joseph M. McLaughlin

**Reconciled disposition:** Commitment kept; it matches history. Issue 1: vacate the obstruction adjustment because it treated denials within Application Note 1 as ordinary material falsehoods. Issue 2: vacate the acceptance denial and remand for independent determination under § 3E1.1. Sentence vacated, case remanded for resentencing.

**Ground:** The adjustment rested on denials of guilt that the commentary's specific exception protects despite their falsity and materiality. The exception limits the general probation-officer example. Stinson makes the commentary authoritative; Rodriguez and Stroud are distinguishable (criminal-history deception is not denial of the offense being sentenced). Acceptance is separate: removing obstruction does not compel credit (Reyes), and the denial cannot stand on the rejected obstruction premise. The Austin ground (conduct outside the offense of conviction) stays contingent on the record; it is not needed for the vacatur and is not shown to be part of the historical ground.

**Remedy:** Vacatur and remand for resentencing, with the district court to apply the correct tests, reconsider acceptance independently, and make any findings those tests require. No appellate fact-finding, no automatic credit. History says "resentencing without the rejected obstruction rationale"; the commitment's further statement that a lawful obstruction finding on independent, unprotected conduct is not barred is consistent with that wording and is retained, but history does not confirm it.

**Join limits:** Retained: no constitutional right-to-lie rationale; no automatic rule that any false statement to a probation officer is obstruction; no rationale that removal of obstruction compels credit; no affirmance of the acceptance denial without confirming it rested on a basis independent of the rejected obstruction premise and within the offense of conviction. Routine agreement with a narrow textual vacatur and remand.

**Basis for any departure from the commitment or from history:** None. The vote, disposition and remedy agree with history. The commitment rests on Cunavelis, Acosta and Stinson-based harmonizing of the notes, and no changed premise touches that basis.
