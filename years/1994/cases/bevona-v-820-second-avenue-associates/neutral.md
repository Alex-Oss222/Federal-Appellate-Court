## Record and issues

The owner belonged to the Realty Advisory Board and was bound by a collective bargaining agreement. Perfect Building Maintenance supplied seven workers performing cleaning, freight-elevator, and security functions. The owner replaced Perfect with Quality Cleaning in October 1992; the new contractor retained none of those workers. The owner declined arbitration, and an ex parte award directed reinstatement. ([Justia Law](https://law.justia.com/cases/federal/appellate-courts/F3/27/37/541697/))

The agreement excluded contractor employees unless another provision brought them within coverage, while protecting work previously performed by covered employees. On reconsideration, the union identified a directly employed superintendent and handyman. The unrebutted evidence described their work as maintenance and repair rather than the cleaning work at issue. Their existence therefore did not necessarily establish the contractual predicate the union needed. ([Justia Law](https://law.justia.com/cases/federal/appellate-courts/F3/27/37/541697/))

### Issue 1: Agreement to arbitrate

**Review and governing law:** Arbitrability is a judicial question reviewed de novo unless the parties have made the requisite agreement to submit it elsewhere. *AT&T Technologies v. Communications Workers*, 475 U.S. 643 (1986), and *United Steelworkers v. Warrior & Gulf Navigation*, 363 U.S. 574 (1960), favor arbitration within the agreement but do not create agreement where the contract excludes the dispute. ([Justia Law](https://law.justia.com/cases/federal/appellate-courts/F3/27/37/541697/))

**Positions and open application:** The union relies on the broad arbitration clause and work-preservation provisions. The owner relies on the express contractor-employee exclusion and the absence of evidence that covered employees historically performed the same work. The question is contractual coverage, not whether retaining the workers would be fair.

**Paths and constraints:** Enforce the award only if the grievance falls within an actual contractual promise to arbitrate. Vacate if the exclusion controls and no applicable exception is established. The owner’s refusal to appear does not itself concede arbitrability.

### Issue 2: Reconsideration based on direct employees

**Review and burden:** Denial of reconsideration receives abuse-of-discretion review. The movant must show evidence material to the governing coverage question; mere novelty is insufficient.

**Positions:** The union argues that the owner’s direct employment of building-service personnel undermines the original premise. The owner answers that different job categories do not activate protection for the contractor’s cleaning workers.

**Paths:** Reconsider if the new evidence could materially establish covered work. Otherwise sustain the denial without resolving an abstract rule that direct employees and contractor employees can never perform overlapping protected work. No directly controlling Supreme Court decision resolves the particular job-category evidence. ([Justia Law](https://law.justia.com/cases/federal/appellate-courts/F3/27/37/541697/))
