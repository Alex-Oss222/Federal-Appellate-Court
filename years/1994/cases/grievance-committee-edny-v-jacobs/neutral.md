## Record and issues

State disciplinary proceedings concerned Jacobs’s representation of Patricia Warmhold, including overbilling, confessions of judgment, and an attempted limitation of malpractice liability. After multiple hearings, the referee sustained charges. The Appellate Division imposed a three-year suspension beginning April 12, 1993, also considering prior discipline. Jacobs had testified and presented evidence in the state proceeding. ([OpenJurist](https://openjurist.org/44/f3d/84/jacobs-grievance-committee-for-the-eastern-district-of-new-york-v-b-jacobs))

The federal court issued an order to show cause with the state opinion, appointed an advisory panel, and held conferences. Jacobs proposed witnesses including people involved in the state proceedings. The panel considered whether the testimony was already available in the record, could previously have been presented, or was material to reciprocal review. It recommended reciprocal discipline without a new evidentiary trial; the four-judge committee adopted that recommendation. ([OpenJurist](https://openjurist.org/44/f3d/84/jacobs-grievance-committee-for-the-eastern-district-of-new-york-v-b-jacobs))

## Law entering the event

**Cutoff:** December 23, 1994. **Simulation holdings:** None supplied.

### Issue 1: Independent reciprocal review

**Review and governing law:** The disciplinary disposition is reviewed for abuse of discretion, with legal and constitutional premises reviewed independently. *Selling v. Radford*, 243 U.S. 46 (1917), and *Theard v. United States*, 354 U.S. 278 (1957), require federal consideration rather than automatic adoption of state discipline. *In re Snyder*, 472 U.S. 634 (1985), addresses federal disciplinary authority. ([OpenJurist](https://openjurist.org/44/f3d/84/jacobs-grievance-committee-for-the-eastern-district-of-new-york-v-b-jacobs))

**Test and burden:** The local reciprocal-discipline rule required Jacobs to establish, by clear and convincing evidence, an infirmity of proof, a denial of notice or opportunity to be heard, or grave injustice warranting departure. This is not a second plenary trial of every state charge.

**Positions:** Jacobs argues that the state proceedings were constitutionally and evidentially defective. The committee relies on the hearings, record, and federal opportunity to identify the specific defects relevant under *Selling*.

**Paths:** Sustain reciprocal discipline if the federal process independently applies the required criteria and the claimed defects are not established. Remand if the committee treated the state judgment as irrebuttably conclusive or failed to address material evidence. Do not review an unappealed state injunction ruling through the disciplinary appeal.

### Issue 2: Notice and a second evidentiary hearing

**Governing law:** *Mullane v. Central Hanover Bank*, 339 U.S. 306 (1950), requires adequate notice. *Mathews v. Eldridge*, 424 U.S. 319 (1976), and *Dixon v. Love*, 431 U.S. 105 (1977), inform the assessment of the interest, risk of error, value of further procedure, and governmental burden. The local rule’s actual requirements must be applied rather than supplemented from assumption. ([OpenJurist](https://openjurist.org/44/f3d/84/jacobs-grievance-committee-for-the-eastern-district-of-new-york-v-b-jacobs))

**Positions:** Jacobs demands fuller notice and live testimony. The committee argues that the order to show cause, attached state decision, conferences, and existing record disclosed the basis for reciprocal action and allowed a meaningful response.

**Paths and constraints:** Require additional evidence-taking if Jacobs identifies a material factual dispute relevant to the federal reciprocal criteria that the existing process cannot fairly resolve. Do not require repetition of testimony simply because the sanction affects a professional license. Conversely, administrative convenience cannot excuse refusal to consider genuinely new material.

### Issue 3: Other constitutional and procedural objections

**Law and reach:** Attorney discipline is not automatically a criminal prosecution carrying every criminal-trial protection. *Minneapolis & St. Louis Railroad v. Bombolis*, 241 U.S. 211 (1916), addresses the Seventh Amendment’s nonapplication to state proceedings. That does not erase independently applicable due-process or self-incrimination protections. ([OpenJurist](https://openjurist.org/44/f3d/84/jacobs-grievance-committee-for-the-eastern-district-of-new-york-v-b-jacobs))

**Positions and paths:** Separate challenges to state-law jurisdiction, jury entitlement, interim arrangements, and the merits of collateral litigation. Decide only issues properly encompassed by the federal order and notice of appeal. An agreed temporary arrangement and an unpreserved objection cannot be treated as proof of a litigated constitutional holding.
