## Historical comparison

On 1994-04-29 the historical panel (Feinberg, Miner, Mahoney) reversed the Rule 12(b)(6) dismissal of the claim against Bates and remanded. Miner wrote the opinion and Feinberg joined it. Mahoney dissented, defending the administrative-cure framework. The majority rejected a categorical rule that administrative reversal cures punishment already served, distinguished the administrative-review cases, and allowed the claim to proceed without adjudicating all elements of liability or damages. It awarded no damages and did not automatically restore the other defendants. Mandate status is unknown.

## Wilfred Feinberg

**Reconciled disposition:** Reverse the dismissal of the claim against Bates insofar as it rests on administrative cure; remand; do not restore the other defendants. This matches both the commitment and history (he joined the reversing opinion).

**Ground:** Administrative reversal after punitive segregation has begun does not, as a matter of law, erase a deprivation already suffered, so it cannot bar the due process damages claim for punishment served (McCann; Wolff; Patterson; Russell as modified). Young is not controlling because no penalty was served there, and it is neither overruled nor disparaged. In his formulation, cure and mitigation bear on liability and damages for time not yet served, not as a categorical bar for time served. History confirms the result and that the cure cases were "distinguished," but history.md does not show how, so his harmonizing formulation is retained as his own and is not confirmed by history. Immunity, justification for the exclusions, causation, and damages are not decided.

**Remedy:** Reverse and remand for the district court, on a developed record, to address Bates's reasons for the exclusions (Ponte), qualified immunity, causation and damages (Carey), and Bates's responsibility for post-reversal retention. No instruction on any outcome and no damages award.

**Join limits:** Unchanged. Will not join an opinion that overrules, disparages, or declines to follow Young or Russell; one that decides immunity, justification for the exclusions, or causation; a contemporaneous-written-reasons requirement; or a holding that Bates is liable for post-reversal detention.

**Basis for any departure from the commitment or from history:** None on disposition or remedy. The commitment concedes his own pre-cutoff record is thin, so his joining the historical opinion is the main individual evidence, and it supports the commitment. Whalen only reinforces the refusal to decide immunity on appeal. One point is open: history.md says the dissent objected to a "later immunity discussion" tied to the majority's treatment of circuit precedent. It does not say that discussion decided immunity. Because history says the majority "distinguished" rather than overruled, his join limit against declining to follow Young or Russell stays; the limit would be tested by drafting that treats Young as displaced.

## Roger J. Miner

**Reconciled disposition:** Reverse insofar as the dismissal rests on administrative cure; remand; express no view on qualified immunity; do not restore the other defendants. Matches the commitment and history (he wrote the reversing opinion).

**Ground:** Same result by the same frame: de novo review under Conley; Young's premise (no penalty served) is absent; Patterson and Russell (as modified) show later correction does not erase accrued liability; and the existence of a violation is separate from immunity and damages, as Gittens illustrates. Reversal of an administrative-cure dismissal does not decide that the exclusion violated Wolff or that Bates is liable.

**Remedy:** Reverse and remand on the same open items: Ponte justification, qualified immunity, causation (Carey, McCann), post-reversal responsibility, and damages. Reversal does not award damages.

**Join limits:** Unchanged. Will not join a rationale deciding immunity or that the right was clearly established; a holding that the exclusion violated Wolff as a matter of law; treatment of Young as wrongly decided; a contemporaneous-written-reasons requirement; or language making the administrative reversal irrelevant to damages (it may mitigate).

**Basis for any departure from the commitment or from history:** None. History's authorship is evidence he would write a narrow reversal, but authorship is not a reconciled component and is left to assembly. Whalen does not matter to him: his commitment says it "confirms" that an undecided immunity defense stays with the district court. That should be read as consistency with Whalen's premise, not as a Whalen holding. The same history ambiguity applies: if the historical majority's immunity discussion went beyond noting that immunity is for remand, history would show more willingness to opine on immunity than the commitment infers. history.md does not say so, so the commitment stands.

## Agreement between the two modeled judges (by component)

- **Disposition:** Agree. Reverse insofar as the dismissal rests on administrative cure; remand; other defendants not restored.
- **Sufficient ground:** Agree on the narrow proposition: administrative reversal after punishment has begun does not, as a matter of law, bar the claim for punishment already served. Young is distinguished or reconciled, not overruled; Patterson and Russell are read as leaving accrued liability intact. Their routes differ only in framing (Feinberg: time served versus time not yet served; Miner: Young distinguished on its facts, violation separated from immunity and damages), and neither bars the other. Agreement on the judgment is not agreement on any broader rationale.
- **Remedy:** Agree. Remand on the same open items (Ponte justification, immunity, causation, post-reversal responsibility, damages) with no instruction on outcome and no damages award.
- **Join limits:** Compatible. Shared: no immunity decision, no contemporaneous-written-reasons rule, no overruling of Young. Feinberg alone adds: no liability holding for post-reversal detention and no disparaging Russell. Miner alone adds: no Wolff violation as a matter of law, no clearly-established rationale, and administrative reversal may mitigate damages. Neither judge's limit contradicts the other's position. A narrow opinion that respects the union of these limits is one both would join. Without the third judge, two of three votes therefore exist for the judgment, the narrow cure-bar ground, and the open-items remand, if the written opinion stays within that union.
