# Walker v. Bates

**Status:** Version 1; proposed, not user-approved. **Approval reference:** None. The source brief states that every Stone position, alternative, and negotiating limit is a proposal pending approval.

## Stone's proposed judicial position

Stone should propose reversal of the dismissal and remand. A disciplinary appeal can prevent a deprivation if it corrects the error before punishment. It can also terminate an ongoing deprivation and mitigate injury. It cannot make time already spent under an allegedly unlawful punitive order disappear.

That conclusion does not constitutionalize every violation of a prison rule. Walker must establish a protected interest and a denial of the process the Constitution requires. The witness right is qualified. Bates may offer legitimate safety, relevance, duplication, or other permissible grounds, and the district court must consider them under *Wolff* and *Ponte*. The appellate court should not infer such grounds merely to rescue dismissal, but neither should it foreclose grounds the governing doctrine permits the official to establish.

The proposal rests on the character and timing of the deprivation. Once the hearing imposed and immediately activated punitive confinement, the question was whether adequate procedure supported that confinement. An administrative remedy capable only of later reversal does not necessarily furnish the predeprivation protection required for an established disciplinary process. The integrated-process argument has force in *Young*, where the sentence never operated; it loses that force where the prisoner has already borne the punishment.

The remedy must remain disciplined. Reversal does not direct a damages award for every day in special housing. The court must distinguish detention caused by Bates's order from any independent lawful basis for confinement and determine who was responsible for continued confinement after reversal. Under *Carey*, actual damages require causal proof; an established procedural violation without proven compensatory injury may support nominal damages. Qualified immunity remains governed by the law clearly established when Bates acted, not by the clarity supplied by this appeal.

Stone should expressly identify *Cleavinger*'s limited holding, rather than improve the argument by overstating Supreme Court authority. His broader proposed circuit rule is that reversal after disciplinary punishment begins is not an automatic defense to an accrued procedural-due-process claim. That is a principled account of *Patterson* and *Young*, not an exemption from prison administration. It follows the Master Jurisprudence's distinction among rights, responsible defendants, immunity, causation, and remedies.

## Proposed negotiating limits

**Preferred and broadest defensible holding:** administrative reversal does not categorically extinguish a claim for disciplinary punishment already imposed without constitutionally sufficient process; reverse and remand.

**Acceptable narrower proposal:** decide only the pleaded facts, in which confinement began immediately and a substantial period preceded reversal, leaving prompt interim review under materially different arrangements open.

**Nonjoin limit:** automatic damages for the full confinement period, strict liability for every witness refusal, treating *Cleavinger* as a direct holding on administrative cure, or importing later prison-liberty restrictions into the April 1994 decision.
