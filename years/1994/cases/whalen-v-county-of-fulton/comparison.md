## Departure from history

No difference was found in the judgment, the vote count, the author or the remedy: both the simulated and the historical panel dismissed the interlocutory appeal for want of appellate jurisdiction by a 2-1 vote, with Judge Meskill writing the opinion of the court and Judge Walker dissenting alone. ## Historical comparison extract

Historically, on 1994-03-29 a panel of Judges Meskill, Walker and Motley dismissed the interlocutory appeal for lack of appellate jurisdiction. Judge Meskill wrote the opinion, joined by Judge Motley: the district court's denial of qualified-immunity summary judgment without prejudice to renewal after discovery was not a sufficiently conclusive determination under the collateral-order doctrine, and the majority did not decide the constitutional or immunity merits. Judge Walker dissented; he would have taken jurisdiction over the legal immunity question and granted qualified immunity because the asserted right was not clearly established. The provisional district-court order remained operative. The simulated judgment is the same: dismissal for want of jurisdiction, 2-1, Judge Meskill writing, Judge Walker dissenting alone. Only one panel member differs, with Judge Stone joining the majority opinion in place of Judge Motley. No changed judgment, and no different ground reaching the same judgment, was found. Source: Whalen v. County of Fulton, 19 F.3d 828, 830-34 (2d Cir. 1994) (No. 93-7457).
