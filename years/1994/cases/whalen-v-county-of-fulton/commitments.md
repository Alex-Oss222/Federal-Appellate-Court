# Commitments: Whalen v. County of Fulton, No. 93-7457 (2d Cir.)

**Stage:** neutral modeling. **Legal cutoff:** 1994-03-29. **Inputs:** neutral.md, ENGINE.md, and the public sources cited below.

## Thomas J. Meskill (Senior Circuit Judge)

**Disposition:**
1. *Appellate jurisdiction over the individual officials' appeal.* Limited review. The appeal is reviewable only to the extent the order leaves open a purely legal immunity question that can be decided on the facts as the Whalens allege them. Any part resting on genuinely disputed material facts, or on the district court's discretion over discovery sequencing as such, is not reviewable and is dismissed.
2. *Immunity, if reached.* The officials are entitled to qualified immunity on the association and substantive due process claims about placement of the sister. No clearly established law gave adoptive parents with no custodial relationship to her a right to her placement or to association with her. He would resolve this on the clearly-established prong, assuming without deciding that some protected interest exists.
3. *Access to courts and procedural due process.* The packet does not permit a separate result. His practice is claim by claim (*Mozzochi*), so the same result follows only where a claim depends on the same asserted right. Otherwise these claims are left to the district court. Source gap.
4. *Counties, departments, and state-law claims.* Not before the panel and not affected. Municipal defendants have no qualified immunity (*Owen*; he sat on *Goldberg*, which applied that rule).

**Ground:** Jurisdiction is decided de novo and first. The test is *Cohen* and *Coopers & Lybrand*, applied through *Mitchell*: a denial is reviewable "to the extent that it turns on" a question of law. His own opinions add two circuit-law steps. In *Dube* he followed *Musso v. Hourigan* and held that a denial of summary judgment that does not actually address the immunity defense is immediately reviewable, so the entitlement not to be forced to litigate is not lost. In *Washington Square Post* (n.1) he held that a denial resting on a misapplication of law is reviewable and that immunity is separate from the merits. The merits step is de novo, on all relevant precedent (*Elder*), under *Harlow* and *Siegert*. *Smith v. OFFER* rested family-integrity protection on biology and on the attachments of daily association, and it assumed rather than decided a foster-family interest. His own *Rivera v. Marcus* protected a custodial half-sister who had raised the children in her household. That existing household was the basis of the interest, and nothing in the packet shows one here.

**Remedy:** Reverse the order in the reviewable part and remand with directions to grant the individual defendants summary judgment on the federal claims resting on the asserted sibling-placement right (the disposition he used in *Washington Square Post* and *Mozzochi*). Remand the rest.

*Alternative path, which matters here:* dismiss the appeal for want of jurisdiction without reaching immunity. *Dube* and *Musso* involved a silent denial, while this order expressly preserves renewal before trial. If the order's text shows the court held back for facts material to the legal question, *Golino* and *Cartier* make it unreviewable. His own *Dube* treated a cryptic statement that summary judgment was denied on immunity as no real ruling on the defense, which shows he looks to what the order actually decided, not its label, and cuts against deciding on the words "without prejudice" alone. The packet omits the order, so this branch cannot be closed.

Negron*, 996 F.2d 1439 (2d Cir. 1993) (per curiam, panel Meskill, Walker, Motley; resolved immunity on the clearly-established prong without deciding the constitutional question); *Musso v. Hourigan*, 836 F.2d 736, 741 (2d Cir. 1988); *Cartier v. Lussier*, 955 F.2d 841, 844 (2d Cir. 1992); *Golino v. City of New Haven*, 950 F.2d 864 (2d Cir. 1991); *Goldberg v. Town of Rocky Hill*, 973 F.2d 70 (2d Cir. 1992) (Pratt, J., panel including Meskill); *Cohen*, 337 U.S. 541 (1949); *Coopers & Lybrand*, 437 U.S. 463 (1978); *Mitchell v. **Join limits:** He would not join a rationale that (a) holds that sibling or extended-family bonds can never carry a protected interest (his *Rivera* opinion quoted *Moore v. East Cleveland* and protected a non-parent relative); (b) takes jurisdiction merely because immunity is invoked, or reviews a discovery-scheduling ruling as such; (c) dismisses on the "without prejudice" label without examining the order; or (d) reaches the institutional defendants without overlap (*Dube*'s pendent-jurisdiction standard). Otherwise routine agreement.

## John M. Walker, Jr. (Active Circuit Judge)

**Disposition:**
1. *Appellate jurisdiction.* Limited review, on a stricter statement than Meskill's. Jurisdiction exists only if the defense "may be established as a matter of law" on the facts viewed for the Whalens, or if the district court committed fundamental error of law. He decides de novo whether disputed facts are material. If the order truly rests on needed, material fact development, he dismisses the appeal.
2. *Immunity, if reached.* The same result as above on the association and substantive due process claims about the sister: no clearly established right, resolved on that prong without deciding whether a constitutional right exists.
3. *Other federal components.* Decided only to the extent the question is purely legal and the same absence of clearly established law controls. Otherwise not reached. Source gap, as above.
4. *Counties and state-law claims.* Untouched.

**Ground:** *Soares* states his rule: collateral-order jurisdiction over a summary-judgment denial "is limited to circumstances where the qualified immunity defense may be established as a matter of law or where the district court has committed fundamental error in applying the law," citing *Cartier* (844), *Mitchell*, and *Washington Square Post* (1292 n.1). In *Soares* he looked past the district court's framing of the issues as factual, took jurisdiction, and reversed on the legal question. In *Easton v. Sundram* he resolved immunity on the clearly-established prong, did not decide whether a liberty interest was alleged, and did not remand for an initial ruling because the question was purely legal. Review is de novo (*Cartier*; *Elder*). On the merits he would apply *Harlow*, *Siegert*, and the packet's distinctions of *Smith v. OFFER* and *Rivera*.

**Remedy:** Reverse in the reviewable part and remand for entry of summary judgment for the individual defendants on the federal claims resting on that right. Leave the remainder to the district court.

*Alternative path, which matters here:* dismissal for want of jurisdiction. It follows if the order is a deferral that depends on material facts, because *Golino* denies review of such a ruling and his *Soares* limit offers no other footing. Nothing in his own writings addresses an express deferral with leave to renew. He cites neither *Musso* nor *Dube* in *Soares*, so he would rest jurisdiction on *Cartier* and *Soares*, not on the silent-denial line. Which branch he takes turns on the unseen order. On the packet's account (pleaded facts, no custodial relationship), the facts material to the legal question appear undisputed.

**Sources:** *Soares v. Connecticut*, 8 F.3d 917 (2d Cir. 1993) (Walker, J.); *Easton v. Sundram*, 947 F.2d 1011 (2d Cir. 1991) (Walker, J.); *Severino v. Negron*, 996 F.2d 1439 (2d Cir. 1993) (per curiam; he sat); and the circuit, Supreme Court, and statutory authorities cited in full under Meskill (*Cartier*, *Golino*, *Washington Square Post* n.1, *Musso*, *Rivera*, *Cohen*, *Coopers & Lybrand*, *Mitchell*, *Harlow*, *Siegert*, *Elder*, *Owen*, *Smith v. OFFER*, § 1291).

**Join limits:** He would not join a rationale that (a) finds jurisdiction without first finding the defense can be decided as a matter of law or that fundamental error occurred; (b) rests on facts the Whalens have not alleged or on resolving a disputed fact; or (c) announces a broad constitutional rule on sibling association when the clearly-established prong decides the case (*Easton*, *Severino*). He would require the clearly-established analysis to canvass all relevant precedent (*Elder*). Otherwise routine agreement.
