## Record and issues

The City administered federal Head Start grants through numerous delegate agencies. Accounts receivable represented federal funds advanced beyond documented allowable expenditures; accounts payable represented drawn federal funds not yet disbursed to satisfy reported obligations. HHS disallowed $620,622 for program year 16 and, for program year 19, $901,639 in receivables and $1,541,362 in payables. The departmental appeals board sustained the disallowances. HHS later offset the sums and interest against a grant. ([law.justia.com](https://law.justia.com/cases/federal/appellate-courts/F3/34/1161/552099/))

The two years present materially different offset records. For year 16, the City supplied internal documentation lacking independent verification. For year 19, it produced an independently audited offset demonstration. The agency nevertheless demanded some original supporting records for years whose retention periods had expired and relied on a contested numerical theory of lost federal recovery. The City conceded in supplemental appellate briefing that proper prior-period adjustments could prevent double recovery, while maintaining a cash-flow objection. ([law.justia.com](https://law.justia.com/cases/federal/appellate-courts/F3/34/1161/552099/))

**Jurisdiction:** Review of the final district judgment under § 1291; federal-question review of final agency action under the APA. **Preservation:** Disallowance authority, arbitrariness, audit closure, offsets, record-retention duties, and interest are presented. Some alternative objections to offsets were not decided by the agency, and one was first advanced on appeal. **Missing material:** Complete audit files, agency hearing record, grant accounts, complaint, and notice dates.

## Law entering the event

**Cutoff:** September 13, 1994. **Simulation holdings:** None supplied.

### Issue 1: Disallowance authority and prior audit closure

**Review and law:** The appellate court independently reviews the administrative record under the APA, without deference to the district court’s legal conclusion. HHS’s delegated audit and grant-administration authority does not exempt its actions from § 706(2)(A). *Overton Park*, 401 U.S. 402 (1971), and *State Farm*, 463 U.S. 29 (1983), require consideration of relevant factors and a rational explanation without substituting judicial policy judgment. ([law.justia.com](https://law.justia.com/cases/federal/appellate-courts/F3/34/1161/552099/))

**Positions:** HHS argues that closing an audit while carrying an unresolved receivable did not forgive the City’s duty to account for federal funds. The City argues that repeated closures accepted the accounting treatment and made later disallowance an unexplained reversal. Under *Boccanfuso*, 882 F.2d 666 (2d Cir. 1989), *Azizi*, 908 F.2d 1130 (2d Cir. 1990), and *Schweiker v. Hansen*, 450 U.S. 785 (1981), government estoppel requires substantially more than administrative delay or acquiescence. ([law.justia.com](https://law.justia.com/cases/federal/appellate-courts/F3/34/1161/552099/))

**Paths:** Sustain a disallowance supported by unaccounted-for funds, warnings, and unmet documentation duties. Reject any conclusion that audit closure necessarily extinguishes all continuing obligations. Conversely, determine what a particular closure actually resolved.

### Issue 2: Retention rules and verified offsets

**Governing law:** The contemporaneous grant regulations required repayment of excess funds, independent audits, and retention of specified records for defined periods. The agency could examine continuing validity but could not revive an expired original-document retention obligation. An age threshold used to identify accounts needing explanation is not necessarily a new categorical financial-management rule. ([law.justia.com](https://law.justia.com/cases/federal/appellate-courts/F3/34/1161/552099/))

**Positions:** The City distinguishes legitimate audited offsets between obligations involving the same delegate agency from undocumented write-offs. HHS emphasizes stale obligations and annual accounting requirements. The year-19 rejection cannot be sustained merely by repeating the valid objection to the unaudited year-16 submission.

**Open questions and remedy:** The permissibility of cross-year offsets and the consequences of earlier City representations were not fully decided by the agency. Remand those matters rather than supply a new appellate rationale. If the agency’s existing reasons fail, renewed agency consideration is appropriate; the court need not itself audit every account.

### Issue 3: Interest

**Law and review:** *United States v. Texas*, 507 U.S. 529 (1993), holds that the Debt Collection Act did not eliminate the federal common-law ability to obtain interest from state and local governments. *West Virginia v. United States*, 479 U.S. 305 (1987), requires consideration of the pertinent interests. Legal availability is reviewed de novo; the appropriate award and rate require the relevant discretionary assessment. ([Justia Law](https://law.justia.com/cases/federal/appellate-courts/F3/34/1161/552099/))

**Positions and paths:** HHS argues that statutory exclusion from an automatic interest provision does not eliminate common-law recovery. The City contests both entitlement and amount. Recognize the possible source of recovery without automatically approving the agency’s rate or aggregate demand. Remand interest on established liability; defer interest questions tied to disallowances still under agency reconsideration.
