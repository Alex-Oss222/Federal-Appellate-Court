## Record and issues

**Material record:** Jeffries, chair of City College’s Black Studies Department, was recommended for another three-year term before his July 20, 1991 off-campus speech in Albany. The speech addressed curriculum and racial representation and included antisemitic assertions. Public controversy followed. University officials later recommended a shorter appointment, and the trustees replaced him as chair. His professorship was distinct from the chairmanship. ([Law Resource](https://law.resource.org/pub/us/case/reporter/F3/021/21.F3d.1238.93-7876.953.html))

The jury found that the speech substantially motivated the adverse action, that defendants would not have taken the same action without it, and that the speech had not actually impaired university operations. It also found action motivated by a reasonable expectation of disruption. The relationship between those findings is central to both the speech claim and punitive damages. The district court imposed individual liability on Harleston, Reynolds, Everett, Badillo, Bloom, and Jacobs, and ordered two years of reinstatement. These are verdict findings and lower-court rulings, not assumptions about how Stone’s panel must resolve them. ([Law Resource](https://law.resource.org/pub/us/case/reporter/F3/021/21.F3d.1238.93-7876.953.html))

**Jurisdiction:** Section 1291, with the underlying § 1983 action arising under federal law. No appellate timeliness dispute is reported; the notice was not retrieved. **Preserved issues:** Speech protection, disruption, causation, immunity, evidentiary use of prior litigation, punitive damages, and equitable relief. **Missing material:** Complete special-verdict form, charge and objections, individual-damages allocations, trial transcript, and precise judgment date.

### Issue 1: Speech protection and employment causation

**Review and governing law:** The constitutional balance is reviewed independently, while supported jury findings are not reweighed. *Pickering v. Board of Education*, 391 U.S. 563 (1968), *Connick v. Myers*, 461 U.S. 138 (1983), and *Rankin v. McPherson*, 483 U.S. 378 (1987), govern public concern and the employer’s operational interests. *Piesco v. City of New York*, 933 F.2d 1149 (2d Cir. 1991), gives particular force to the actual-disruption requirement for speech of substantial public concern. *Mt. Healthy*, 429 U.S. 274 (1977), supplies the motivating-factor and same-decision allocation. ([Law Resource](https://law.resource.org/pub/us/case/reporter/F3/021/21.F3d.1238.93-7876.953.html))

**Positions:** Defendants emphasize the chair’s administrative responsibilities and reasonably anticipated harm. Jeffries emphasizes the public subject, the limited policymaking character of the chairmanship, favorable evidence about departmental operations, and the verdict finding no actual impairment. Offensiveness does not itself resolve the balance; neither does the label “department chair.”

**Lawful paths:** Apply the controlling circuit treatment of operational harm to the actual duties and verdict. Sustain liability if defendants’ justification fails that rule and the same-decision defense fails; otherwise modify or reverse the judgment on a legally supported basis. Do not substitute a later Supreme Court standard.

### Issue 2: Individual causation and qualified immunity

**Review and law:** Legal immunity questions receive de novo review. *Harlow v. Fitzgerald*, 457 U.S. 800 (1982), and *Anderson v. Creighton*, 483 U.S. 635 (1987), require clearly established law and objective reasonableness at the time of each defendant’s conduct. Section 1983 requires each defendant’s actionable participation; membership in a multimember body does not automatically establish individual liability. ([Law Resource](https://law.resource.org/pub/us/case/reporter/F3/021/21.F3d.1238.93-7876.953.html))

**Positions and temporal limit:** Jeffries relies on the officials’ material participation and the notice supplied by existing speech cases. Defendants argue that they reasonably anticipated disruption and that some individuals did not supply the decisive votes. Cases arising after the 1991 actions may explain the law in 1994 but cannot retroactively provide 1991 notice. A defendant’s ability to influence or cause the decision must be assessed individually.

**Paths:** Uphold liability only for defendants whose participation and legally sufficient causation are established. Decide immunity separately from whether punitive damages are justified. Evidence of earlier litigation may be relevant to notice, but not used simply to infer a propensity to violate rights.

### Issue 3: Punitive damages and reinstatement

**Governing law and review:** *Smith v. Wade*, 461 U.S. 30 (1983), requires the culpability necessary for punitive damages. *Finnegan v. Fountain*, 915 F.2d 817 (2d Cir. 1990), and circuit special-verdict authorities require a legally supportable relationship between the findings. Equitable relief receives abuse-of-discretion review within correct legal premises. ([Law Resource](https://law.resource.org/pub/us/case/reporter/F3/021/21.F3d.1238.93-7876.953.html))

**Positions:** Defendants contend that a reasonable expectation of disruption cannot be reconciled with punitive culpability. Jeffries argues that the findings concerning the university’s collective action need not describe the motives of each liable individual. His reinstatement claim seeks restoration of the chairmanship period lost, not permanent entitlement to the position.

**Lawful paths:** Reconcile the findings if a fair reading permits it; otherwise order a new trial limited to punitive damages if liability and equitable relief are independently separable. Preserve a properly measured reinstatement remedy without guaranteeing future reappointment.
