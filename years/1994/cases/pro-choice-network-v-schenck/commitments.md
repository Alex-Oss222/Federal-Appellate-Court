# Commitments: Pro-Choice Network of Western New York v. Schenck, Nos. 92-7302, 93-7918 (2d Cir.)

**Stage:** neutral modeling. **Legal cutoff:** 1994-09-06. **Inputs:** neutral.md, ENGINE.md, and the public sources cited below.

## James L. Oakes (Senior Circuit Judge)

**Disposition:**
- Pendent jurisdiction: affirm retention of the state claims; no dissolution on that ground.
- Fixed fifteen-foot zones at entrances and driveways: affirm.
- Moving fifteen-foot zones around persons and vehicles: vacate and remand.
- Listener-triggered cessation command: vacate as written.
- Noise term: affirm an objective, health-protective limit, conformed to Rule 65(d) on remand.
- Obstruction, trespass, crowding and shoving terms: affirm. The two-counselor allowance is conformed to the zone and cessation rulings.

**Ground:**
- *Pendent jurisdiction.* Power under *Gibbs* rested on the § 1985(3) claim, which was not insubstantial when filed. Under *Hagans v. Lavine*, a claim is insubstantial only if devoid of merit or foreclosed by prior decisions, so *Bray's* later rejection of the theory removes no power. Section 1367 is not the source, because the action was commenced September 24, 1990 and § 1367 reaches only actions commenced on or after December 1, 1990. Retention is reviewed for abuse of discretion under *Cohill* (economy, convenience, fairness, comity). The district court applied those factors: about three years of litigation, contempt hearings, a dormant state action, and an injunction resting partly on state trespass and N.Y. Civ. Rights Law § 40-c. Circuit law affirms retention where substantial resources are committed (*Enercomp*, *Raucci*, *Purgess*), although *Gibbs* counsels dismissal when federal claims fall before trial (*Castellano*). *Town of West Hartford v. Operation Rescue* is distinguishable, because the federal claim there was insubstantial and the court lacked power.
- *Fixed zones.* Under *Madsen* a content-neutral injunction may burden no more speech than necessary to serve a significant government interest. That is stricter than ordinary time, place and manner review but is not strict scrutiny. *Madsen* sustained a 36-foot entrance and driveway zone, weighing the failure of the first order. Fifteen feet is narrower, and the described findings (secret blockades, dispersal and return, contempt, a restraining order that did not hold) are reviewed for clear error.
- *Moving zones.* These carry the exclusion through public space even when no one is obstructed. The decree already bars obstruction, crowding and shoving. *Madsen* invalidated the 300-foot no-approach provision as burdening more speech than needed to prevent intimidation and ensure access. The described record has no finding showing why the conduct terms would fail around persons and vehicles.
- *Cessation.* *Madsen* held that the "consent" requirement alone invalidated its no-approach provision. This term turns on the listener's reaction, not on following, intimidation or interference with departure, and *Boos* bars treating the audience's emotive reaction as the justification.
- *Noise.* *Madsen* upheld noise limits protecting patients. An objective volume standard does not depend on viewpoint, but Rule 65(d) requires specific terms.

**Remedy:** Affirm in part, vacate in part, and remand for modification of the preliminary injunction. The district court may impose narrower conduct-based protections (no following, encircling or blocking) on specific findings. The access and obstruction terms operate independently and survive.

**Sources:** His own writings: *Carlin Communications, Inc. v. FCC*, 749 F.2d 113 (2d Cir. 1984) (content-based rule set aside as overinclusive; the proponent must show why less speech-restrictive means fail); *Deeper Life Christian Fellowship, Inc. v. Board of Education*, 852 F.2d 676 (2d Cir. 1988) (First Amendment preliminary injunction; deference to interim relief; loss of First Amendment freedoms is irreparable harm); *National Advertising Co. v. Town of Babylon*, 900 F.2d 551 (2d Cir. 1990) (proponent bears the burden of justifying the restriction; severability analysis). He wrote no pendent-jurisdiction opinion that I could find; he sat on, but did not write, *Kavit v. A.L. Stamm & Co.*, 491 F.2d 1176 (2d Cir. 1974), and *Enercomp, Inc. v. McCorhill Publishing, Inc.*, 873 F.2d 536 (2d Cir. 1989). Governing authorities: *United Mine Workers v. Gibbs*, 383 U.S. 715 (1966); *Carnegie-Mellon University v. Cohill*, 484 U.S. 343 (1988); *Hagans v. Lavine*, 415 U.S. 528 (1974); *Bray v. Alexandria Women's Health Clinic*, 506 U.S. 263 (1993); *Madsen v. Women's Health Center, Inc.*, 512 U.S. 753 (1994); *Boos v. Barry*, 485 U.S. 312 (1988); 28 U.S.C. § 1367 with Pub. L. 101-650, § 310(c); *Raucci v. Town of Rotterdam*, 902 F.2d 1050 (2d Cir. 1990); *Purgess v. Sharrock*, 33 F.3d 134 (2d Cir. 1994); *Castellano v. Board of Trustees*, 937 F.2d 752 (2d Cir. 1991); *Town of West Hartford v. Operation Rescue*, 915 F.2d 92 (2d Cir. 1990); *New York State National Organization for Women v. Terry*, 886 F.2d 1339 (2d Cir. 1989) (Rule 65(d) discussion; no constitutional privilege to assault or harass); 799 F. Supp. 1417 (W.D.N.Y. 1992); 828 F. Supp. 1018 (W.D.N.Y. 1993). Pinpoints are not verified except those in neutral.md.

**Join limits:** He would not join a rationale that (a) treats *Gibbs's* dismissal sentence as mandatory or § 1367 as the source; (b) sustains the moving zones or cessation command on captive-audience or message-distress reasoning; (c) invalidates every fifteen-foot zone, or applies strict scrutiny to the whole decree, contrary to *Madsen*; or (d) affirms the fixed zones on bare numerical reasonableness instead of record findings. A plausible alternative is a limited remand for site-specific findings on the fixed zones, but only if the findings prove undifferentiated.

## Thomas J. Meskill (Senior Circuit Judge)

**Disposition:**
- Pendent jurisdiction: affirm retention.
- Fixed entrance and driveway zones: affirm.
- Moving zones: vacate and remand for findings. This is his closest component.
- Listener-triggered cessation command: vacate as written.
- Noise term: affirm as an objective limit, with Rule 65(d) conformity.
- Obstruction, trespass and threat or crowding terms: affirm.

**Ground:**
- *Pendent jurisdiction.* The analysis is the same as above: *Gibbs* power from a substantial federal claim at filing, no reliance on § 1367, and abuse-of-discretion review under *Cohill* and circuit decisions affirming retention after resources were committed. His *Young* opinion agreed with vacating the state-law ruling there, where the majority found no case or controversy and no common nucleus, a power problem absent here. His *Dube* majority applied *Pennhurst* only to injunctions against state officials, so it is no barrier here.
- *Fixed zones.* *Madsen* controls. In *Carew-Reid* he reviewed a content-neutral restriction for abuse of discretion, accepted a prophylactic limit aimed at a recognized evil without proof that each speaker violated it, and said the narrow tailoring requirement "is not so demanding." Those points support deference to differentiated findings about entrances and driveways, and *Madsen* sustained a larger entrance zone.
- *Moving zones.* *Carew-Reid* was an ordinance case decided before *Madsen* required "somewhat more stringent" scrutiny of injunctions, so it does not control. In his *Young* partial dissent he found a total ban on peaceful solicitation to burden "substantially more speech than necessary," because intimidating conduct could be targeted directly. The moving zone is the same kind of blanket exclusion, and the existing conduct terms already cover obstruction, crowding and shoving. The record described lacks a finding that those terms failed around persons and vehicles.
- *Cessation.* *Madsen's* consent holding controls. A narrower term aimed at following or persisting after refusal could be justified on findings.
- *Noise.* *Carew-Reid* treated noise reduction as a substantial interest, and *Madsen* upheld noise limits.
- *Threats and obstruction.* *Mozzochi* treated a threatening communication as unprotected, and *Terry* confirms that there is no privilege to obstruct, trespass or harass.

**Remedy:** Affirm in part, vacate in part, and remand for modification and findings. The access terms stay in force.

**Sources:** His own writings: *Carew-Reid v. Metropolitan Transportation Authority*, 903 F.2d 914 (2d Cir. 1990); *Young v. New York City Transit Authority*, 903 F.2d 146 (2d Cir. 1990) (Altimari, J., for the court; Meskill, J., concurring in part and dissenting in part); *Dube v. State University of New York*, 900 F.2d 587 (2d Cir. 1990); *Mozzochi v. Borden*, 959 F.2d 1174 (2d Cir. 1992). He wrote no opinion I could find applying *Cohill* to retained state claims. Governing authorities are those listed for Judge Oakes. No pinpoints are verified for his opinions.

**Join limits:** He would not join a rationale that (a) disregards clear-error review of the district court's findings; (b) makes the mere existence of a less restrictive alternative dispositive, because *Carew-Reid* declined to treat that as part of tailoring, although *Madsen* counts smaller zones as relevant; (c) invalidates every fifteen-foot zone or applies strict scrutiny to the whole decree; (d) treats § 1367 as the source; or (e) sustains the cessation command on captive-audience or emotive-impact reasoning. A plausible alternative on the moving zones is to affirm them if the findings show that conduct-only terms failed around patients. That turns on findings not before me, and I do not rank it above vacatur.
