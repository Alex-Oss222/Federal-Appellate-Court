# Reconciled positions: Pro-Choice Network of Western New York v. Schenck, Nos. 92-7302, 93-7918 (2d Cir.)

**Stage:** historical reconciliation. **Inputs:** neutral.md, commitments.md, history.md, ENGINE.md.

## Historical comparison

History (panel event of 1994-09-06): retention of the developed pendent state claims and refusal to dissolve the injunction wholesale were affirmed. Applying *Madsen*, the majority rejected the fifteen-foot restrictions and the cease-on-demand requirement for sidewalk counselors, and sustained independently lawful access, trespass and noise protections, provision by provision. Meskill wrote, joined by Altimari. Oakes dissented from invalidating "the challenged protective provisions," stressing the obstruction record, patient protection and the district court's remedial judgment.

Premises that survive here: the same decree, record, issues, argument date (1994-03-24), cutoff, and governing law, including *Madsen*, decided during the appeal. The one changed premise is that Altimari's seat is filled by another person. That alters neither the law nor the record, and it does not bear on how either retained judge reads them.

Differences between commitments and history: (1) Meskill's commitment affirms the fixed zones, while history has him writing to reject the fifteen-foot restrictions. (2) Oakes's commitment vacates the moving zones and the cessation command, while history shows him dissenting from invalidation, in terms too general to say whether the dissent reached those two provisions. All other components match history in result. History does not separate fixed from moving zones, does not say which provisions the dissent would have sustained, and does not state the majority's sub-ground for the zones.

## James L. Oakes

**Reconciled disposition:** Unchanged from the commitment. Affirm retention of the pendent state claims. Affirm the fixed entrance and driveway zones. Vacate the moving zones. Vacate the cessation command as written. Affirm an objective noise limit, conformed to Rule 65(d). Affirm the obstruction, trespass, crowding and shoving terms; the two-counselor allowance is conformed to the zone and cessation rulings.

**Ground:** Unchanged. *Gibbs* power rested on a not-insubstantial § 1985(3) claim at filing (*Hagans*), so *Bray* removed no power; § 1367 is not the source; retention is an abuse-of-discretion question under *Cohill*. Under *Madsen* the fixed zones are narrower than the 36-foot zone sustained there and rest on record findings reviewed for clear error. The moving zones and the cessation command fail *Madsen*'s necessity requirement and its consent holding, and *Boos* bars a message-distress rationale.

**Remedy:** Affirm in part, vacate in part, remand. The district court may impose narrower conduct-based protections (no following, encircling or blocking) on specific findings.

**Join limits:** As in the commitment: no treatment of *Gibbs* as mandatory or of § 1367 as the source; no captive-audience or message-distress rationale for the moving zones or cessation; no invalidation of every fifteen-foot zone and no strict scrutiny; no affirmance of the fixed zones on bare numerical reasonableness. History confirms the fixed-zone limit: he would dissent from a holding that vacates the fixed zones on this record. His only stated alternative, a limited remand for site-specific findings, is conditioned on findings proving undifferentiated, which nothing before me shows.

**Basis for any departure from the commitment or from history:** None. The fixed-zone commitment agrees with history through the dissent. For the moving zones and cessation, history does not say he would have sustained either. The dissent's stated emphases (obstruction record, patient protection, deference to remedial judgment) were already inputs to the commitment (*Deeper Life*, the fixed-zone affirmance). No changed premise explains a difference, so the commitment stays. The moving zones are the component where history most plausibly points the other way. If the dissent reached them, affirmance on the crowding and interference record is a plausible alternative, not ranked above the commitment. The cessation vacatur is more secure because *Madsen*'s consent holding is controlling authority.

## Thomas J. Meskill

**Reconciled disposition:** Affirm retention of the pendent state claims. **Fixed entrance and driveway zones: vacate and remand (changed from the commitment's affirm).** Vacate the moving zones and remand for findings. Vacate the cessation command as written. Affirm an objective noise limit with Rule 65(d) conformity. Affirm the obstruction, trespass, threat and crowding terms.

**Ground:** Retention, moving zones, cessation, noise and threats are unchanged from the commitment. For the fixed zones, *Madsen*'s requirement that an injunction burden no more speech than necessary is not met by this decree on this record. The record as described does not show why the surviving obstruction and trespass terms could not secure entrances and driveways without a fifteen-foot exclusion. The sub-ground (insufficient necessity findings, or breadth on the findings that exist) cannot be fixed from history.md. The vacatur rests on that legal insufficiency, not on rejecting any found fact, so clear-error review is respected.

**Remedy:** Affirm in part, vacate in part, remand for modification and findings. Access, obstruction, trespass and noise terms stay in force. The district court may adopt a separately justified entrance-access remedy on site-specific findings.

**Join limits:** (a) no disregard of clear-error review; (b) no rationale making a less restrictive alternative dispositive by itself; (c) revised: no categorical invalidation of all buffer zones and no strict scrutiny, but vacatur of these zones on this record is acceptable; (d) no reliance on § 1367; (e) no captive-audience or emotive-impact basis for the cessation ruling. Added: no rationale dissolving the injunction wholesale or the surviving access, trespass and noise terms.

**Basis for any departure from the commitment or from history:** The fixed-zone change only. No changed legal, factual or procedural premise explains it, and Altimari's replacement does not. History is stronger than the commitment's basis for four reasons. First, it is this judge's own authored application of *Madsen* to this decree and record, while the commitment inferred his view from pre-*Madsen* ordinance and solicitation opinions and found no clinic-access writing. Second, the commitment says its fixed-zone result "assumes entrance and driveway findings as described," and that site layout and differentiation of findings were not seen. The opinion was written on the full record. Third, the commitment relied on *Carew-Reid*'s "not so demanding" tailoring language for the fixed zones while discounting the same case for the moving zones as pre-*Madsen* and ordinance-based, an internal inconsistency I have not corrected. Fourth, every historical premise survives.
## Agreement between the two modeled judges (by component)

- **Retention of pendent state claims:** Agree (affirm). Two votes exist; the grounds are compatible.
- **Fixed entrance and driveway zones:** Disagree. Oakes affirms; Meskill vacates and remands. These two judges supply no two-vote result either way, so this component depends on the third vote. The only identified bridge, a limited remand for site-specific findings, is not shown to be acceptable to Oakes.
- **Moving zones:** Agree on vacatur and remand. Oakes allows narrower conduct-based protections on findings; Meskill remands for findings and notes affirmance if findings show conduct terms failed. This is compatible, but it is Meskill's closest component and Oakes's most history-exposed one, so the agreement is the least secure.
- **Cessation command:** Agree (vacate as written) on *Madsen*'s consent holding and *Boos*. Both bar a captive-audience rationale and allow a narrower follow-or-persist term on findings.
- **Noise:** Agree (affirm an objective limit, conformed to Rule 65(d)).
- **Obstruction, trespass, crowding and threat terms:** Agree (affirm).
- **Two-counselor allowance:** Oakes conforms it to the zone and cessation rulings; Meskill states no position. Because they differ on the fixed zones, the conformity cannot yet be matched.
- **Remedy form:** Both say affirm in part, vacate in part, and remand; content differs only through the fixed-zone ruling. Authorship and separate writings are not decided here.
