## Departure from history

The simulated panel (Oakes, Meskill, Stone-Khoury) points the same way as the historical panel (Oakes, Meskill, Altimari) on every component: retention of the pendent state claims affirmed, the fifteen-foot restrictions and the counselor cease-on-refusal command set aside, and the access, trespass and noise terms sustained. It differs in remedy and reasoning on the fixed zones, which it vacates and remands for site-specific Madsen findings without prejudice to a renewed fixed zone (the historical court is recorded as rejecting the fifteen-foot restrictions), and it adds a remand to conform the noise wording to Rule 65(d) that the historical summary does not record. The lineup differs: Stone-Khoury joins Meskill's opinion in Altimari's place, and Oakes joins the court on every point except the fixed zones, where the historical Oakes is recorded as dissenting from the invalidation of the protective provisions.

## Historical comparison extract

On 1994-09-06 the historical panel (Oakes, Meskill, Altimari) affirmed in part and reversed in part the preliminary injunction, affirmed the refusal to dissolve it wholesale after the federal claim was dismissed, and kept the developed pendent state claims after Bray. Applying Madsen provision by provision, it rejected the fifteen-foot restrictions and the requirement that sidewalk counselors cease on a listener's demand, and sustained the access, trespass and noise protections. Meskill wrote, joined by Altimari; Oakes dissented from invalidating the protective provisions.

The simulated judgment points the same way on every component, so the judgment is unchanged in substance. Reasoning and remedy differ on the fixed zones: the simulated court vacated them and remanded for site-specific findings, holding no fixed zone categorically invalid. The lineup differs: Stone-Khoury replaces Altimari, and Oakes joins the court except on the fixed zones. Meskill again writes the opinion.

Source: Pro-Choice Network of W.N.Y. v. Schenck, Nos. 92-7302, 93-7918, 67 F.3d 359, 367-77 (2d Cir. 1994).

**Supreme Court merits decision as general law.** Schenck v. Pro-Choice Network of Western New York, 519 U.S. 357 (decided 1997-02-19), is binding law from that date regardless of the procedural path, and it cannot influence the 1994-09-06 decision. It reviewed this injunction under Madsen and held the fixed zones at doorways, driveways and driveway entrances, and the cease-and-desist limit within them, valid, and the floating zones invalid. Its invalidation of the floating zones is consistent with the simulated vacatur of the moving zones. It bears directly on the simulated holdings that the fixed zones were legally insufficient on the findings and that the cease-on-refusal command is vacated as written.
