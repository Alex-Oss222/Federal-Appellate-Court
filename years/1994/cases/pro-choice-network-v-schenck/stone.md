### Component 2

**Component:** Fixed and moving fifteen-foot restrictions

**Jurisdiction and reach:** The challenged zones form part of the injunction under review; Madsen was decided during the appeal and addressed in supplemental submissions. The statutory route and unsettled jurisdictional predicates in the neutral baseline remain controlling; this proposal does not assume them away.

**Proposed disposition:** Vacate the current fifteen-foot speech exclusions, with a distinct opportunity to justify a narrower fixed entrance restriction.

**Governing law:** Independent constitutional review of the legal burden; clear error for historical findings; abuse of discretion for equitable choices within the correct speech standard. A content-neutral injunction may burden no more speech than necessary to serve a significant governmental interest. Fixed access corridors and zones moving around people or vehicles impose different burdens and require separate necessity analysis.

Madsen v. Women’s Health Center, Inc., 512 U.S. 753, 764-66 (1994), supplies the controlling injunction standard and recognizes an entrance-access restriction on its own record. First Amendment; Fed. R. Civ. P. 65(d); the supported state-law prohibitions on obstruction and trespass. A prior violation may support necessity but is not a categorical prerequisite to every preventive restriction.

**Legal relationship:** Applying binding law.

**Proposed holding:** The moving exclusion’s actual speech burden is insufficiently tied to access on this record, and the fixed restriction requires its own necessity findings rather than the same undifferentiated rationale.

**Legal reasoning:** Stone should propose affirmance of the injunction's core prohibitions and of the decision not to dissolve it wholesale after *Bray*, while vacating the fifteen-foot speech exclusions and the cease-and-desist restriction on this record. The mandate should permit a renewed, separately justified fixed access zone if the district court identifies facts showing that a narrower obstruction prohibition cannot adequately protect the actual entrances and approaches. He should not declare fixed zones inherently invalid or make a previously violated injunction an indispensable legal prerequisite to every preventive remedy.

His starting point should be that neither the clinics' lawful medical work nor the protesters' religious and political advocacy is optional in the legal analysis. The relevant state-law rights support protection against trespass and interference; the First Amendment protects peaceful communication in traditional public forums. No hybrid abortion-and-speech doctrine gives one side an automatic advantage. *Madsen* supplies the governing test.

On the existing findings, the moving restriction captures too much conduct disconnected from access. A counselor standing or walking without blocking another person may be forced away as the protected person moves. The government must justify that actual burden, not substitute the proposition that some members of the group previously obstructed patients. An injunction may reach persons acting in concert with wrongdoers under Rule 65, but association does not erase the requirement that the prohibited conduct be adequately connected to the violation.

A fixed entrance zone warrants a more cautious explanation. Doorways and vehicle entrances can require preventive clearance; the existence of peaceful events under the earlier order does not disprove every need for such clearance. Stone should therefore rest the present vacatur on insufficiently differentiated findings and scope, not announce that a district judge must allow another blockade before acting. A renewed decree must distinguish a stationary access corridor from an exclusion surrounding every moving person, consider whether passage remains genuinely possible, and explain why the selected dimensions are necessary. Those are applications of *Madsen*, not a judicially invented permanent footage rule.

**Preferred scope:** The moving exclusion’s actual speech burden is insufficiently tied to access on this record, and the fixed restriction requires its own necessity findings rather than the same undifferentiated rationale. The lawful outer boundary is the issue and remedial limit stated here, not a broader rule about unpresented facts.

**Fallback position:** Proposed, not approved: invalidate the moving zones and cease-and-desist provision while remanding only the fixed-zone portion for findings under *Madsen*. A properly supported fixed entrance restriction is legally acceptable in principle; this is not approval of the present fifteen-foot requirement or of facts not found. The case-level nonjoin limits in Component 1 apply; no trade in accuracy or jurisdiction is authorized.

**Remedy and remand:** Preserve obstruction and trespass commands; permit a renewed fixed access zone only on a supported, differentiated application of Madsen.

### Component 3

**Component:** Listener-triggered cessation, noise, and physical-access commands

**Jurisdiction and reach:** The cease-and-desist requirement and noise/access terms are distinct provisions under review; their validity cannot be decided merely from approval or rejection of the zones. The statutory route and unsettled jurisdictional predicates in the neutral baseline remain controlling; this proposal does not assume them away.

**Proposed disposition:** Vacate the listener-triggered counseling prohibition; preserve the independently lawful physical-access and objective noise restrictions.

**Governing law:** Independent First Amendment and Rule 65(d) analysis, with proper review of underlying findings and remedial discretion. A public listener may decline communication and proceed freely, but objection to the message alone is not a censor’s authority. Objective prohibitions on physical interference, coercive following, and medically dangerous noise serve different interests from protection against offense.

Boos v. Barry, 485 U.S. 312 (1988), rejects protection from political offense as a general suppression interest. Madsen supplies the burden/necessity rule for this injunction. First Amendment; Fed. R. Civ. P. 65(d); state-law access and trespass protections.

**Legal relationship:** Applying binding law.

**Proposed holding:** An injunction can secure free passage and safe treatment without making rejection of a message sufficient to prohibit otherwise protected public advocacy.

**Legal reasoning:** The listener-triggered command presents an independent defect. People must remain free to leave, refuse literature, and obtain treatment without being physically impeded. The decree can forbid blocking, coercive following, and harassment defined through objective conduct. It cannot simply make an unwilling listener the final censor of otherwise protected public advocacy. The initial permission to speak does not cure an unjustified prohibition on all further counseling after rejection.

Stone should retain the objective noise restriction and physical-access commands. Protecting political speech is not a reason to strip clinics of enforceable protection against actual interference. The appropriate remedy modifies the overbroad portions rather than dissolving the entire decree. Nor should the court resolve newly suggested claims under later-enacted federal legislation that were not the source of the appealed injunction.

**Independent or alternative considerations:** No additional rule is proposed beyond the interpretation and application stated here.

**Preferred scope:** An injunction can secure free passage and safe treatment without making rejection of a message sufficient to prohibit otherwise protected public advocacy. The lawful outer boundary is the issue and remedial limit stated here, not a broader rule about unpresented facts.

**Fallback position:** Proposed: decide the cessation provision as written and reserve narrower conduct-based restrictions. The case-level nonjoin limits in Component 1 apply; no trade in accuracy or jurisdiction is authorized.

**Remedy and remand:** Modify the invalid speech term, not the entire injunction; retain objectively defined access and safety commands.

**Reserved questions:** A properly supported restriction on objectively coercive following or harassment, and particular enforcement questions not presented.
