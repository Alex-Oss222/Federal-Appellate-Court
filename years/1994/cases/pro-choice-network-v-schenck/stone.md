# Pro-Choice Network of Western New York v. Schenck

**Status:** Version 1; proposed, not user-approved. **Approval reference:** None. The source brief states that every Stone position, alternative, and negotiating limit is a proposal pending approval.

## Stone's proposed judicial position

Stone should propose affirmance of the injunction's core prohibitions and of the decision not to dissolve it wholesale after *Bray*, while vacating the fifteen-foot speech exclusions and the cease-and-desist restriction on this record. The mandate should permit a renewed, separately justified fixed access zone if the district court identifies facts showing that a narrower obstruction prohibition cannot adequately protect the actual entrances and approaches. He should not declare fixed zones inherently invalid or make a previously violated injunction an indispensable legal prerequisite to every preventive remedy.

His starting point should be that neither the clinics' lawful medical work nor the protesters' religious and political advocacy is optional in the legal analysis. The relevant state-law rights support protection against trespass and interference; the First Amendment protects peaceful communication in traditional public forums. No hybrid abortion-and-speech doctrine gives one side an automatic advantage. *Madsen* supplies the governing test.

On the existing findings, the moving restriction captures too much conduct disconnected from access. A counselor standing or walking without blocking another person may be forced away as the protected person moves. The government must justify that actual burden, not substitute the proposition that some members of the group previously obstructed patients. An injunction may reach persons acting in concert with wrongdoers under Rule 65, but association does not erase the requirement that the prohibited conduct be adequately connected to the violation.

The listener-triggered command presents an independent defect. People must remain free to leave, refuse literature, and obtain treatment without being physically impeded. The decree can forbid blocking, coercive following, and harassment defined through objective conduct. It cannot simply make an unwilling listener the final censor of otherwise protected public advocacy. The initial permission to speak does not cure an unjustified prohibition on all further counseling after rejection.

A fixed entrance zone warrants a more cautious explanation. Doorways and vehicle entrances can require preventive clearance; the existence of peaceful events under the earlier order does not disprove every need for such clearance. Stone should therefore rest the present vacatur on insufficiently differentiated findings and scope, not announce that a district judge must allow another blockade before acting. A renewed decree must distinguish a stationary access corridor from an exclusion surrounding every moving person, consider whether passage remains genuinely possible, and explain why the selected dimensions are necessary. Those are applications of *Madsen*, not a judicially invented permanent footage rule.

Stone should retain the objective noise restriction and physical-access commands. Protecting political speech is not a reason to strip clinics of enforceable protection against actual interference. The appropriate remedy modifies the overbroad portions rather than dissolving the entire decree. Nor should the court resolve newly suggested claims under later-enacted federal legislation that were not the source of the appealed injunction. Master Jurisprudence §§ 6, 9, 25; Speech; Personal Liberty; Remedies.

## Proposed negotiating limits

**Preferred and broadest defensible holding:** remove the unsupported moving exclusions and listener-controlled censorship, vacate the present fixed restriction for a distinct necessity analysis, and preserve all independently valid access, trespass, and health-related noise provisions.

**Acceptable narrower proposal:** invalidate the moving zones and cease-and-desist provision while remanding only the fixed-zone portion for findings under *Madsen*. A properly supported fixed entrance restriction is legally acceptable in principle; this is not approval of the present fifteen-foot requirement or of facts not found.

**Non-join limits:** a right to obstruct access, a categorical ban on all buffer zones, an immunity from review because abortion access is important, a listener's veto based on disagreement, or treating all anti-abortion advocacy as inherently threatening or unprotected conduct.
