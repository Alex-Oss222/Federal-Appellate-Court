Subsequent loss of that claim does not automatically strip jurisdiction over a substantially developed common nucleus of state claims; judicial economy, convenience, fairness, and comity guide retention. Section 1367 is not retroactively treated as the original source for the September 1990 filing.

**Legal reasoning:** Gibbs and Cohill preserve a discretionary judgment after the federal claim drops away. The existing access dispute, record, and investment of judicial resources provide lawful reasons for retention. **Independent or alternative considerations:** No additional rule is proposed beyond the interpretation and application stated here.

**Preferred scope:** The loss of the federal theory did not mechanically require termination of the developed pendent state litigation. The lawful outer boundary is the issue and remedial limit stated here, not a broader rule about unpresented facts.

Case-level approved scope: remove the unsupported moving exclusions and listener-controlled censorship, vacate the present fixed restriction for a distinct necessity analysis, and preserve all independently valid commands protecting clinics and patients from obstruction, trespass, interference with entrances and driveways, and objectively harmful noise. Permit the district court on remand, if properly presented and supported, to consider a narrower, objectively defined restriction on deliberate unwanted close approaches after a clear refusal. ### Component 2

**Component:** Fixed and moving fifteen-foot restrictions

**Jurisdiction and reach:** The challenged zones form part of the injunction under review; Madsen was decided during the appeal and addressed in supplemental submissions. The statutory route and unsettled jurisdictional predicates in the neutral baseline remain controlling; this proposal does not assume them away.

**Proposed disposition:** Vacate the current fifteen-foot speech exclusions, with a distinct opportunity to justify a narrower fixed entrance restriction. On a sufficient record, the district court may also consider a separate, more limited rule against knowingly continuing a deliberate close approach for individualized counseling after an unambiguous refusal. Stone does not approve any eight-foot distance on the existing record.

**Governing law:** Independent constitutional review of the legal burden; clear error for historical findings; abuse of discretion for equitable choices within the correct speech standard. A content-neutral injunction may burden no more speech than necessary to serve a significant governmental interest. Fixed access corridors and zones moving around people or vehicles impose different burdens and require separate necessity analysis. A nominal eight-foot personal bubble remains a moving proximity restriction even if it operates only within a fixed geographic clinic-access area; its smaller size and a refusal trigger do not exempt it from *Madsen* or Rule 65(d). *Madsen* rejected an inadequately justified broad no-approach and consent requirement; the distance cannot be shortened to eight feet without independently answering the same First Amendment objections.

Madsen v. Women’s Health Center, Inc., 512 U.S. 753, 764-66 (1994), supplies the controlling injunction standard and recognizes an entrance-access restriction on its own record. First Amendment; Fed. R. Civ. P. 65(d); the supported state-law prohibitions on obstruction and trespass. A prior violation may support necessity but is not a categorical prerequisite to every preventive restriction.

**Legal relationship:** Applying binding law.

**Proposed holding:** The moving fifteen-foot exclusion’s actual speech burden is insufficiently tied to access on this record, and the fixed restriction requires its own necessity findings rather than the same undifferentiated rationale. Whether a narrower, refusal-triggered close-approach restriction could be imposed is left for a properly supported remand, not decided as a freestanding eight-foot holding.

**Legal reasoning:** Stone should propose affirmance of the injunction's core prohibitions and of the decision not to dissolve it wholesale after *Bray*, while vacating the fifteen-foot speech exclusions and the cease-and-desist restriction on this record. The mandate should permit a renewed, separately justified fixed access zone if the district court identifies facts showing that a narrower obstruction prohibition cannot adequately protect the actual entrances and approaches. He should not declare fixed zones inherently invalid or make a previously violated injunction an indispensable legal prerequisite to every preventive remedy.

His starting point should be that neither the clinics' lawful medical work nor the protesters' religious and political advocacy is optional in the legal analysis. The relevant state-law rights support protection against trespass and interference; the First Amendment protects peaceful communication in traditional public forums. No hybrid abortion-and-speech doctrine gives one side an automatic advantage. *Madsen* supplies the governing test.

On the existing findings, the moving restriction captures too much conduct disconnected from access. A counselor standing or walking without blocking another person may be forced away as the protected person moves. The government must justify that actual burden, not substitute the proposition that some members of the group previously obstructed patients. An injunction may reach persons acting in concert with wrongdoers under Rule 65, but association does not erase the requirement that the prohibited conduct be adequately connected to the violation.

A fixed entrance zone warrants a more cautious explanation. Doorways and vehicle entrances can require preventive clearance; the existence of peaceful events under the earlier order does not disprove every need for such clearance. Stone should therefore rest the present vacatur on insufficiently differentiated findings and scope, not announce that a district judge must allow another blockade before acting. A renewed decree must distinguish a stationary access corridor from an exclusion surrounding every moving person, consider whether passage remains genuinely possible, and explain why the selected dimensions are necessary. Those are applications of *Madsen*, not a judicially invented permanent footage rule.

Stone also distinguishes a fixed outer geographic area from an individualized moving restriction used within it. Suppose a district court identifies particular approaches to a clinic where unwanted close pursuit has occurred. A possible narrower remedy would prohibit an enjoined counselor, after hearing a clear refusal of further individualized contact, from knowingly moving toward or following that person to continue the encounter within a specified close distance. An eight-foot radius is a proposed example, not a number derived from the present record or from a constitutional entitlement to personal space. The court would have to find that this particular distance and trigger are necessary, decide whose conduct is covered, define the geographic area and notice requirements, and consider less speech-restrictive means.

That narrower provision would regulate deliberate, unwanted approach and pursuit rather than the content of advocacy. It would not require silence, prevent speech from a stationary lawful location, prohibit general public advocacy, punish an incidental encounter, or force a demonstrator to move away merely because the patient walks toward or past the demonstrator. Nor would the words “stop harassing me” automatically convert all subsequent speech into harassment. Stone’s protection of the patient’s ability to disengage is a limit on coercive conduct that must be independently supported under governing law, not a listener-controlled power to suppress ideas.

**Independent or alternative considerations:** The refusal-triggered close-approach restriction is a newly proposed, conditional remedial alternative for possible consideration on remand. It is not treated as an existing holding of Madsen, a current factual finding, or a presently valid eight-foot zone.

**Preferred scope:** Invalidate the existing moving speech exclusion and vacate the fixed exclusion for its own necessity analysis. Leave the district court room to examine a properly presented, objectively defined restriction on continued close approaches after a clear refusal, only if a sufficient record demonstrates that it burdens no more speech than necessary. No general personal-space constitutional right, permanent footage rule, or listener veto is adopted.

**Fallback position:** Approved as available, not selected: invalidate the moving zones and cease-and-desist provision while remanding only the fixed-zone portion for findings under *Madsen*. A properly supported fixed entrance restriction is legally acceptable in principle; this is not approval of the present fifteen-foot requirement or of facts not found. The case-level nonjoin limits in Component 1 apply; no trade in accuracy or jurisdiction is authorized.

**Remedy and remand:** Preserve all independently valid prohibitions against obstruction, trespass, interference with clinic approaches, and medically harmful noise. Permit a renewed fixed access zone only on a supported, differentiated application of *Madsen*. If properly raised, allow the district court to consider, on adequate findings and with Rule 65(d) specificity, a geographically limited and refusal-triggered restriction on deliberate close approach or pursuit. A proposed eight-foot distance cannot be imposed merely because it sounds moderate; its actual operation and necessity must be established, and the narrower terms must genuinely differ from the invalid blanket consent requirement.

**Reserved questions:** Whether any eight-foot or other distance is necessary on an adequate record; the precise trigger, covered conduct, defendants, location, duration, and enforcement terms of a possible close-approach provision; no categorical prohibition on fixed access zones, no permanent footage rule, and no requirement to await another blockade.

**Separate writing:** Proposed only. A separate opinion would defend or delimit this proposition: The existing moving restriction burdens too much protected speech, but a narrowly confined prohibition on deliberate close pursuit after an express refusal is not necessarily equivalent to a general moving exclusion or a listener veto. The fixed restriction requires its own necessity findings, and neither form may be sustained by an invented eight-foot rule. It would explain why the authorities and record support the disposition stated above and answer the contrary position addressed in the legal reasoning. A concurrence in the judgment is proposed only if the court adopts an independently accepted narrower ground; a dissent or partial dissent would be appropriate only if the eventual judgment or reasoning conflicts with the stated legal limits. ### Component 3

**Component:** Listener-triggered cessation, noise, and physical-access commands

**Jurisdiction and reach:** The cease-and-desist requirement and noise/access terms are distinct provisions under review; their validity cannot be decided merely from approval or rejection of the zones. The statutory route and unsettled jurisdictional predicates in the neutral baseline remain controlling; this proposal does not assume them away.

**Proposed disposition:** Vacate the listener-triggered prohibition on further counseling as written; preserve independently lawful physical-access and objective noise restrictions. Do not foreclose a distinct, sufficiently justified rule requiring enjoined persons to stop knowingly pursuing or deliberately approaching an unwilling patient at close range after a clear refusal.

**Governing law:** Independent First Amendment and Rule 65(d) analysis, with proper review of underlying findings and remedial discretion. A public listener may decline communication and proceed freely, but objection to the message alone is not a censor’s authority. Objective prohibitions on physical interference, coercive following, and medically dangerous noise serve different interests from protection against offense. A clear refusal can be relevant to whether subsequent knowing close pursuit is unwanted, but it cannot alone justify silencing otherwise lawful expression.

Boos v. Barry, 485 U.S. 312 (1988), rejects protection from political offense as a general suppression interest. Madsen supplies the burden/necessity rule for this injunction. First Amendment; Fed. R. Civ. P. 65(d); state-law access and trespass protections.

**Legal relationship:** Applying binding law.

**Proposed holding:** An injunction can secure free passage and safe treatment without making rejection of a message sufficient to prohibit otherwise protected public advocacy. A rule addressing deliberate unwanted close approach after a clear refusal presents a different tailoring question that is not resolved by sustaining or invalidating the existing blanket cease-and-desist command.

**Legal reasoning:** The listener-triggered command presents an independent defect. People must remain free to leave, refuse literature, and obtain treatment without being physically impeded. The decree can forbid blocking, coercive following, and harassment defined through objective conduct. It cannot simply make an unwilling listener the final censor of otherwise protected public advocacy. The initial permission to speak does not cure an unjustified prohibition on all further counseling after rejection.

Stone distinguishes the patient’s right to end a close personal encounter from a supposed right to stop all public speech. A clearly communicated “no,” “leave me alone,” or “stop approaching me” can give notice that a deliberate renewed close approach or persistent pursuit is unwanted. If the record establishes the need and Madsen is satisfied, a decree could require the enjoined counselor to stop that specified approach or pursuit. It could not forbid the counselor to express the same viewpoint from a lawful stationary position, communicate generally with the public, or remain where the patient chooses to walk past without obstruction. Consent to a conversation is not required for every instance of public speech, and a patient’s refusal is not a general cancellation of First Amendment rights.

A hypothetical rule against knowingly approaching within eight feet after a clear refusal would need an objectively defined trigger, demonstrable necessity for that exact distance and covered geography, rules about incidental proximity, and enforcement language that does not turn disagreement into contempt. A fixed geographic limit on where the rule operates does not change the moving nature of an eight-foot person-centered radius. The court may consider such a measure on remand; Stone cannot treat it as proved or presently imposed.

Stone should retain independently valid objective noise restrictions, commands against trespass and entrance obstruction, and physical-access and safety protections for patients, medical staff, and clinics. Patients can refuse contact, proceed to care without being blocked, and seek enforcement of lawful prohibitions on harassment defined by objective unlawful conduct. Protecting political speech is not a reason to strip clinics of enforceable protection against actual interference. The appropriate remedy modifies the overbroad portions rather than dissolving the entire decree. Nor should the court resolve newly suggested claims under later-enacted federal legislation that were not the source of the appealed injunction.

**Independent or alternative considerations:** Proposed remedial distinction: a refusal by itself does not prohibit speech, but a knowing, deliberate close approach to continue unwanted individualized counseling after a clear refusal may be restricted if objective terms, the record, and Madsen support it. The hypothetical eight-foot boundary is not adopted as a holding.

**Preferred scope:** Invalidate the existing listener-controlled prohibition and preserve independent access and medical-noise protections. Leave the district court free to consider a properly justified, conduct-based rule against continued close pursuit after refusal without deciding that every refusal creates an enforceable personal bubble.

**Fallback position:** Approved as available, not selected: decide only the cessation provision as written and expressly reserve the constitutionality of any future refusal-triggered close-approach rule. The case-level nonjoin limits in Component 1 apply; no trade in accuracy or jurisdiction is authorized.

**Remedy and remand:** Modify the invalid speech term, not the entire injunction; retain all independently valid and objectively defined clinic-access, anti-trespass, physical-safety, and medically justified noise commands. If the district court considers a replacement, it must identify the proven conduct, justify any geographic and distance limits, and write a command that enjoined parties can understand and obey. If a refusal-triggered term cannot be justified under *Madsen*, Stone preserves the other lawful injunction provisions instead of treating the clinics as unprotected.

**Reserved questions:** Whether evidence supports restricting an intentional approach within eight feet or any other particular distance after a clear refusal; whether narrower conduct-based restrictions would suffice; particular enforcement questions not presented.

**Separate writing:** Proposed only. A separate opinion would defend or delimit this proposition: A patient may refuse a close personal approach without acquiring a veto over peaceful public advocacy, and a court may regulate proven close pursuit through specific, narrowly tailored terms rather than through a blanket cease-and-desist order. It would explain why the authorities and record support the disposition stated above and answer the contrary position addressed in the legal reasoning. A concurrence in the judgment is proposed only if the court adopts an independently accepted narrower ground; a dissent or partial dissent would be appropriate only if the eventual judgment or reasoning conflicts with the stated legal limits. No panel alignment, author assignment, or agreement to a particular separate opinion is established.
