## Supreme Court merits decision, if any

*Schenck v. Pro-Choice Network of Western New York*, 519 U.S. 357 (1997); No. 95-1065; argued 1996-10-16, decided 1997-02-19; Rehnquist, C.J. Question: whether the injunction's fifteen-foot fixed zones, floating zones around people and vehicles, and cease-and-desist rule for sidewalk counselors violated the First Amendment under *Madsen*. Holding: the fixed buffer zones around doorways, driveways and driveway entrances, and the cease-and-desist limit inside them, are valid; the floating zones are invalid because they burden more speech than necessary (Parts II-B and II-D joined by Stevens, O'Connor, Souter, Ginsburg, Breyer; Scalia, Kennedy, Thomas concurring in part and dissenting in part; Breyer concurring in part and dissenting in part). Effect: the en banc judgment, which had affirmed all three, was affirmed as to the fixed zones and cease-and-desist and reversed as to the floating zones, and remanded.

## Dependence on the historical judgment

- **Rehearing in banc:** sought by plaintiffs to restore two provisions. It depended on the panel's majority holding invalidating them; a panel judgment sustaining both would give plaintiffs nothing to seek, and defendants would be the possible movants.
- **Certiorari, grant, argument and decision:** sought by defendants against the en banc affirmance of the buffer-zone and cease-and-desist provisions, and the Court's ruling answers those provisions as an en banc court had left them. They depend on the Second Circuit having finally sustained the fifteen-foot restrictions, including the floating zones. If a differently decided judgment invalidated fewer or more provisions, the petition, the grant, and the disposition's partial affirmance may not fit.
- As general law, *Schenck* governs from 1997-02-19 regardless of the path taken.
