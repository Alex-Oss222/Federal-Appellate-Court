## Supreme Court merits decision, if any

*Schenck v. Pro-Choice Network of Western New York*, 519 U.S. 357 (1997); No. 95-1065; argued 1996-10-16, decided 1997-02-19; Rehnquist, C.J. Question: whether the injunction's fixed fifteen-foot zones, floating zones around people and vehicles, and cease-and-desist rule for sidewalk counselors violate the First Amendment under *Madsen*. Holding: the fixed zones at doorways, driveways and driveway entrances, and the cease-and-desist limit within them, are valid; the floating zones burden more speech than necessary and are invalid. Scalia (with Kennedy and Thomas) and Breyer concurred and dissented in part. Effect: the en banc judgment, which had affirmed all three features, was affirmed as to the fixed zones and cease-and-desist and reversed as to the floating zones, and remanded.

## Dependence on the historical judgment

- **Rehearing in banc:** sought by plaintiffs to restore two provisions; it depended on the panel majority having invalidated both. A panel judgment sustaining them would give plaintiffs nothing to seek.
- **En banc decision, certiorari, grant, decision:** the petition came from defendants against the en banc affirmance of the buffer-zone and cease-and-desist provisions. The questions on the floating zones arose only after the panel decision, at en banc argument (Breyer, J., 519 U.S. at 395, 399). These steps depend on a Second Circuit that finally sustained the fifteen-foot restrictions; a different panel outcome on either provision may leave the petition, the grant, or the partial affirmance without a fit.
- As general law, *Schenck* governs from 1997-02-19 regardless of path.
