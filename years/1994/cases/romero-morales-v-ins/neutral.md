## Record and issues

Romero-Morales, a Honduran national, entered without inspection in September 1992. Represented at an October hearing, he conceded deportability and received a November 16 setting for an asylum application. Released shortly before that date, he traveled to New York, where relatives could provide support and help secure counsel. New counsel sent appearance, venue, and asylum papers shortly before the hearing. The papers arrived at Harlingen, while the hearing was at the detention facility in Los Fresnos. The IJ knew papers had been submitted but proceeded in absentia and did not allow prior counsel to explain the circumstances fully. ([Law Resource](https://law.resource.org/pub/us/case/reporter/F3/025/25.F3d.125.93-4205.1214.html))

**Jurisdiction:** The historical review route is former 8 U.S.C. § 1105a, with the special in absentia provisions of former § 1252b. The motion to reopen was filed within the statutory 180-day period. Exact petition and administrative-order dates should be verified from the docket. **Preservation:** The nonappearance explanation, venue request, counsel circumstances, and reopening standard were before the agency. The merits of asylum were not adjudicated. **Missing findings:** Actual financial inability, communications and advice from counsel, ability to travel, and the circumstances in which the documents were directed to Harlingen. ## Law entering the event

**Cutoff:** May 27, 1994. **Simulation holdings:** None supplied.

### Issue 1: Reopening and exceptional circumstances

**Review and governing test:** Denial of reopening is reviewed for abuse of discretion; statutory interpretation is reviewed under the then-applicable agency-deference framework. Former § 1252b required the government’s prescribed clear, unequivocal, and convincing showing concerning notice and deportability for an in absentia order. Reopening within 180 days required the alien to establish exceptional circumstances beyond his control. The statute’s severe-illness and death examples did not eliminate the need to evaluate the actual explanation. ([Law Resource](https://law.resource.org/pub/us/case/reporter/F3/025/25.F3d.125.93-4205.1214.html))

**Supreme Court and circuit authority:** *Chevron*, 467 U.S. 837 (1984), and *Reno v. Flores*, 507 U.S. 292 (1993), frame the historical allocation of interpretive responsibility. *Anderson v. McElroy*, 953 F.2d 803 (2d Cir. 1992), requires a reasoned exercise of discretion on the material circumstances. No directly controlling Supreme Court holding resolves this factual combination. ([Law Resource](https://law.resource.org/pub/us/case/reporter/F3/025/25.F3d.125.93-4205.1214.html))

**Positions:** The INS stresses actual notice and the absence of an approved venue change. Romero-Morales stresses indigence, prompt efforts to obtain counsel, submission of an asylum application, and the IJ’s refusal to hear an explanation. Filing a venue motion does not automatically excuse attendance; conversely, knowing the scheduled date does not by itself resolve whether circumstances beyond the alien’s control prevented attendance.

**Paths:** Uphold denial if the agency applied the proper standard to supported findings. Otherwise remand for findings and a reasoned determination. The court need not resolve every possible relationship between the older “reasonable cause” language and the newer exceptional-circumstances provision to require adequate consideration of the record.

### Issue 2: Counsel, venue, and meaningful opportunity to explain

**Governing law:** The statutory opportunity for retained counsel and the regulations governing good-cause continuances and venue changes apply independently of the asylum merits. *Montilla v. INS*, 926 F.2d 162 (2d Cir. 1991), and *Augustin v. Sava*, 735 F.2d 32 (2d Cir. 1984), require attention to the procedural protections actually provided. *Ungar v. Sarafite*, 376 U.S. 575 (1964), rejects a mechanical approach to continuance questions. ([Law Resource](https://law.resource.org/pub/us/case/reporter/F3/025/25.F3d.125.93-4205.1214.html))

**Positions and reach:** Romero-Morales argues that preventing counsel from explaining the pending papers distorted the very inquiry needed to decide nonappearance. The INS emphasizes orderly proceedings and the absence of an automatic right to relocate a hearing.

**Remedy constraints:** A procedural defect supports renewed consideration under the governing statute; it does not establish asylum eligibility, excuse every unapproved move, or compel a favorable exceptional-circumstances finding.
