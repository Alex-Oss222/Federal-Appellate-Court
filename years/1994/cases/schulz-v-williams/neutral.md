## Record and issues

The Libertarian candidates submitted 17,234 signatures for statewide nomination, against a 15,000-signature requirement. The board found 10,305 valid. The challenged requirements included election-district, assembly-district, and ward information; other defects also affected the petition. Candidates first brought a state proceeding challenging the board’s jurisdiction through service objections. That proceeding ended adversely before the federal merits judgment. ([OpenJurist](https://openjurist.org/44/f3d/48/schulz-v-williams))

A federal preliminary order was vacated on an earlier appeal for lack of an evidentiary hearing. After the October hearing, the district court granted a permanent injunction providing an additional three-day opportunity and relief from the district-number requirements. The record also concerned a separate statute providing free voter-registration lists to recognized parties but not independent bodies. Evidence addressed the time spent obtaining district data and the practical assistance those lists would provide. ([OpenJurist](https://openjurist.org/44/f3d/48/schulz-v-williams))

**Jurisdiction:** Final injunctive relief is reviewable under §§ 1291 and 1292(a)(1), but appellants require Article III standing because the state defendants did not appeal. **Preservation:** Standing, preclusion, both statutory challenges, and remedy are presented. **Related proceedings:** The September state judgment and the October appellate hearing-remand order are pre-entry history. **Missing material:** Full petitions, state pleadings, October trial record, list-access evidence, and the original injunction. Future election results are excluded from the neutral record.

## Law entering the event

**Cutoff:** November 2, 1994. **Effective simulation holdings:** None supplied. The earlier hearing-remand order is preserved as procedural history, not as a holding that strict scrutiny governs the merits.

### Issue 1: Intervenor standing and proper defendants

**Review and law:** Standing is reviewed independently. *Diamond v. Charles*, 476 U.S. 54 (1986), holds that intervention alone does not establish standing to maintain an appeal. *Lujan v. Defenders of Wildlife*, 504 U.S. 555 (1992), requires concrete injury, causation, and redressability. *Fulani v. League of Women Voters*, 882 F.2d 621 (2d Cir. 1989), addresses competitive electoral injury. ([OpenJurist](https://openjurist.org/44/f3d/48/schulz-v-williams))

**Positions:** Long identifies competitive injury to Conservative Party candidates and the party’s continued recognized status. Plaintiffs contest whether the intervenors have a legally sufficient personal stake. The affidavit must support actual competitive injury, not generalized concern with lawful elections.

**Paths:** Reach the merits if at least one appellant has standing for the relief sought; otherwise dismiss. The board members’ enforcement responsibilities must independently support prospective relief against them. The presence of political stakes does not dispense with either inquiry.

### Issue 2: Claim preclusion

**Law:** Under 28 U.S.C. § 1738, *Migra v. Warren City School District*, 465 U.S. 75 (1984), and New York transactional-preclusion law, a state judgment can bar claims that could have been raised, including constitutional ones. *Golkin v. Abrams*, 803 F.2d 55 (2d Cir. 1986), is relevant circuit authority. ([OpenJurist](https://openjurist.org/44/f3d/48/schulz-v-williams))

**Positions:** Intervenors argue that the prior election proceeding provided a forum for the federal theories. Schulz disputes the adequacy or scope of that litigation. The other plaintiffs emphasize that they were not parties and that shared political interests do not automatically establish privity.

**Paths:** Determine preclusion plaintiff by plaintiff. Schulz’s participation can bar his claim without necessarily binding the voter plaintiffs or the party. Do not merge standing and preclusion or infer control of the state case without evidence.

### Issue 3: District-information requirements under § 6-140

**Governing law:** *Anderson v. Celebrezze*, 460 U.S. 780 (1983), *Burdick v. Takushi*, 504 U.S. 428 (1992), and *Norman v. Reed*, 502 U.S. 279 (1992), require evaluation of the character and magnitude of the burden against the state’s asserted interests. Severe burdens require correspondingly demanding justification; reasonable, nondiscriminatory administration receives greater latitude. The inquiry is not automatically strict scrutiny or unstructured rational-basis review. ([OpenJurist](https://openjurist.org/44/f3d/48/schulz-v-williams))

**Positions:** Plaintiffs emphasize testimony that obtaining district numbers consumed much of petitioning time and that independent bodies lacked free lists. Intervenors emphasize verification, the 42-day petition window, the support threshold, and evidence that independent petitions regularly succeeded.

**Open application and paths:** Assess actual combined operation without assuming that a modest signature percentage proves practical access. Uphold reasonable requirements supported by administrative interests; invalidate a burden only under the correct balancing framework and evidentiary record. A failure of the list-distribution statute does not automatically make every petition-information requirement unconstitutional.

### Issue 4: Unequal free-list distribution under § 5-602

**Governing authority:** *Socialist Workers Party v. Rockefeller*, 314 F. Supp. 984 (S.D.N.Y. 1970), summarily affirmed, 400 U.S. 806 (1970), addressed materially similar preferential distribution. The Supreme Court’s summary action binds on the precise issues necessarily decided, not every possible election-list dispute. ([OpenJurist](https://openjurist.org/44/f3d/48/schulz-v-williams))

**Positions:** Plaintiffs argue that giving a practical petitioning resource free to recognized parties while withholding it from independent bodies is unjustified unequal assistance. Intervenors distinguish party status and administrative arrangements. Renumbering or reenacting materially identical language does not itself eliminate the earlier decision’s force.

**Paths and limits:** Enjoin the unequal treatment if the earlier holding controls. Keep separate any enrollment-list provision not properly adjudicated. The court must identify the discrimination actually proved and the officials capable of remedying it.

### Issue 5: Relief despite an alternative merits ground

**Review and law:** Review the permanent injunction for abuse of discretion under correct legal premises. Circuit equitable-remedy cases require a fit between injury and relief. *McCarthy v. Briscoe*, 429 U.S. 1317 (1976), is an in-chambers opinion, not a full-Court merits holding; its relevance to support evidence must be described accordingly. ([OpenJurist](https://openjurist.org/44/f3d/48/schulz-v-williams))

**Positions:** Intervenors argue that invalidating the district-information theory removes the injunction’s foundation. Plaintiffs contend that the lost list access already impaired petitioning and that prospective equal distribution alone would not repair the completed injury.

**Paths:** Sustain appropriately tailored temporary petition relief on an independently valid ground if the record establishes the connection. Otherwise modify or remand. Do not guarantee ballot placement without required support or use later election results to justify the earlier injunction.
