## Record and issues

The Goldmans invested in Midcontinent Drilling Associates-II and deducted partnership losses in 1981 and 1982. The offering memorandum advertised deductions several times the cash investment, warned of likely IRS scrutiny, and described an unproven drilling technology. Their accountant, Stephen Burr, lacked oil-and-gas expertise, relied on the same memorandum, and received a sales commission. These are material to reasonable reliance, not merely evidence that the investment ultimately failed. ([Law Resource](https://law.resource.org/pub/us/case/reporter/F3/039/39.F3d.402.94-4027.242.html))

An IRS settlement ultimately memorialized on Form 870-AD addressed the 1981 year. It allowed the initial cash investment as a deduction and specified that year’s liability. The taxpayers contended that the settlement also resolved later years. The Commissioner disallowed the 1982 deduction. The opinion’s reference to signing an investment agreement in “December 1991” conflicts with its repeated 1981 investment chronology; the exact signing date remains a source discrepancy rather than an invented correction. ([Law Resource](https://law.resource.org/pub/us/case/reporter/F3/039/39.F3d.402.94-4027.242.html))

**Jurisdiction:** The opinion expressly identifies a timely appeal under 26 U.S.C. §§ 7482(a) and 7483. **Preservation:** Settlement scope and negligence are contested. Enhanced interest under § 6621(c) was not independently challenged on appeal; the Commissioner had conceded the § 6659 addition below. **Missing material:** Original settlement instruments, Tax Court trial record, and precise decision date.

## Law entering the event

**Cutoff:** November 2, 1994. **Simulation holdings:** None supplied. The substantive additions are those applicable to the 1982 tax year.

### Issue 1: Settlement scope and official authority

**Review and law:** Findings about agreement formation receive clear-error review; interpretation of unambiguous terms is de novo. *Sullivan v. Commissioner*, 985 F.2d 704 (2d Cir. 1993), and *Goodheart Clothing v. Laura Goodman Enterprises*, 962 F.2d 268 (2d Cir. 1992), supply the relevant review and contract principles. *Dorl v. Commissioner*, 507 F.2d 406 (2d Cir. 1974), limits reliance on assurances by officials without compromise authority. Section 7121’s closing-agreement framework matters to any asserted multiyear resolution. ([Law Resource](https://law.resource.org/pub/us/case/reporter/F3/039/39.F3d.402.94-4027.242.html))

**Positions:** The taxpayers rely on the correspondence and its references to later deductions. The Commissioner distinguishes a condition barring future partnership deductions from a promise forgiving liability for future years. The final form expressly identifies 1981, and the officer’s assignment did not establish authority for the broader compromise alleged.

**Paths:** Enforce the supported scope of the agreement. Do not convert a settlement condition concerning other years into a release of those years. No directly controlling Supreme Court decision requires the taxpayers’ reading of this instrument.

### Issue 2: Negligence and professional reliance

**Review, test, and burden:** Negligence findings receive clear-error review. Under the applicable § 6653 provisions, the taxpayers bear the burden of overcoming the determination of lack of reasonable care. The inquiry is objective and circumstance-specific. *United States v. Boyle*, 469 U.S. 241 (1985), informs reasonable professional reliance but arose in the distinct context of filing responsibility. ([Law Resource](https://law.resource.org/pub/us/case/reporter/F3/039/39.F3d.402.94-4027.242.html))

**Positions:** The Goldmans point to their review of the memorandum, Burr’s advice, and an accounting firm’s later Schedule K-1 transmission. The Commissioner emphasizes the extraordinary deductions, disclosed warning signs, Burr’s commission and lack of expertise, and the transmittal’s disclaimer. The Tax Court’s *Wright* decision, issued June 23, 1994, is available persuasive authority but involved materially greater investigation.

**Lawful paths and limits:** Sustain negligence where the finding reasonably follows from the actual warning signs and reliance evidence. Reverse or remand if the court treated any unsuccessful shelter investment as automatically negligent. The source does not support a universal rule that all compensated advisers are disqualified; the commission, known role, expertise, and independent work matter together.
