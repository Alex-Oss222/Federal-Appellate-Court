# Karibian v. Columbia University

**Status:** Version 1; proposed, not user-approved. The present request authorizes preparation, not approval of a case-specific vote. **Baseline:** The January 25, 1994 neutral packet. **Conditions:** The participation conditions above. **Approved narrower join, fallback, or separate writing:** None supplied.

**Component: Federal harassment claims.**  
**Jurisdiction and reach:** The preserved Title VII claims under § 1291, excluding abandoned retaliation. **Proposed vote:** Reverse summary judgment and remand. **Legal relationship:** Apply *Meritor*, *Harris*, and the circuit’s agency cases; resolve the open application to submission obtained through supervisory authority.

**Proposed holding and reasoning:** An employee does not lose protection by submitting to an employment condition that Title VII forbids. Likewise, an employer’s lack of notice cannot by itself defeat liability when delegated authority enabled the supervisor to impose that condition. Karibian must still prove unwelcome conduct, the relevant employment nexus, and the requirements of each harassment theory. Columbia remains free to contest her account and Urban’s authority at trial. This proposal follows Stone’s insistence that summary judgment not resolve credibility and that statutory protection retain its enacted operation. ([Law Resource](https://law.resource.org/pub/us/case/reporter/F3/014/14.F3d.773.93-7188.513.html))

**Component: State claims and remedy.**  
Restore the state claims for consideration with the federal claims. Do not direct liability, damages, or a finding that the later layoff was retaliatory. The remand should identify the rejected economic-loss prerequisite and require distinct analysis of agency-based and notice-based employer responsibility.
