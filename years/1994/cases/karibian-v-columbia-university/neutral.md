## Record and issues

**Material record:** Karibian worked in Columbia’s Telefund operation while attending the university. Urban occupied a supervisory position with influence over assignments, schedules, raises, and promotions. Their sexual relationship is conceded; its voluntariness is disputed. Karibian alleges that Urban tied favorable employment treatment to sexual compliance and that she submitted because she feared adverse consequences. Her account includes coercive and violent conduct. These are allegations and summary-judgment evidence, not appellate findings. ([Law Resource](https://law.resource.org/pub/us/case/reporter/F3/014/14.F3d.773.93-7188.513.html))

Karibian made confidential complaints to university personnel in 1988. The confidentiality requests and the university’s response are material to notice-based liability, but they do not resolve whether Urban used delegated authority to accomplish the harassment. After a nonconfidential complaint in January 1990, Columbia removed Urban’s supervisory responsibilities and obtained his resignation. Telefund later closed, and Karibian was laid off. The district court treated the absence of economic loss as fatal to quid pro quo liability and found insufficient grounds for notice-based employer liability. ([Law Resource](https://law.resource.org/pub/us/case/reporter/F3/014/14.F3d.773.93-7188.513.html))

**Jurisdiction:** Review of the final district-court disposition lies under 28 U.S.C. § 1291; the underlying federal claim arises under Title VII. ## Law entering the event

### Issue 1: Coerced submission without economic loss

**Preservation and review:** Preserved; summary judgment and the governing legal rule receive de novo review. Karibian receives favorable reasonable inferences, but must identify evidence supporting actionable harassment.

**Governing law and burdens:** Title VII and the contemporaneous EEOC regulation address conditioning employment benefits on submission to sexual demands. *Meritor Savings Bank v. Vinson*, 477 U.S. 57 (1986), distinguishes unwelcomeness from apparent participation and rejects economic injury as a universal prerequisite to harassment liability. *Carrero v. New York City Housing Authority*, 890 F.2d 569 (2d Cir. 1989), supplies circuit treatment of the harassment categories. These authorities predate the cutoff. ([Law Resource](https://law.resource.org/pub/us/case/reporter/F3/014/14.F3d.773.93-7188.513.html))

**Competing positions and open question:** Karibian argues that requiring a lost benefit protects employees who resist but excludes those who submit to an unlawful condition. Columbia argues that promotions and favorable treatment undermine the asserted employment injury and that consensual conduct is not discrimination. The critical distinction is between genuinely welcome conduct and submission obtained through supervisory leverage, not between employees who received or lost compensation.

**Lawful paths and remedy:** Reverse the economic-loss premise and remand if the evidence permits a finding of coerced submission; affirm only if the properly viewed record independently defeats the claim. Reversal would not establish that coercion occurred or entitle Karibian to a particular damages award.

### Issue 2: Employer responsibility for supervisory harassment

**Preservation and review:** Preserved legal challenge to employer attribution, reviewed de novo.

**Governing law:** *Meritor* requires attention to agency principles and rejects both universal employer liability and an absolute notice requirement. *Kotcher v. Rosa & Sullivan Appliance Center*, 957 F.2d 59 (2d Cir. 1992), addresses notice and corrective mechanisms, but must be read alongside the distinction between ordinary coworker-type misconduct and harassment accomplished through delegated authority. *Harris v. Forklift Systems*, 510 U.S. 17 (1993), governs the objectively and subjectively abusive environment inquiry. ([Law Resource](https://law.resource.org/pub/us/case/reporter/F3/014/14.F3d.773.93-7188.513.html))

**Competing positions:** Karibian’s strongest attribution theory is that Urban could extract compliance because Columbia entrusted him with consequential supervisory powers. Columbia emphasizes confidential complaints, available reporting mechanisms, and its response when authorized to act. Neither position permits resolving disputed coercion or the actual scope of Urban’s authority against Karibian at summary judgment.

**Lawful paths:** Determine whether the evidence supports agency-based attribution independently of notice. If so, remand for the underlying harassment and attribution findings. Otherwise, assess the adequacy of notice, complaint procedures, and corrective action under the governing circuit rule. Restoration of viable federal claims requires reconsideration of the accompanying state claims, not automatic judgment on them.
