## Record and issues

The employer’s stock-incentive compensation plan involved contingent stock-appreciation benefits. A 1987 recapitalization accelerated cash payments. The pension plans included regular salary and specified bonus or incentive compensation in earnings, while granting interpretive discretion to the committee. Participants argued that the cash distributions fell within that earnings definition. ([Law Resource](https://law.resource.org/pub/us/case/reporter/F3/037/37.F3d.55.93-9038.html))

An earlier committee expressed a preliminary inclination toward inclusion but did not make a final binding determination. A reconstituted committee denied inclusion in 1988; a later express amendment excluded the payments. The timing and personnel changes support a conflict inquiry but do not themselves prove that the earlier plan text required inclusion. The district court rejected the claims, addressing interpretation and the administrator’s decision. ([Law Resource](https://law.resource.org/pub/us/case/reporter/F3/037/37.F3d.55.93-9038.html))

**Jurisdiction:** Final judgment in an ERISA benefits action, reviewable under § 1291. **Preservation:** Review standard, ambiguity, administrator discretion and conflict, plan practice, and tax-definition arguments are presented. **Missing material:** Full plan versions, committee minutes, complete trial findings, class definition, and notice date.

## Law entering the event

**Cutoff:** September 26, 1994. **Simulation holdings:** None supplied.

### Issue 1: Review standard and permissible interpretation

**Governing Supreme Court authority:** *Firestone Tire & Rubber v. Bruch*, 489 U.S. 101 (1989), supplies de novo review absent delegated discretion and deferential review where discretion is conferred, with a conflict considered as a factor. It does not establish automatic de novo review whenever a conflict is alleged. ([Law Resource](https://law.resource.org/pub/us/case/reporter/F3/037/37.F3d.55.93-9038.html))

**Circuit law and burden:** *Heidgerd v. Olin*, 906 F.2d 903 (2d Cir. 1990), prevents rewriting clear plan terms. Whether the language is ambiguous is a legal question; the administrator’s permissible resolution of an ambiguity receives the review the plan and *Firestone* require. The participants must show entitlement or an impermissible denial under that standard. ([Law Resource](https://law.resource.org/pub/us/case/reporter/F3/037/37.F3d.55.93-9038.html))

**Positions:** Participants emphasize the plan’s references to incentive and additional compensation, the cash form of payment, and the change in committee personnel. The plans distinguish regular compensation from contingent stock-appreciation awards and point to the absence of a settled contrary practice.

**Paths:** Require inclusion if the plan unambiguously commands it or the administrator’s interpretation is arbitrary under the proper conflict-sensitive review. Otherwise uphold a reasonable interpretation, even if another reading is possible. Do not give the later amendment retroactive effect merely because it makes the exclusion explicit.

### Issue 2: Tax definitions, fiduciary duties, and interpretive aids

**Law:** ERISA fiduciary duties require administration consistent with lawful plan terms; they do not invariably require maximizing every claimed benefit. Tax provisions defining compensation for qualification or limits do not necessarily dictate the pension formula. *Masella v. Blue Cross*, 936 F.2d 98 (2d Cir. 1991), must be applied in its own review context rather than used to displace an express discretionary grant. ([Law Resource](https://law.resource.org/pub/us/case/reporter/F3/037/37.F3d.55.93-9038.html))

**Positions and uncertainty:** Participants argue that broader tax definitions and ambiguity favor inclusion. The plans invoke tax-qualification concerns and the distinction between stock incentives and ordinary earnings. Some offered rationales may be weak even if the ultimate interpretation remains permissible.

**Remedy constraints:** Review the actual decision and conflict without demanding that every administrator rationale be independently decisive. If denial fails the governing standard, remand for proper administration or award benefits only where the record permits no other lawful result.
