## Supreme Court merits decision, if any

Chandris, Inc. v. Latsis, 515 U.S. 347 (1995), argued 1995-02-21, decided 1995-06-14. O'Connor, J., for six Justices; Stevens, J., concurring in the judgment with Thomas and Breyer.

**Question:** what employment-related connection to a vessel in navigation is needed for Jones Act seaman status.

**Holding:** seaman status requires duties that contribute to the vessel's function or mission and a connection to a vessel (or identifiable fleet) in navigation that is substantial in both duration and nature (under about 30 percent of working time in a vessel's service is a rule-of-thumb floor). The drydock instruction was erroneous: whether a vessel is in navigation is normally for the jury, and the charge swept too broadly.

**Effect:** the Second Circuit's vacatur and new trial were affirmed. The Court read the circuit's third prong as "duration and nature," not "duration or nature," and told the district court to use its own two-element charge. It declined to consider a Rule 51 argument not raised in the petition (515 U.S. at 353 n.*).

## Dependence on the historical judgment

- **Petition, grant, decision.** Sought by the defendants, who lost the defense verdict through vacatur. They depended on a judgment that vacated for retrial, announced a four-part charge, held the drydock exclusion erroneous, and treated the unobjected instructions as plain error. A judgment affirming the defense verdict would make Latsis the petitioner, and "affirmed" would not transfer. A judgment declaring Latsis a seaman as a matter of law would conflict with the Court's jury-question holding. A judgment that also vacates and remands for retrial on the charge and drydock error would still fit a petition by the defendants.
- **General law.** The 1995 holding binds after 1995-06-14 regardless of the simulated judgment and corrects the circuit's "duration or nature" wording.
