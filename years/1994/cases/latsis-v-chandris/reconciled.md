## Historical comparison

The panel disposition of 1994-03-24 (20 F.3d 45) vacated the defense judgment and remanded for a new trial. Oakes wrote the opinion and Cardamone joined. Kearse dissented, contesting reversal under the preservation rules and disagreeing with the majority's treatment of the drydock/navigation issue. The majority found the charge erroneous in its treatment of the employment relationship and in the categorical exclusion of the drydock period, handled both under the applicable preservation analysis, resolved no disputed vessel-time fact, and did not hold Latsis a seaman. The remedy was a new trial under a corrected seaman-status framework, with unresolved liability and damages questions left undecided.

Premises on which the historical result depended: the same trial record, the same legal cutoff (1994-03-24, the date of the historical decision, so no later authority separates the two settings), the preserved objection to the drydock exclusion against the unpreserved broader objections, and a general verdict that reached only status. Premises that changed: another judge now holds Cardamone's seat, and the case is taken on a papers resubmission rather than on the 1993-12-06 argument. Neither change alters the record, the law, or the preservation facts that Oakes or Kearse would apply. history.md is compressed; it states Kearse's grounds only at the level of the two items above.

## James L. Oakes

**Reconciled disposition:** Vacate the judgment for the shipowners and remand for a new trial. Issue 2 not reached; if reached, no relief on the unpreserved charge language.

**Ground:** The categorical instruction removing the six-month German assignment from the status inquiry was legal error, because Galileo was in navigation when Latsis was hurt at sea and the later assignment is evidence on the enduring fleet relationship, not a claim for an injury during the overhaul. The scale of the conversion goes to weight. The error is not shown harmless under Rule 61 and 28 U.S.C. § 2111, given the 72 versus 10 percent conflict and a general verdict. The test remains Wilander's employment-related connection to a vessel in navigation plus the circuit's more or less permanent connection requirement; Salgado's aid-to-navigation element is displaced.

**Remedy:** New trial on all issues, since the jury reached only status. No appellate finding of seaman status or of the vessel-time proportion. Retrial charge under Wilander and the circuit connection cases.

**Join limits:** As committed: no revival of the aid-to-navigation element; no holding that Latsis is a seaman as a matter of law or that resolves the 72 versus 10 dispute; no holding that conversion time always counts regardless of the vessel's condition; no relief on Issue 2 unless Schaafsma and Abou-Khadra are met.

**Basis for any departure from the commitment or from history:** None on disposition, ground, or remedy; the commitment and history match, including the new-trial remedy. One difference in scope: history says the majority found the charge erroneous in its treatment of the employment relationship generally and applied preservation analysis to the broader framework, while the commitment treats Issue 2 as not reached. No changed premise explains this, and the compressed history does not say the majority granted relief on unpreserved language independent of the preserved objection. The commitment is kept. An opinion by this judge may address the employment-relationship formulation as guidance for retrial, tied to the preserved exclusion, without holding Issue 2 satisfied. Authorship is an assembly question; history's authorship is noted and not adopted here.

## Amalya L. Kearse

**Reconciled disposition:** Affirm the judgment for the shipowners; do not join vacatur. This departs from the commitment, which was vacatur on a narrow preserved-objection basis.

**Ground:** The two grounds history attributes to the dissent: (a) reversal is not supported under the preservation rules, so no relief may rest on the unobjected broader charge (Rule 51, Schaafsma, Abou-Khadra, Smith v. Lightning Bolt), and the preserved objection to the exclusion does not by itself carry a new trial; (b) the majority's treatment of the drydock/navigation question is rejected, which is read as sustaining or tolerating the exclusion of the conversion period given Galileo's out-of-navigation status (the West and McKinley line). Whether she regards the exclusion as correct or as non-prejudicial is not stated in history.md; either supports affirmance and the choice is left open. The shared law is unchanged: Wilander's connection requirement, no aid-to-navigation element, no fixed time-fraction test.

**Remedy:** None beyond affirmance. No determination of seaman status, negligence, causation, or damages.

**Join limits:** She cannot join vacatur, whether on the narrow Issue 1 basis or a broader one. She cannot join any relief resting on the unpreserved charge without the Schaafsma and Abou-Khadra showing. She cannot join revival of the aid-to-navigation element, a fixed time-fraction test, a holding that conversion work is always probative, or an appellate determination of seaman status. She could join an affirmance resting on either ground (a) or ground (b).

**Basis for any departure from the commitment or from history:** The departure from the commitment is on disposition and ground. The commitment itself named affirmance (West and McKinley making conversion time irrelevant, or the jury crediting the 10 percent estimate) as a plausible alternative and kept vacatur only because her writings gave no basis to prefer it; its basis was the absence of a seaman-status opinion and a non-exhaustive search (the search was throttled). History is positive evidence of her vote on this very record and on this very pair of issues. Its premises survive: same record, same law at the cutoff, same preserved and unpreserved split. The changed premises (a different judge in the third seat, resubmission on papers) bear on who forms a majority, not on how she would read the instruction or the preservation rules, so no changed premise supports staying with the commitment. The commitment's preference for the injury-at-sea distinction therefore yields to history on this point, and the commitment text itself is left unaltered.
