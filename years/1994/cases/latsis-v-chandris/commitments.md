# Commitments: Latsis v. Chandris, Inc., No. 93-7704 (2d Cir.)

**Stage:** neutral modeling. **Legal cutoff:** 1994-03-24. **Inputs:** neutral.md, ENGINE.md, and the public sources cited below.

Both judges are modeled independently. Issue 1 is the preserved challenge to the categorical exclusion of the German drydock assignment. Issue 2 is the unpreserved challenge to the broader connection charge. Governing law common to both: *McDermott Int'l, Inc. v. Wilander*, 498 U.S. 337 (1991) ("The key to seaman status is employment-related connection to a vessel in navigation"; the aid-to-navigation element is gone; the inquiry is fact specific and, where reasonable persons could differ, goes to the jury); *Southwest Marine, Inc. v. Gizoni*, 502 U.S. 81 (1991) (job label does not decide status; ship repairers can qualify); *West v. United States*, 361 U.S. 118 (1959) (for a vessel in a repair yard for an overhaul, the focus is on the ship's status, the pattern of repairs and the extent of the work; the case is a seaworthiness-warranty case, not a Jones Act status case).

## James L. Oakes (Senior Circuit Judge)

**Disposition:** Issue 1: vacate the judgment for the shipowners and remand for a new trial, because the instruction removing the six-month German assignment from the jury's status inquiry was legal error that cannot be shown harmless. Issue 2: not reached; retrial supersedes it. If reached, no relief, because the objections were unpreserved and the demanding showing is not made.

**Ground:** The test is an employment-related connection to a vessel in navigation. The circuit's requirement of a more or less permanent connection survives, but Salgado's aid-to-navigation element is displaced by *Wilander*. In *Salgado* (his own opinion) he affirmed a no-seaman finding as supported by the evidence and treated the permanently assigned pump engineer in *Harney* as a jury question; he treats status as a fact question once the evidence supports an inference. The exclusion answered a different question from the one the jury had. Galileo was in navigation when Latsis was hurt at sea. West asks whether a worker injured during an overhaul is owed duties by a vessel then out of maritime service. It does not say that an employee's later assignment cannot be evidence of the enduring fleet relationship. The scale of the conversion goes to weight, and Chandris may argue it. Review of the instruction is independent. Under Rule 61 and 28 U.S.C. § 2111 the question is prejudice. The status evidence conflicted sharply (about 72 percent of time at sea against about 10 percent), and the general verdict cannot show how the jury weighed it without the excluded period. His reasoning in *Rivera* applies: a charge error that "may well" have supported the verdict requires retrial.

**Remedy:** New trial on all issues. The jury reached only status, so no separable finding survives (*Rivera*, reasoning on scope of retrial). No appellate finding on seaman status or on the disputed time proportion. On retrial the district court charges under *Wilander* and the circuit connection cases and lets the jury weigh the conversion's scale. Negligence, causation and damages are left for trial.

**Sources:** *Salgado v. M. J. Rudolph Corp.*, 514 F.2d 750 (2d Cir. 1975) (Oakes, J., for Kaufman, Mansfield and Oakes; quoted test fragments "more or less permanent connection" and "aid to navigation" verified; pin 755 from neutral.md, unverified); *Rivera v. Hudson Waterways Corp.*, 603 F.2d 1049 (2d Cir. 1979) (per curiam, Oakes on panel; attention to which charge objections were preserved). Also *Harney v. William M. Moore Bldg. Corp.*, 359 F.2d 649 (2d Cir. 1966) (crewman status a jury question where the evidence permits the inference); *Klarman v. Santini*, 503 F.2d 29 (2d Cir. 1974); *Schaafsma v. Morin Vermont Corp.*, 802 F.2d 629 (2d Cir. 1986); *Abou-Khadra v. Mahshie*, 4 F.3d 1071 (2d Cir. 1993) (unpreserved charge error reviewed only for plain error); *Air et Chaleur, S.A. v. Janeway*, 757 F.2d 489 (2d Cir. 1985).

**Join limits:** He cannot join any rationale that revives *Salgado*'s aid-to-navigation element (displaced by *Wilander*). ## Amalya L. Kearse (Active Circuit Judge)

**Disposition:** Issue 1: vacate and remand for a new trial, on a narrower basis than a rule of general application: the objection to the drydock exclusion was specific and preserved, the exclusion was a misstatement of what the jury may consider, and the record does not allow a finding of harmlessness. Issue 2: not reached; if reached, no plain error and no relief.

**Ground:** I found no seaman-status opinion by her, so her position comes from circuit law, the statutes and her preservation and jury-question writings. The test is *Wilander*'s employment-related connection to a vessel in navigation plus the circuit's more or less permanent connection requirement (*Salgado*, *Harney*, *Klarman*). Status goes to the jury unless one conclusion is possible (*Wilander*, *Gizoni*). The exclusion tied the six months to whether Galileo was in navigation during the work. That rationale fits an injury during an overhaul (*West*), but this injury occurred on a voyage while the ship was in navigation, and the assignment is offered on the separate question of enduring relationship. Therefore the categorical bar was erroneous, although the scale of the conversion remains a legitimate argument about weight. Her *Smith v. Lightning Bolt* opinion enforces objection requirements and she would not stretch the preserved objection to reach unobjected charge language; Issue 2 therefore fails under *Schaafsma* and *Abou-Khadra* as Rule 51 was applied in this circuit before the cutoff. Prejudice is assessed under Rule 61 and § 2111; the disputed time proportions and a general verdict prevent a harmlessness finding. A plausible alternative, affirmance because *West* and *McKinley* make conversion time legally irrelevant or because the jury could credit the supervisor's 10 percent estimate regardless, matters here. Her writings give no basis to prefer it over the injury-at-sea distinction, which the circuit's connection cases support.

**Remedy:** Vacate; remand for a new trial. The retrial charge may be corrected afresh, and the forfeiture of Issue 2 does not bind the district court on retrial. No determination of seaman status, negligence, causation or damages.

**Sources:** *Smith v. Lightning Bolt Prods., Inc.*, 861 F.2d 363 (2d Cir. 1988) (Kearse, J.; unobjected instructions and special-verdict questions not reviewable as to those not objected to); *Fiacco v. City of Rensselaer*, 783 F.2d 319 (2d Cir. 1986) (Kearse, J.; jury verdict consistency); *Johannessen v. Gulf Trading & Transp. Co.*, 633 F.2d 653 (2d Cir. 1980) (Brieant, J., for Kaufman, Kearse and Brieant; Jones Act jury-question standard, directed verdict reversed); *Diebold v. Moore McCormack Bulk Transp. Lines, Inc.*, 805 F.2d 55 (2d Cir. 1986) (Mansfield, J., for panel with Kearse; directed verdict reversed, Jones Act). Joined *Black v. Red Star Towing & Transp. Co.*, 860 F.2d 30 (2d Cir. 1988) (en banc) (maintenance and cure indemnity; no relevance to status). Other authority as for Oakes, with *Anderson v. Branen*, 17 F.3d 552 (2d Cir. 1994) (Altimari, J.; unobjected charge reviewed only for "plain error affecting substantial rights that goes to the very essence of the case").

**Join limits:** She cannot join a rationale that bypasses the Issue 1 objection by reaching the unpreserved charge without the *Schaafsma*/*Abou-Khadra* showing, or one that revives the aid-to-navigation element. She would not join a holding that adopts a fixed time-fraction test, since none exists in *Wilander*, *Gizoni* or circuit law and *Robison* is persuasive only. She cannot join a holding that treats conversion work as always probative, given *West*. She would not join an appellate determination of seaman status. Agreement on vacatur and retrial is routine.
