## Stone's Position

### Component 1

**Component:** Seaman-status instruction and preserved drydock exclusion

**Jurisdiction and reach:** Latsis specifically objected to excluding the six-month drydock assignment. The stipulated contribution-to-function element does not resolve his employment connection. The statutory route and unsettled jurisdictional predicates in the neutral baseline remain controlling; this proposal does not assume them away.

**Proposed disposition:** Vacate and remand for a new trial on the preserved drydock-instruction ground.

**Governing law:** Independent review of the legal instruction; competing evidence and inferences belong to the jury. Civil prejudice is assessed under Rule 61. A seaman needs an employment-related connection to a vessel in navigation; useful work alone is insufficient. Whether later repair service bears on an enduring employment relationship differs from whether an injury during reconstruction itself would be covered.

McDermott International, Inc. v. Wilander, 498 U.S. 337, 353-55 (1991), removes the aid-to-navigation requirement but preserves an employment connection; Southwest Marine, Inc. v. Gizoni, 502 U.S. 81 (1991), preserves fact-sensitive status analysis; West v. United States, 361 U.S. 118 (1959), addresses a vessel undergoing major repairs in its own setting. 46 U.S.C. app. § 688; Fed. R. Civ. P. 51, 61; Salgado v. M. J. Rudolph Corp., 514 F.2d 750, 755 (2d Cir. 1975), and related circuit connection precedents retain force except where Wilander displaced the navigation-duty element.

**Legal relationship:** Applying binding law; distinguishing precedent by the question for which repair service is relevant.

**Proposed holding:** The vessel’s repair status does not categorically make the worker’s continuing assignment irrelevant to seaman status for an earlier shipboard injury.

**Legal reasoning:** Stone should propose a new trial on the preserved drydock-instruction issue. The holding should protect the jury's assessment of an enduring vessel relationship without declaring Latsis a seaman as a matter of law or imposing a numerical threshold absent from governing law.

The Jones Act protects a maritime employment relationship, not merely a job title. Useful shipboard work is necessary but not sufficient. Conversely, a fleet employee does not necessarily become a stranger to the fleet each time a vessel undergoes a lengthy refit. The proper inquiry considers the character, continuity, and substantiality of the actual assignment. Here Latsis sailed with the vessel to the repair location, supervised work connected to its return to service, and continued his fleet employment afterward. Those facts could matter even if the vessel was temporarily out of navigation.

Chandris must remain free to argue that the six-month conversion was qualitatively land-based and that Latsis's ordinary work lacked the requisite vessel connection. The competing ten-percent and seventy-two-percent accounts should not be resolved on appeal. But an instruction excluding the entire conversion period makes one legal characterization conclusive before the jury evaluates what that assignment shows. On this record, the period was sufficiently substantial to make its exclusion prejudicial to the status determination.

An older illustration, Judge Learned Hand's *The J. S. Warden*, 175 F. 314, 315 (S.D.N.Y. 1910), emphasizes function in the vessel's work rather than traditional nautical titles. That district-court reasoning is historically useful but does not displace circuit requirements concerning connection and navigation. **Proposed disposition:** Do not rest reversal on an unnecessary expansion of civil plain-error review.

**Governing law:** The circuit’s demanding civil plain-error standard under Rule 51; Schaafsma and Abou-Khadra require the necessary exceptional showing, not merely a better alternative instruction. **Legal relationship:** Applying binding law.

**Proposed holding:** The preserved drydock error supplies the proposed new trial; the unpreserved general formulation does not require a separate doctrinal ruling.
