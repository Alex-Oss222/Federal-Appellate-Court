**General law note.** Chandris, Inc. v. Latsis, 515 U.S. 347 (1995), decided 1995-06-14, binds the Second Circuit and the district court on any retrial from that date, whether or not its case-specific path is admitted for the simulated case; it does not reach back to the 1994-03-24 decision. As recorded in the follow-up file, it holds that seaman status requires duties that contribute to the vessel's function or mission and a connection to a vessel (or identifiable fleet) in navigation that is substantial in both duration and nature, with about 30 percent of working time in a vessel's service as a rule-of-thumb floor; it reads the circuit's third prong as "duration and nature," not "duration or nature"; and it treats whether a vessel is in navigation as normally a jury question. Effect on the simulated holding: proposition 1 (a conversion or repair assignment is not categorically irrelevant, and its weight is for the jury) is consistent with the jury-question holding. The simulated opinion's statements of the permanence formulation ("more or less permanent") and of "no numerical threshold" are the points the 1995 "duration and nature" formulation and rule-of-thumb floor would govern or refine on retrial. That tension is flagged for the follow-up and precedent-index step, not decided here. If the case-specific path is later marked unresolved, the 1995 law still governs.
