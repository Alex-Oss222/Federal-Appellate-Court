## Historical comparison extract

Historically the panel (Lumbard, Oakes, Mahoney) affirmed Castillo's conviction and sentence on 1994-01-28. Judge Mahoney wrote, joined by Judge Oakes; Judge Lumbard dissented, disputing the rehabilitation rationale and the treatment of the sole firearm eyewitness's repeated statements as harmless. The majority admitted the prior statements for rehabilitation, found no reversible prejudice, rejected the testimony and Rule 33 claims, found the reasonable-doubt charge adequate, and sustained the role adjustment; no new trial or resentencing was ordered. Source: United States v. Castillo, 14 F.3d 802 (2d Cir. 1994), No. 92-1279.

The simulated judgment is a changed judgment on the firearm count and sentence: both are vacated and remanded, because officers' repetition is not rehabilitation and not harmless. Judge Stone sits in place of Judge Mahoney; Judge Lumbard writes for himself and Judge Stone, Judge Oakes dissenting in part. The Rule 33, reasonable-doubt and role-adjustment rulings reach the same results, and the testimony claim is left undecided with no relief rather than rejected.
