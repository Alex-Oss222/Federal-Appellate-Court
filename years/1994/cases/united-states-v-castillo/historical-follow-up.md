## Supreme Court merits decision, if any

None. The Court denied certiorari and did not grant, hear, vacate or remand. A denial is not approval of the Second Circuit's reasoning.

## Dependence on the historical judgment

- **Certiorari petition and denial.** Sought by Castillo, the only appellant whose issues the opinion decides, from a judgment that left his conviction and sentence in place with no remand. The questions in the petition are not known. Fit test: any Second Circuit judgment that also affirms Castillo's convictions and sentence would still fit a petition by Castillo. A reversal or a sentencing remand would leave him without an adverse final judgment on the affected counts, and any petition would then more naturally come from the government. Because the petition's questions are unknown, compatibility on a specific issue (prior-statement rehabilitation, Rule 33 timeliness, reasonable-doubt charge, supervisory-role adjustment) cannot be tested.
- **Denial.** Terminal event with no precedential effect. It depends only on there having been a petition from this affirmance.
- **Fernandez.** The opinion states that his appeal "will be heard and decided separately." No Supreme Court event in No. 93-8879 concerns him.
