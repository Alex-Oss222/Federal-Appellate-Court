# United States v. Castillo

**Status:** Version 1; proposed, not user-approved. **Approval reference:** None. The source brief states that every Stone position, alternative, and negotiating limit is a proposal pending approval.

## Stone's proposed judicial position

Stone should propose reversal of the firearm conviction for a new trial because the repeated reports lacked a sufficiently distinct rehabilitative purpose and the error cannot confidently be treated as harmless. His reason is not distrust of police testimony as a category. It is the evidentiary structure of this prosecution: one eyewitness supplied the disputed firearm evidence, the weapon was not recovered, the initial report omitted it, and other officers' repetition risked supplying the corroboration the investigation itself lacked.

*Pierre* should be applied, not repudiated. A prior statement may resolve ambiguity when its context explains why two accounts are compatible. Here the central defect is that additional witnesses repeated the same substantive accusation. Calling that use rehabilitation does not explain how it resolves the asserted discrepancy. Nor does a limiting instruction necessarily neutralize a presentation that invites the jury to count multiple official witnesses as corroboration of a single source. The earlier *Castillo* decision makes particular care appropriate because it already identified the danger of bolstering the contested gun evidence; it does not conclusively decide harmlessness at a different trial.

Under *Kotteakos*, Stone should examine the actual prosecutorial use of the statements and their importance to this verdict. The government cannot prevail merely by identifying sufficient admissible evidence. Where the disputed element is supplied principally by the witness being bolstered, repetition by fellow officers can materially affect the jury's evaluation. A new trial, rather than acquittal, follows because the proposed error concerns the manner of proof, not a determination that no rational jury could find the firearm element.

Stone should separately uphold the Rule 33 timeliness ruling and reject the reasonable-doubt objection. He should not convert the time bar into a merits holding that counsel could prohibit testimony, or hold that prior advice necessarily establishes a later knowing waiver. The renewed trial may eliminate the practical need to resolve that episode here; any surviving collateral claim requires its own lawful channel and developed record. He can also accept the district court's supported supervisory findings while requiring resentencing to reflect the disposition of the firearm count. This approach implements the Master Jurisprudence's rules on actual-trial harmlessness, personal trial rights, review of facts, and separation of error from remedy, §§ 8-9 and Criminal Procedure and Punishment.

## Proposed negotiating limits

**Preferred and broadest defensible holding:** inadmissible repetitive rehabilitation, prejudicial on this record, requiring a new firearm trial. The opinion may explain the limits of *Pierre* but should not prohibit all prior consistent statements offered for a genuinely clarifying purpose.

**Acceptable narrower proposal:** reversal resting on the particular prosecutorial use and centrality of these statements, without an exhaustive account of all permissible rehabilitation. A remand for an attorney-interference inquiry would be independently acceptable only if that issue remains necessary and is reachable despite the Rule 33 problem; it is not an automatic substitute for the evidentiary remedy.

**Nonjoin limit:** treating repetition as corroboration solely because multiple officers heard the original witness, equating sufficiency with harmlessness, or announcing that counsel controls the defendant's personal choice to testify. Stone also should not join an acquittal mandate unsupported by an insufficiency holding.
