# United States v. Castillo

**Case:** United States v. Castillo, docket 92-1279

**Historical event and date:** Panel disposition on 1994-01-28; 14 F.3d 802 (2d Cir. 1994). Only the selected event is described.

**Historical disposition:** Affirmed the challenged conviction and sentence, including rejection of the new-trial claims.

**Historical panel:** J. Edward Lumbard, James L. Oakes, J. Daniel Mahoney.

**Opinion author and joins:** J. Daniel Mahoney wrote the opinion. Joined by Oakes.

**Historical holding and grounds:** The majority permitted the prior statements for rehabilitation under its reading of the credibility attack and limiting instructions, and did not find reversible prejudice in the trial. It rejected the asserted grounds involving the decision to testify and the untimely Rule 33 motion, found the reasonable-doubt instruction adequate, and sustained the supervisory-role adjustment on the sentencing record. These are component-specific grounds, not a general authorization to use any repeated accusation substantively.

**Separate opinions:** Lumbard dissented. He disputed the rehabilitation rationale and the treatment of repeated statements by the sole firearm eyewitness as harmless, emphasizing the actual credibility contest and the government’s use of the repetition.

**Remedy and mandate:** The criminal judgment remained in place; no new trial or sentencing remand was ordered. The separate mandate’s issuance date and any stay or recall status are not established by an independently supplied docket; they are left unknown.
