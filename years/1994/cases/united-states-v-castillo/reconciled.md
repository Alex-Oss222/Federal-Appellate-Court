## Historical comparison

The panel of Lumbard, Oakes and Mahoney affirmed the conviction and sentence in full on 1994-01-28 (14 F.3d 802), rejecting the new-trial claims. Mahoney wrote and Oakes joined. Lumbard dissented, disputing the rehabilitation rationale and the treatment of the repeated statements of the sole firearm eyewitness as harmless, and stressing the real credibility contest and the government's use of the repetition. The majority admitted the statements for rehabilitation on its reading of the credibility attack and the limiting instructions and found no reversible prejudice. It rejected the decision-to-testify and untimely Rule 33 grounds, found the reasonable-doubt charge adequate, and sustained the supervisory-role adjustment on the sentencing record. No new trial or sentencing remand was ordered. Mandate status is unknown.

## J. Edward Lumbard

**Reconciled disposition:** Issues 2 to 5: affirm. Issue 1: the repetition by the officers was not confined to proper rehabilitation and substantially influenced the verdict on the § 924(c) count; vacate that conviction and remand for a new trial on that count only.

**Ground:** Issue 1. Under Pierre and Brennan a consistent statement is rehabilitative only if it has force beyond repetition, for example by answering the arrest-report omission or reconciling "waistband" with "gunpoint". The government conceded the motive to fabricate preceded the reports, so Check and Quinto bar substantive use. To the extent the officers merely repeated the waistband account, they multiplied the sole eyewitness's accusation, and the government's use of it as corroboration of a firearm that was never recovered made the error substantial under Kotteakos. The ground is admissibility and use, not other-evidence sufficiency. Issue 2: no finding on the disputed attorney-client exchange; Rock and Strickland; leave to § 2255 (Dukes). Issue 3: untimely, and known conduct is not newly discovered evidence (Dukes). Issue 4: reject; the charge is materially the Torres formulation read as a whole, and nothing is Cage-type. Issue 5: affirm on a finding of organizing or directing (Beaulieau; Boothe).

**Remedy:** Vacate the § 924(c) conviction and remand for a new trial limited to that count, with resentencing of the aggregate because the 84-month term includes it. The other three convictions stand, as in 924 F.2d 1227. The role ruling is unchanged; remand for role findings only if the adjustment rests on possession alone.

**Join limits:** Unchanged from the commitment. Also, in application: he cannot join an affirmance of the firearm count that treats the officers' repetition as properly corroborating the eyewitness, discounts the government's use, or rests on the strength of other evidence.

**Basis for any departure from the commitment or from history:** The commitment's headline (affirm) departs because its stated condition failed: it affirmed on the premise that the packet showed no summation use beyond credibility, and it set the remedy at a limited new trial if repetition or summation use substantially influenced the verdict. History's description of his dissent (the government's use of the repetition; harmlessness rejected for a single-eyewitness count) is direct evidence that the omitted fact is present on this record. The retained judge, the record and the governing law are the same as in history, so history is strong evidence and his historical vote is followed. The dissent disputes the "rehabilitation rationale"; the commitment allowed rehabilitation only for statements answering the two attacks, and I read the dissent as concluding that the reports as admitted and used went beyond that. The record does not say which officers' statements he would exclude, so that part is inferred. On Issues 2 to 5 there is no difference from history or the commitment.

## James L. Oakes

**Reconciled disposition:** Affirm on all issues, including Issue 1 and the § 924(c) conviction.

**Ground:** Issue 1. Statement by statement under Pierre and Brennan: reports that answered the arrest-report omission or the waistband versus gunpoint attack were admissible for credibility only, under a credibility-only instruction; there was no substantive use under Rule 801(d)(1)(B). On the actual summation and instructions, the repetition did not substantially influence the verdict under Kotteakos, as the Diaz approach requires without reliance on other proof. Issue 2: no finding on the disputed exchange on direct appeal; leave to § 2255. Issue 3: untimely (Dukes). Issue 4: reject; Torres and Magnano bind and the charge is materially that formulation. Issue 5: affirm, on an express finding of organizing or directing (the Beaulieau pattern), not custody of drugs and money.

**Remedy:** Affirm the judgment; no new trial and no sentencing remand. The conditional new-trial remedy in his commitment is not triggered.

**Join limits:** Unchanged from the commitment: no substantive Rule 801(d)(1)(B) use, no treatment of bare repetition as rehabilitation, no sufficiency-based harmlessness, no appellate finding on counsel's conversation, no Rule 33 relabeling, no structural treatment of disfavored wording, no possession-only role finding. He joins approval of the summation only on an examination of what was argued.

**Basis for any departure from the commitment or from history:** The headline matches the commitment, and the commitment matches history, so there is no departure. The only difference is the conditional remedy, which history resolves against reversal: he actually joined a majority that admitted the statements for rehabilitation and found no reversible prejudice on the same record, after hearing the argument. No changed premise displaces that vote; Mahoney's absence changes the author, not the record. On Issue 2, history says the majority "rejected" the testify grounds. I keep the commitment, since a rejection of the direct claim without finding the exchange is consistent with it, and history does not say the majority found the facts.

## Agreement between the two modeled judges (by component)

- **Issue 1, governing legal propositions:** Agree. No substantive use under Rule 801(d)(1)(B); bare repetition is not rehabilitation; Kotteakos asks about influence on the verdict, not sufficiency; summation use must be examined.
- **Issue 1, application, judgment and remedy:** Disagree. Lumbard vacates the firearm count for a new trial and resentencing; Oakes affirms with no remand. This was the historical split, and the substitution does not change it.
- **Issue 2:** Agree: no appellate finding; § 2255 is the channel.
- **Issue 3:** Agree: untimely; no relabeling.
- **Issue 4:** Agree: reject, as the charge is materially Torres.
- **Issue 5:** Agree: affirm on an organizing or directing finding. Their remand triggers differ only in wording (Lumbard: possession alone; Oakes: no express finding).
- **Overall judgment and remedy:** Disagree. These two judges supply two votes together for Issues 2 to 5 and for the Issue 1 legal propositions, but not for the judgment as a whole or the Issue 1 outcome. The vote on the judgment and the firearm-count remedy therefore depends on the third seat. Authorship and separate writings are open; the historical authorship by Mahoney does not carry over.
