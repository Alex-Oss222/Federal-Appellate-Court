## J. Edward Lumbard (Senior Circuit Judge)

**Disposition:**
- Issue 1 (officers repeating Johnson's reports): affirm admission as limited rehabilitation only; no substantive use under Rule 801(d)(1)(B).
- Issue 2 (right to testify): make no finding on the disputed attorney-client exchange; leave the claim to 28 U.S.C. § 2255.
- Issue 3 (Rule 33): motion untimely; no newly discovered evidence; no new trial.
- Issue 4 (reasonable-doubt charge): reject, if the charge is materially the formulation Torres approved.
- Issue 5 (§ 3B1.1(c)): affirm.

**Ground:**
- Issue 1. Abuse of discretion; Kotteakos for preserved error. Under Pierre a consistent statement may rehabilitate if it has probative force beyond repetition, for example by clarifying the claimed inconsistency or casting doubt that it exists; Brennan accepts a specific attack on credibility without a single identified impeaching statement. The government concedes the motive to fabricate preceded the reports, so Check and Quinto bar substantive use. Zito, his opinion, let relatives repeat a witness's statements after the defense suggested fabrication, as trial-court discretion; it predates the Rules and does not reach substantive use. The reports qualify only to the extent they answer the arrest-report omission or reconcile "waistband" with "gunpoint", and the packet shows no summation use beyond credibility. His harmless-error opinions (Smith; Ianniello) rest on overwhelming independent proof, which a single-eyewitness firearm count with no gun recovered lacks, so he would decide Issue 1 on admissibility and instruction, not harmlessness.
- Issue 2. The right is personal (Rock); ineffectiveness needs deficient performance and prejudice (Strickland). Dukes points ineffectiveness claims to § 2255, and in Dukes and Boothe he reached them on direct appeal only where the record settled them. This record does not.
- Issue 3. Dukes: the seven-day and two-year limits are jurisdictional; evidence is "newly discovered" only if found after trial and not discoverable earlier with due diligence. Dukes refused to treat first-raised ineffectiveness as new evidence even though defendants often cannot appreciate it before trial ends; that applies more strongly to a conversation Castillo knew of when it occurred.
- Issue 4. Charge read as a whole. Torres (binding) rejected a challenge to "abiding conviction" language, followed by the traditional "hesitate to act" formulation and repeated statements of the government's burden; Magnano is similar. Ianniello, his opinion, reads Sullivan as covering a select few violations such as a failure to charge on reasonable doubt, not any imperfect wording. The packet shows no Cage-type language.
- Issue 5. Clear error for findings, independent review of guideline meaning (Beaulieau; Jacobo). Boothe, his opinion, affirmed a § 3B1.1(c) organizer adjustment on evidence of recruiting, storing, obtaining a vehicle and transporting; Beaulieau affirmed one resting on weighing, packaging, pricing and supply. Weighing and packaging the cocaine and holding $80 of the $90 fits that pattern if the district court found organizing or directing, not mere possession.

**Remedy:** affirm the judgment. Where it matters: if repetition or summation use substantially influenced the verdict (Kotteakos), a new trial limited to the § 924(c) count, with resentencing because the 84-month aggregate includes it; the other convictions were not reversed in Castillo, 924 F.2d 1227. Remand for findings on the role adjustment only if it rests on possession alone.

## James L. Oakes (Senior Circuit Judge)

**Disposition:**
- Issue 1: affirm admission, statement by statement, to the extent each repeated report answers a specific attack; cumulative repetition of the waistband account would be error.
- Issue 2: no finding on direct appeal; leave to § 2255.
- Issue 3: Rule 33 motion untimely; no new trial.
- Issue 4: reject, on the same condition as above.
- Issue 5: affirm if an express finding of organizing or directing exists; otherwise vacate that adjustment and remand for findings.

**Ground:**
- Issue 1. In Brennan a targeted attack allowed rehabilitation with grand jury testimony that failed Rule 801(d)(1)(B) because the motive was continuous; it was admitted because it clarified how far the witness had implicated the defendant and cast doubt on the claimed inconsistency, under a credibility-only instruction. Maultasch upheld third-party repetition on redirect once the defense opened the door. He would match each officer's testimony and the summation to the defense's two attacks (the arrest-report omission; waistband versus gunpoint). In Diaz he rejected harmlessness for a misleading instruction without leaning on the strength of the evidence, consistent with Kotteakos here.
- Issue 2. No writing on allocation of the decision to testify. Rock and Strickland govern; in DiPaolo he applied Strickland on the direct-appeal record. He sat in Dukes, which sends ineffectiveness claims to § 2255. The affidavit is not a finding and counsel's account is absent.
- Issue 3. Dukes (sat; no separate writing) controls: jurisdictional limits and the diligence definition of newly discovered evidence.
- Issue 4. Sat in Ivic, 700 F.2d 51 (Friendly, J.), which affirmed an unobjected charge despite criticized embellishment and urged trial judges to use the standard formulation. Torres and Magnano are binding. Diaz shows he will read an actual charge for misdirection, but the "abiding conviction" language has circuit approval.
- Issue 5. No authored § 3B1.1 opinion located; he sat in Jacobo and Parker, which affirmed role findings resting on hiring a courier, or on recruiting, assigning roles and controlling proceeds. In Darmand and Bryser he vacated sentencing determinations resting on a possibly wrong framework or insufficient findings. He would therefore require the role relationship, not custody of drugs and money, to appear in the findings; the packet's facts match Beaulieau.

**Remedy:** affirm. Where it matters: new trial limited to the § 924(c) count with resentencing if Issue 1 error substantially influenced the verdict; limited remand for role findings if absent.

Authorities as listed for Lumbard.

**Join limits:** Same legal barriers as Lumbard: substantive Rule 801(d)(1)(B) use (Brennan holds such statements fail where the motive is continuous), bare repetition, sufficiency-based affirmance, appellate fact-finding on counsel's conversation, Rule 33 relabeling, structural treatment of mere disfavored wording, and possession-only role findings. He would not join approval of the summation use without examining what the prosecutor argued.
