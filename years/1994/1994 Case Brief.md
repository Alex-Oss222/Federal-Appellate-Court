#### Record and issues

Urban occupied a supervisory position with influence over assignments, schedules, raises, and promotions. Their sexual relationship is conceded; its voluntariness is disputed. After a nonconfidential complaint in January 1990, Columbia removed Urban’s supervisory responsibilities and obtained his resignation. Telefund later closed, and Karibian was laid off. The district court treated the absence of economic loss as fatal to quid pro quo liability and found insufficient grounds for notice-based employer liability. #### Law entering the event

##### Issue 1: Coerced submission without economic loss

**Preservation and review:** Preserved; summary judgment and the governing legal rule receive de novo review. Karibian receives favorable reasonable inferences, but must identify evidence supporting actionable harassment.

**Governing law and burdens:** Title VII and the contemporaneous EEOC regulation address conditioning employment benefits on submission to sexual demands. *Meritor Savings Bank v. Vinson*, 477 U.S. 57 (1986), distinguishes unwelcomeness from apparent participation and rejects economic injury as a universal prerequisite to harassment liability. *Carrero v. New York City Housing Authority*, 890 F.2d 569 (2d Cir. 1989), supplies circuit treatment of the harassment categories. These authorities predate the cutoff. ([Law Resource](https://law.resource.org/pub/us/case/reporter/F3/014/14.F3d.773.93-7188.513.html))

**Competing positions and open question:** Karibian argues that requiring a lost benefit protects employees who resist but excludes those who submit to an unlawful condition. Columbia argues that promotions and favorable treatment undermine the asserted employment injury and that consensual conduct is not discrimination. The critical distinction is between genuinely welcome conduct and submission obtained through supervisory leverage, not between employees who received or lost compensation.

**Lawful paths and remedy:** Reverse the economic-loss premise and remand if the evidence permits a finding of coerced submission; affirm only if the properly viewed record independently defeats the claim. Reversal would not establish that coercion occurred or entitle Karibian to a particular damages award.

##### Issue 2: Employer responsibility for supervisory harassment

**Preservation and review:** Preserved legal challenge to employer attribution, reviewed de novo.

**Governing law:** *Meritor* requires attention to agency principles and rejects both universal employer liability and an absolute notice requirement. *Kotcher v. Rosa & Sullivan Appliance Center*, 957 F.2d 59 (2d Cir. 1992), addresses notice and corrective mechanisms, but must be read alongside the distinction between ordinary coworker-type misconduct and harassment accomplished through delegated authority. *Harris v. Forklift Systems*, 510 U.S. 17 (1993), governs the objectively and subjectively abusive environment inquiry. ([Law Resource](https://law.resource.org/pub/us/case/reporter/F3/014/14.F3d.773.93-7188.513.html))

**Competing positions:** Karibian’s strongest attribution theory is that Urban could extract compliance because Columbia entrusted him with consequential supervisory powers. Columbia emphasizes confidential complaints, available reporting mechanisms, and its response when authorized to act. Neither position permits resolving disputed coercion or the actual scope of Urban’s authority against Karibian at summary judgment.

**Lawful paths:** Determine whether the evidence supports agency-based attribution independently of notice. If so, remand for the underlying harassment and attribution findings. Otherwise, assess the adequacy of notice, complaint procedures, and corrective action under the governing circuit rule. Restoration of viable federal claims requires reconsideration of the accompanying state claims, not automatic judgment on them.

## United States v. Knoll
### Cycle 001 · Criminal · Nos. 92-1580 and 92-1586 · Proposed event: February 14, 1994

### neutral.md

#### Identity and assignment

**Historical caption:** United States v. David R. Knoll and Ted W. Gleave. **Circuit and admission source:** Second Circuit, 16 F.3d 1313, docket and procedural header. **Primary group:** 1, Criminal. **Secondary subjects:** Bankruptcy offenses, limitations, private searches, sufficiency, trial conduct, self-representation, and speedy trial. **Principal questions:** Whether the false-declaration count is time-barred and whether officials participated in an unfinished private search of stolen law-office files. **Selection reason:** The listed criminal case combines statutory offense boundaries with suppression and trial-review questions. **Canonical reference:** Cycle 001, these two docket numbers. **Lower tribunal and judgment:** Western District of New York, Judge William M. Skretny. Judgments entered September 28, 1992, following sentencing on September 25. Gleave was convicted on counts three and four; Knoll on counts four and eight. Each received concurrent 27-month sentences, with all but three months suspended; Gleave also received a $5,000 fine. **Requested relief:** Defendants seek relief from their convictions on the grounds below; the government seeks affirmance. ([OpenJurist](https://openjurist.org/16/f3d/1313))

**Historical argument:** June 17, 1993. **Simulation submission:** Proposed January 18, 1994 resubmission on the existing record, conditional on Stone’s qualification. **Proposed event:** February 14, 1994.

**Historical panel:** Richard J. Cardamone, John D. Mahoney, and Southern District Judge Miriam Goldman Cedarbaum, sitting by designation. Cardamone was active at the historical argument and took senior status November 13, 1993; Mahoney and Cedarbaum were active. **Replacement:** Mahoney, under the fixed last-listed Second Circuit-member rotation. **Resulting panel:** Cardamone, Stone, and Cedarbaum. **Participation gaps:** Stone’s qualification and recusal records and a designation covering resubmission remain unverified. **Further departure:** None. ([OpenJurist](https://openjurist.org/16/f3d/1313))

#### Record and issues

**Material record:** Knoll, a lawyer, and Gleave opened Cayman Islands bank accounts in 1982. Gleave subsequently filed bankruptcy papers omitting the accounts and his connection to Atlantis International. The parties dispute the beneficial ownership and control of substantial funds. Count three concerns concealment; count four concerns the false bankruptcy declaration. Count eight concerns Knoll’s assistance with a false response to a 1987 financial questionnaire connected to collection of Gleave’s criminal fine. Gleave was acquitted of that count. ([OpenJurist](https://openjurist.org/16/f3d/1313))

In June 1986, private persons burglarized Knoll’s office seeking incriminating material to exchange for favorable treatment. The district court rejected testimony suggesting official advance involvement. After initial deliveries, however, an assistant United States attorney expressed disappointment and sought more useful material. Documents taken from the files led investigators to bank records. The unresolved factual question is whether private examination of the files was complete before that encouragement, or whether officials joined an ongoing search of previously unexamined material. ([OpenJurist](https://openjurist.org/16/f3d/1313))

**Jurisdiction:** Final criminal judgments under § 1291. The notice dates were not separately retrieved; no timeliness objection is reported. **Preservation:** Knoll expressly moved to dismiss count four as untimely, and Gleave joined his motions. Suppression was litigated before trial. **Related proceedings:** The underlying bankruptcy and fine-collection proceedings explain the charged acts, but are not independently before this panel. **Missing material:** The complete suppression transcript, trial objections, speedy-trial exclusion calendar, and appellate briefs. The New York State Association of Criminal Defense Lawyers appeared as amicus; its separate briefing was not retrieved, so no distinct argument is attributed to it. ([OpenJurist](https://openjurist.org/16/f3d/1313))

#### Law entering the event

**Cutoff:** February 14, 1994, before disposition. **Effective simulation records and holdings:** None supplied.

##### Issue 1: Limitations on count four

**Review and law:** The preserved statutory issue is reviewed de novo. Section 3282 ordinarily requires indictment within five years. Section 3284 postpones commencement for concealment of bankruptcy assets until discharge or denial of discharge. The court must determine the offense Congress made continuing, not merely whether the same conduct also concealed assets. No directly controlling Supreme Court construction of this precise interaction was identified. ([OpenJurist](https://openjurist.org/16/f3d/1313))

**Positions:** Defendants emphasize that the declaration was completed in 1982, while indictment occurred February 22, 1990. The government’s strongest argument is that the false declaration formed part of ongoing concealment. The statutory difficulty is that count four charged a false declaration, a distinct offense, rather than concealment.

**Paths and remedy:** If § 3284 does not reach the charged declaration offense, set aside count four for both defendants and direct its dismissal. The government cannot preserve it by relabeling the offense after trial. That conclusion does not decide count three.

##### Issue 2: Private search and official participation

**Review, test, and burdens:** Historical findings receive clear-error review; constitutional application is reviewed independently. Defendants must establish an invasion of their own protected interests. *Burdeau v. McDowell*, 256 U.S. 465 (1921), excludes genuinely private searches from the Fourth Amendment. *United States v. Jacobsen*, 466 U.S. 109 (1984), limits governmental expansion beyond a completed private search. *Walter v. United States*, 447 U.S. 649 (1980), and *Lustig v. United States*, 338 U.S. 74 (1949), require attention to the particular intrusion and governmental participation. ([OpenJurist](https://openjurist.org/16/f3d/1313))

**Positions and unresolved facts:** Knoll cannot overturn the finding of no preburglary official involvement merely by preferring contrary testimony. He can argue that encouragement to search additional closed files constituted participation in a later, unfinished search. The government can prevail if the relevant contents had already been privately exposed, or if its conduct did not cause any additional intrusion. Theft of a container does not necessarily eliminate privacy in all unexamined contents.

**Paths and remedy:** Affirm the preburglary findings if not clearly erroneous, but remand for findings about the sequence and scope of file examination. If unlawful participation is found, address the relationship between that search, the letters, and the bank evidence, including any independently supported exception to exclusion. A factual remand is not an acquittal or an automatic new trial.

##### Issue 3: Gleave’s personal privacy claim

**Review and law:** The personal-interest requirement follows *Rakas v. Illinois*, 439 U.S. 128 (1978). *United States v. Miller*, 425 U.S. 435 (1976), governs interests in bank records and information conveyed for banking transactions. The court must distinguish the particular bank letters and records from confidential attorney-client communications generally. ([OpenJurist](https://openjurist.org/16/f3d/1313))

**Positions:** Gleave relies on the seizure from his lawyer’s files; the government emphasizes that the relevant letters conveyed business information to the bank and were not privileged legal communications. The location of a copy in a law office does not automatically establish or defeat Gleave’s personal Fourth Amendment interest.

**Paths:** Decide the interests in the particular materials proved by the record, without declaring all client papers unprotected or allowing Gleave to assert Knoll’s rights.

##### Issue 4: Sufficiency and remaining trial challenges

**Standards and governing law:** Sufficiency requires evidence permitting a rational jury to find each element beyond a reasonable doubt, viewed favorably to the verdict. Section 2 permits liability through an innocent intermediary, so Gleave’s acquittal does not itself defeat Knoll’s conviction. The account designation and use bear on whether the questionnaire answer was false. *Faretta v. California*, 422 U.S. 806 (1975), governs knowing self-representation. Rules 403 and 404(b), the Fifth Amendment, and the Speedy Trial Act separately govern the other objections. ([OpenJurist](https://openjurist.org/16/f3d/1313))

**Positions:** Defendants contest account ownership and characterization, prosecutor comments, use of prior misconduct, Knoll’s self-representation, and delay. The government relies on the account documents, Knoll’s role in preparing answers, the comments’ context, motive relevance, repeated self-representation warnings, and excluded periods largely attributable to defense requests.

**Limits and remedies:** Assess each objection under its own preservation and error rule. The available account supports responsive-comment and motive explanations, but the missing transcripts prevent inventing objection dates or a day-by-day speedy-trial calculation. Insufficient evidence would require acquittal; prejudicial trial error would support a new trial; neither follows merely from the need for suppression findings.

##### Issue 1: Contract enforcement without trial

**Review and governing law:** De novo review of summary judgment and unambiguous contract language. Rule 56 requires a genuine evidentiary dispute, not unsupported argument. New York contract law governs performance, breach, and damages. *Leberman v. John Blair & Co.*, 880 F.2d 1555 (2d Cir. 1989), recognizes the implied covenant without converting it into authority to rewrite express obligations. The Supreme Court’s 1986 summary-judgment decisions supply the procedural framework. ([Law Resource](https://law.resource.org/pub/us/case/reporter/F3/021/21.F3d.522.93-7762.819.html))

**Positions:** Bidermann argues that foreign attachment violated the forum clause and that unrelated bargaining demands breached good faith. Rexnord argues that attachment secured assets rather than adjudicated the contract merits, that remedies were cumulative, and that it had no duty to extend an agreed deadline.

**Open question and paths:** The court must construe the actual forum and remedies provisions together. If they permit security proceedings abroad and no evidence supports a contractual defense, enforce the admitted obligation. If a material contractual restriction or supported defense was overlooked, remand. Refusal to grant a gratuitous extension is not itself a triable breach.

##### Issue 2: Postpetition entry and the automatic stay

**Review and governing law:** De novo interpretation of 11 U.S.C. § 362(a)(1). The stay operates immediately upon filing. *In re Fugazy Express*, 982 F.2d 769 (2d Cir. 1992), and *In re 48th Street Steakhouse*, 835 F.2d 427 (2d Cir. 1987), establish its force. The issue is whether a purely ministerial act, involving no remaining judicial discretion, constitutes prohibited continuation. No directly controlling Supreme Court resolution of this exact clerical-entry question was identified. ([Law Resource](https://law.resource.org/pub/us/case/reporter/F3/021/21.F3d.522.93-7762.819.html))

**Positions:** Bidermann emphasizes that judgment was entered after filing. Rexnord distinguishes completion of adjudication from the clerk’s recording of it. The relevant facts are when the judge exercised discretion and whether anything substantive remained.

**Lawful paths and constraints:** Uphold the entry if it merely recorded a completed prepetition decision. Vacate if postpetition judicial action was necessary. Any ministerial exception must remain narrow and does not authorize postpetition enforcement or collection.

##### Issue 1: Originality and protected contribution

**Review and law:** Summary judgment and the legal protection question are reviewed de novo. Sections 101, 102, and 103 protect sufficiently original expression and compilation choices, not facts as such. *Feist Publications v. **Positions:** Maclean points to predictive judgment and discretionary selection. CCC argues that vehicle values and their efficient presentation are functional information. The correct inquiry separates public facts and underlying valuation methods from original contributions actually copied.

**Paths:** Reject protection for mechanical facts or unoriginal arrangement, but do not deny all protection simply because forecasts are numerical. Any infringement judgment must identify the protected material, not reward effort alone.

##### Issue 2: Merger

**Open question and paths:** Determine the proper level of abstraction and the actual scope of copying.
