# Effective simulation precedent index

**Current through:** 1994-09-06 (Pro-Choice Network v. Schenck).

Historical law supplies the starting legal world. Relevant historical Supreme Court decisions, Second Circuit authority, statutes, rules, and other governing sources are researched for each event. This file is not an exhaustive restatement of 1994 law and imports nothing from `scotus-career`.

No separate Holdings or Standards and Tests document is maintained. Final event records retain the holdings, applied tests, review standards, burdens, and qualifications. This file links to them and tracks later treatment; it does not replace those records.

Simulation holdings are entered from final event records as they are decided. The fictional background opinion described in the character sheet has not been admitted as a separate controlling legal baseline.

## Update rules

- Add an entry only from a final record with supported controlling authority. Name the actual judges joining the operative proposition.
- Link to the record's complete rule, qualifications, and reasoning rather than duplicating them in competing trackers.
- Distinguish precedential holdings from party-specific judgments and relief. Pending procedure belongs in Standing State. Separate writings can be retrieved from the case records but do not enter this table as controlling law.
- Apply holdings from their proper effective dates, including within the same year. Do not wait for annual close.
- When later historical Supreme Court law supersedes, narrows, or otherwise affects a circuit proposition, record that treatment and its date. Do not rewrite the earlier record to pretend the later law already existed.
- Check the difference between a decision's effect as precedent and its effect on the finality or mandate of a particular case.
- A replaced selected-case opinion cannot coexist as an independent historical controlling disposition of that same event. Identify any preserved earlier stage explicitly.
- Correct indexing mistakes against final records. Substantive changes to final simulation adjudications require the authorized correction process.
